# Meeting brief: barrel detector, 2026-10-28

Written for Tanush, to read before the meeting and to update Dave from. Everything here has a number in `EXPERIMENTS.md` or a record in `DECISIONS.md`.

## The one-paragraph state

v1 is done: per-pixel color segmentation that, on a white matte table under window light, finds 84 % of orange barrel area and 81 % of blue (IoU) on five never-seen frames, with precision 0.96 or better and nothing flagged on an empty-table frame. It adapts its thresholds to each image; the only thing learned from labels is one RGB color box per class. Dave's email of 10/24 said: good enough, stop polishing pixels, move to barrel detection, use geometry and the known barrel size to fill in what shadows hide, skip wood tables. We agreed. v1.5 is in the works: the scaffolding v2 (detection) needs. The meeting is about v1 results and v2 next steps.

## v1, in plain terms

**What it does, per image.** (1) Throw away rows above the horizon. (2) Throw away pixels that are less colorful than the image's own wall rows above the horizon (the saturation floor, derived from the image: lands at 44–55 here). (3) Throw away pixels that match a robust color model of those same wall rows. (4) Keep what falls inside an orange or blue RGB box fit on labeled barrel pixels. (5) A pixel in both boxes goes to orange if red > blue. Output: two flat-color images per the spec.

**Numbers.** Test set (5 frames, touched once per milestone): orange IoU 0.843, blue 0.813, orange precision 0.96, blue 0.98, empty frame 0 pixels flagged. Cross-validation estimate on the other 16 frames was 0.825 / 0.793, so no overfitting. Selection used a rule written down before any sweep (differences under 0.02 don't count; any false pixels on an empty frame disqualify).

**What the ablations showed.**
- The saturation floor and the wall model do essentially all of the separating. An RGB box alone scores 0.04, because the barrel color clusters are smears along the brightness axis (Dave's "box vs diagonal" question: diagonal; PCA linearity 0.59 orange, 0.47 blue). The box only works after the floor removes the gray stuff.
- The horizon gate changes nothing on this table (no barrel pixel is above row 105 in any frame). The brightness gate changes nothing either. Both stay as switches.
- The bottom-rows "table prior" was redundant (the floor already removed the white table) and actively harmful on close barrels: the barrel's shadow in the bottom rows inflates the prior's spread and it then swallows the lid. Off in v1. Needs a cap on its spread before it can be trusted on a dark table.
- Per-image re-fitting of the boxes (the planned v1.5 idea) made things worse (−0.07) and a YUV chroma box tied. With one table and one light there is nothing for adaptation to adapt to. Both kept as switches.

**Where the errors are** (measured pixel by pixel).
- 21 % of true blue pixels are shadowed lower faces that fall below the saturation floor. Orange loses 2 % this way. This is the biggest miss, and it is exactly what Dave says geometry should handle instead.
- Orange false pixels are two things: my hand (skin sits inside the orange box: orange IoU on the pool is 0.77 with the hand frames, 0.84 without) and the barrel's own reflection in the glossy table next to a close barrel (brownish, just above the floor). Everything else is a one-pixel halo at edges.
- IoU is harsh: one pixel around a far barrel 28 px wide costs ~10 %. Per-frame precision is 0.91–0.99.

**Synthetic probe.** Darkening or color-casting the held-out images while training on the originals: the per-image floor keeps empty frames clean where a fixed floor floods them with blue (7.7 % of pixels under a bluish cast). The fixed RGB boxes are what break under color casts; that is the lighting-dependent part. Not real data; it only says which parts depend on lighting.

## Measurements

- **Barrel:** 24 mm tall, 18 mm diameter at the lid and base, 25.5 mm at the bulge (80 mm circumference), 6 mm stripe. `worldmap.py` uses 25 × 22; the 22 is between the two diameters. The planners' obstacle radius should arguably use the bulge (12.75 mm); the ground-projection offset should arguably use the base (9 mm) instead of 11.
- **Camera (AIM-4A8B6118):** focal length 355–405 px, tilt 21.5–25.5°, horizon row 55–100. Three methods (barrel width at five distances, ruler marks, barrel bottom rows) disagree by about 10 % because the barrel diameter, the ruler's zero, and the contact-point assumption each carry a few percent. Dave's 23° / 400 are inside the range. A mark at camera height (43.47 mm) on a far wall would pin the horizon independently of all three; planned in v1.5.
- `camera.py`'s 70° × 50° field of view is inconsistent with f = 400 (it would need f ≈ 457–515); the focal length is the right one to keep.

## v1.5: in the works (don't present, just mention)

Scaffolding so v2 starts from a working harness, no detector yet:
- **Per-barrel ground truth.** The existing class masks get split into individual barrels automatically (touching barrels are the hard case: in the masks, three touching barrels are one blob), with a click-to-fix tool. Each barrel gets a box, its color, and flags (occluded, cut by the frame edge, top-only).
- **Geometry module.** Camera model: horizon row, image row ↔ ground distance, expected barrel width and height at any row, bottom edge from top edge or stripe row, image point → ground position replicating the robot's own `project_to_ground`. Tested against pinned numbers.
- **Detection evaluation.** Boxes matched by overlap; precision/recall/F1 per class; bottom-edge error in pixels; ground-position error in mm; false detections on empty frames disqualify. Per condition, like the pixel eval.
- **Cheap pixel fixes as switches** (small-blob removal, hole filling), scored under the same cross-validation; expected to tie.
- **A trivial reference detector** (mask → connected components → boxes), clearly labeled a baseline. It sets the floor v2 must beat and proves the harness end to end. It will merge touching barrels by design.
- **A measured set.** ~10 frames on the same table with ruler-measured barrel positions, so position error in mm can be scored, plus the horizon frame. Kept out of all tuning.
- **Integration stub.** The exact five-field dict the world map reads (`OrangeBarrel`/`BlueBarrel`, `originx, originy, width, height` at 320 × 240), and a hook for the per-frame `user_image` callback. Not wired to the robot.

## v2 as proposed (detection, first iteration)

From a mask to a list of barrels: color, box, and ground position for each, using the known size and the camera model to fill in what the colors miss (bottom edge from the top edge or the stripe when the lower face is in shadow; expected width per row to split touching barrels and reject things of the wrong size; reflections rejected because they sit below the contact row). Scored by the detection eval above. Output in the aivision spec shape so it can replace the vendor detector in `worldmap.py`.

## Things to ask or decide in the meeting

1. **What counts as success for detection?** Proposed primary: per-class F1 at IoU 0.5, plus ground-position error in mm. Is there a target (e.g. every barrel within 30 cm found, position within 15 mm, which is the planner's obstacle inflation)?
2. **Integration route.** (a) Replace aivision entirely by writing our detections into the status the world map reads, or (b) run alongside for display and comparison first? (a) also raises the "held barrel" test in worldmap, which uses box position thresholds.
3. **Latency budget** for the host-side detector (the world map only updates when the robot is stopped; does that stay true?).
4. **Barrel size in worldmap:** 22 mm diameter and the 11 mm projection offset vs the measured 18 mm base / 25.5 mm bulge, and the `max(h, 1.1·w)` height pad. Fix in worldmap, or compensate on our side?
5. **Ground-position convention** for the measured set: barrel axis in the robot base frame (x forward, y left), distance measured to the near edge of the base at table level. OK?
6. **Occluded and edge-cut barrels:** should the eval ignore them (standard practice) or count them?
7. **Skin:** v1 confuses hands with orange. Low priority unless it creates false *barrels*; a hue test would fix it. Park or do?
