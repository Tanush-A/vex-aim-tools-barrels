## 3. The detector — `src/barrels/detector.py` (v2; steps 1–3 built 2026-11-03, the stripe step the default since 2026-11-05, hardened a–g 2026-11-08/09)

**What it does.** Takes the class mask from section 1 and returns a list of barrels. Each barrel is a `Barrel` record (`detect_types.py`): `color` (orange or blue), `bbox` = `(x0, y0, x1, y1)` in pixels, half-open (x1 and y1 are one past the last pixel), `confidence` (0–1), `flags` (`bottom_inferred` / `top_inferred` when that edge was computed rather than seen; `occluded` when the stripe was partly hidden and the full box drawn anyway; `truncated` when the box reaches a frame edge), `area` (how many mask pixels it came from) and `source` (`"det2"` for a barrel found from its stripe, `"det1"` for a stripeless piece). `detect(mask, cfg, cam)` is the one entry point: mask in, barrels out, same signature as the dumb reference `detect_cc.detect`, so either can be plugged into `detect_image.py` or the experiment scripts. `draw(image, barrels)` paints a 1-px rectangle per barrel — yellow for an orange barrel, green for a blue one, so the lines stand out from the barrels — and nothing else: the v2 deliverable image.

Nothing is remembered between images and nothing is fit per image. The numbers in `DetConfig` (table below) were written down before the first run and have not been tuned.

### Step 1 — blobs (`find_blobs`)

Take every pixel that is orange *or* blue (both at once, so a barrel's body and its stripe land in the same blob). Run a 3 × 3 *morphological close* over it — "fill any hole or gap up to 3 px wide" — so a shaded, speckled face does not break a barrel into two or three bits. Then find the 8-connected components (groups of touching barrel pixels; diagonal touching counts). Throw away components under 20 px (specks). For each surviving blob, record its bounding box and how many of its pixels were orange and how many blue.

*`touch_02`:* the mask has 3409 orange and 2337 blue pixels. They form **two** blobs: one at x 149–237, y 122–178 (88 px wide, 56 tall, 1763 orange / 1884 blue — this is the orange *and* blue barrel merged, because the orange barrel's blue stripe touches the blue barrel's body, exactly the case Dave's notes call "color adjacency"), and one at x 240–288, y 122–175 (48 wide, 1646 orange / 453 blue — the right orange barrel on its own; the 453 blue are its stripe).

### Step 2 — how many barrels is this blob? (`expected_width_px`, `split_blob`)

Section 2 says how wide one barrel is at the blob's distance. Which distance? If the blob's bottom is inside the frame, its bottom row gives the distance (the base sits on the table, and the bottom row of a thing on the table is the most reliable distance cue). If the blob touches the frame's bottom edge, the real bottom is somewhere below the frame and the row is meaningless, so the top row is used instead (`expected_width_px` makes that choice).

Divide the blob's width by that expected width and round to the nearest whole number: that is how many barrels stand side by side in the blob. One → done. Two or more → cut the blob into that many pieces. Where to cut: not at the exact halfway point but at the *narrowest column* near it — barrels bulge in the middle, so two touching barrels have a waist where they meet, and the column with the fewest barrel pixels in a window of ± a quarter barrel-width around the equal-spacing point is that waist (if several columns tie, the one nearest the equal-spacing point wins). Each piece gets its own tight box around its own pixels and its own orange/blue counts, and its **color is whichever count is larger** — that is the body color, because the stripe is always the minority of a barrel's pixels.

*`touch_02`, first blob:* bottom row 178 → the expected barrel width at that row is 53.4 px. 88 ÷ 53.4 = 1.65 → rounds to **2** barrels. The narrowest column near x = 193 is found and the blob is cut there: piece A, x 149–193, 1301 orange / 335 blue → **orange**; piece B, x 193–237, 462 orange / 1549 blue → **blue**. Second blob: 48 ÷ 51.8 = 0.93 → **1** barrel, 1646 orange / 453 blue → orange. Three barrels, correct colors, at this point without the geometry having drawn a single edge.

### Step 3 — the bottom edge (`predicted_bottom`, `resolve_bottom`)

This is the step that exists because the bottom edge is the one the world map will use and the one most often hidden (shadow, another barrel, clutter, the frame edge). The top row of a piece says how far away the barrel is — a 24 mm-tall thing whose top appears at that row can only be at one distance — and from the distance, section 2 says where its base must be: that is `predicted_bottom(cam, top_row)`. Then `resolve_bottom` compares the predicted bottom with the bottom the mask shows:

- If they agree to within a tolerance — 15 % of the predicted box height, but never less than 4 px — the seen bottom is kept (it was seen; the geometry is only a check).
- If they disagree, the mask's bottom is wrong for one of the known reasons (a shadowed face made the blob too short, a reflection below the barrel made it too tall, an occluder hides the base) and the **predicted bottom replaces it**, with the flag `bottom_inferred`.
- Exception: when both the seen bottom and the predicted one are at or below the frame edge, the box is simply cut at the frame (no inference needed, nothing visible to infer from).
- Finally the bottom is clipped to the frame and any box touching a frame edge gets `truncated`.

*`touch_02`, piece A:* top row 122 → axis 182 mm away → predicted bottom 182.6. Seen bottom 175. Tolerance = max(4, 0.15 × (182.6 − 122)) = 9.1 px; the difference is 7.6 → **seen bottom kept**, no flag. Same for the other two (differences 9.5 and 7.6 against tolerances 9.5 and 9.1).

*`far_02`, the middle orange barrel:* top row 106 → 290 mm → predicted bottom 144.4; the mask's bottom is 136 because the shaded lower face fell below the saturation floor; tolerance 5.8; difference 8.4 → **bottom replaced by 144**, flagged `bottom_inferred`. The hand-labeled truth has the bottom at 137, so here the inference overshot by 7 px: at 30 cm the camera numbers are good to about 5 px and the tolerance is only 6, so the geometry can "correct" a bottom that was in fact right. That is a known weakness of step 1 at far range (see "What can go wrong").

### Shape checks — is this piece barrel-shaped for its row?

After the bottom is settled, the expected width at that bottom row is computed once more and the piece must satisfy all of:

- expected width ≥ 16 px, i.e. the barrel is nearer than about 60 cm (anything "smaller" would be off the far end of a table — or above the horizon, where the expected width is infinite or zero);
- piece width between 0.4 × and 1.6 × the expected width (narrower is a sliver — a stripe fragment, a reflection edge; wider is not one barrel).

*`far_02`:* the hand resting on the table edge produces orange specks around rows 60–85, near the horizon. Expected width there is 1.8–4.4 px → **all dropped** by the 16 px rule. That is how the hand's fingers disappear without any "skin detector".

### Step 4 — the stripe is the barrel (`find_stripes`, `barrel_from_stripe`; built 2026-11-03, the default since 2026-11-05)

Steps 1–3 box anything barrel-colored and barrel-sized. Step 4 adds the one thing that proves "barrel": the **stripe**, a band of the other color around the middle. With it, every stripe is one barrel, wherever the blob boundaries happen to fall, so touching and partly hidden barrels separate by themselves, and an orange thing without a stripe is not a barrel.

*Finding stripes.* Take the orange pixels as "body" (then repeat with blue). For every column, note where body pixels exist above and below. A **band** is a connected region of non-body pixels that has body above it *and* below it in every one of its columns — that is the sandwich. The band's pixels are either blue (a proper stripe) or unlabeled (a stripe the segmentation lost to shadow): either counts, and the ratio sets the confidence. The band must have stripe proportions for its row: the height of its colored core (10th-to-90th percentile of its blue rows) between 0.45 and 1.6 × the stripe height the geometry expects there, the whole band (core plus shadow) at most 3 ×, and the band at least 0.4 barrel-widths wide and not taller than wide. A band wider than 1.4 barrels is the merged stripes of touching same-color barrels (their stripes meet at the bulges) and is cut into equal parts.

*A barrel from its stripe.* The stripe's middle row (the median row of its colored pixels) gives the distance; the distance gives the full box the geometry predicts: top, bottom and width (section 2's table). Then the seen body extent in the stripe's own columns — walked upward from the band and downward from it while body pixels keep coming, gaps of 3 rows allowed — supplies the top and bottom when they agree with the prediction within the same 15 % tolerance as step 3; otherwise the predicted edge is drawn and flagged (`top_inferred`, `bottom_inferred`). A stripe narrower than 0.8 barrel-widths is partly hidden: the box is widened to a full barrel, on the side where something barrel-colored borders the stripe (the occluder), and flagged `occluded`. Clip, `truncated` as before.

*Stripeless pieces.* The step-1 pieces are then only kept where no stripe barrel already covers them **and** a stripe could not have been seen: the piece reaches the frame bottom (a close barrel, stripe below the frame — `topstripe_01`, `top_01`) or the expected stripe at that distance is thinner than 4 px. Everything else without a stripe is dropped — the hand, a tomato, a reflection.

*The close-up piece, two corrections (2026-11-23, TODO A9, `experiments/2026-11-23_closeup_shape/`, branch `wip/closeup-shape`, on by default there, awaiting the walkthrough).* "Reaches the frame bottom" is decided on the **resolved** box: a piece whose seen bottom disagrees with the bottom its top row predicts gets the predicted bottom, clipped to the frame. So a flat orange print whose own pixels end at row 368 counted as "reaching the frame bottom" because its top row (286) predicts a base at row 653 — the box on the empty trap frame `4A8B_window_abpwhite_occlx_15`. For a piece cut only by the frame bottom the width is still a second reading that agrees with the top row (it must be 0.4–1.6 × the width that row predicts). For a piece that the frame's **side** cuts as well, the width says nothing (only a lower bound is seen), so nothing but the top row is left.

- **`closeup_side`.** A close-up piece that the frame's left or right side also cuts must be seen from its top row down to where that row puts the stripe's top (or down to the frame bottom when that is higher), within the step-3 tolerance (15 % of that height, at least 4 px). A real barrel there shows its upper body; a print shows its own shape. *The fruit:* top row 286 → axis distance 26 mm → stripe top at row 602, below the frame → it must be seen down to row 480 − 0.15 × (480 − 286) = 451; its pixels end at 368 → no box. *Dave's `snap_10`, a blue barrel cut by the right edge:* top row 338, seen down to 480 → kept. In the census (`results/2026-11-23_closeup_shape/census/census.md`) the three real side-cut close-ups are all seen to the frame bottom (reach 1.00), the fruit 0.42; the rule is not asked anywhere else on the train frames or on the 337 recorded frames.
- **`closeup_stripe`.** A piece whose top row puts the stripe's bottom row past the frame bottom is a close-up too. Between "stripe below the frame" (found as a close-up) and "body below the stripe in view" (found from the stripe) lies one position where the frame bottom cuts the stripe: the stripe finder needs body below the stripe and sees none, and the piece's seen bottom agrees with the predicted bottom, so it is not "reaching the frame bottom" either and was refused as stripeless. *`4A8B_overheaddim_palewood_close_01`:* top row 241 → stripe rows 434–483, base 508; seen bottom 477, within 40 px of 508 → before: no box; now (292, 241, 568, 477) against the label (291, 243, 566, 480). The census found this one piece of the kind on all train frames, real; nothing false.

Both read only the camera model and the existing tolerances; `closeup_side` is checked after `closeup_stripe` makes a piece a close-up, so a side-cut piece of either kind must show its upper body.

*`touch_02`:* three bands found — blue between orange at x 152–191, orange between blue at 189–235, blue between orange at 241–288 — three barrels, no width rule needed. *`cluster_01`:* three of the four bunched barrels have a visible stripe and come out right; the fourth, directly behind another of the same color, shows no stripe and is missed (and the width rule, no longer trusted alone, is not allowed to guess it). That is the honest limit: **a barrel whose stripe is completely hidden is not found.**

*Status.* The default since 2026-11-05 (`DetConfig.require_stripe=True`): it beat steps 1–3 on the train frames under both rules (below). `detect_image.py --step1` runs steps 1–3 alone for comparison.

### The hardening (a–g; 2026-11-08/09, walkthrough acknowledged and merged 2026-11-09)

The campus attribution (`EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-07) confirmed the two flaws pre-registered after the first live run and found a third; a fourth showed up under the new color model. Each is a `DetConfig` field, so the 2026-11-05 behaviour stays reproducible (`stripe_mode="bands"`, `body_min=0`, `height_max=0`).

- **(a) A dark gap is not a stripe** (`dark_stripe=False`). The 2026-11-03 finder accepted a band with *no* stripe-colored pixel as a "shadowed stripe". On the laptop-lid scene that made a floating box out of the dark gap under a false orange smear; under the hue model it made boxes out of gaps in the patchily labeled home wall. Off by default; `dark_stripe=True` restores it.
- **(b) The stripe is measured from its own pixels** (`stripe_mode="cores"`). The old band was *everything that is not body color* between body above and below, so unlabeled shadow glued to a stripe made its band taller than 3 × the expected stripe height and the stripe was dropped (campus `core_01`, `plain_01`, `close_01`, `window_01`). Now a stripe is a connected component of the *other* color whose every pixel has body color within 3 stripe heights above **and** below it (the sandwich, pixel by pixel — so a stripe touching a neighbouring barrel's body is cut from that body), with the same proportions checks as before on the component itself: height 0.45–1.6 × the expected stripe height, at least 0.4 barrels wide, not taller than wide; wider than 1.4 barrels → cut evenly as before. Shadow around the stripe changes nothing because it is never part of the component.
- **(c) A box must hold body color** (`body_min=0.1`). In the stripe's own columns, outside the stripe rows, the box must contain more body-colored than stripe-colored pixels, and at least 10 % body color. `core_01`'s pair: the blue barrel's body, lying between its own orange stripe below and the orange neighbour's bulge above, had been boxed as an orange barrel.
- **(d) A stripeless piece must be barrel-tall** (`height_min=0.5, height_max=1.5`). Step 3's shape checks looked at width only; a blue jacket above the table edge in `core_01`, 24 px wide and 45 tall with its bottom just under the horizon, passed as a "far barrel whose stripe is too thin to see". Now the piece's height must also be 0.5–1.5 × the barrel height the geometry expects at its bottom row (skipped for pieces cut by the frame edge).

- **(f) The box is as wide as the body, not the stripe** (`body_width=1.2`, 2026-11-09). A stripe's outermost pixels curve away from the camera and go dark, so a box copied from the stripe's columns ran about 15 % narrow on the big campus barrels (Tanush saw it on `core_02`; the IoU ≥ 0.5 match never would). Now, when the stripe is "full" (not the partly-hidden path), the box's left and right edges come from the body-colored columns joined to the stripe above and below it (`_body_columns`: walk outward while a column holds ≥ 2 body pixels, gaps of 2 allowed), never into another found stripe's columns (a touching same-color neighbour), and at most 1.2 × the expected width. Median box-width ratio on the campus train frames 0.90 → 0.96, worst 0.74 → 0.88 (`EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-09); the score is unchanged because the rule did not see the flaw. `body_width=0` is the old behaviour.
- **(g) A stripe barrel covers a stripeless piece only if they share rows** (2026-11-09). A step-1 piece is dropped when a stripe barrel "already accounts for it"; that test looked at columns only, so in the live run with Dave a far blue barrel standing above a close-up orange one in the picture made the close-up vanish (its own stripe was below the frame, the allowed stripeless case). The test now also requires the two boxes to overlap vertically (`cover_rows=True`; `False` = the old rule). Also in the detector on `barrels` today. Caution measured in the sweep re-run: with the *old* finder and v1 pixels, the rows test lets clutter pieces through (campus negatives 0 → 5 false boxes), because the column test had been masking them by accident; with the hardened finder and the hs pixels it changes nothing but the close-up.
- **(e) A stripe cut by the frame edge is hidden on that side.** The "partly hidden" rule widens a narrow stripe to a full barrel on the side where something barrel-colored borders it; a frame edge now counts as that side too, so a barrel half out of the frame gets its box drawn into the edge (and clipped, `truncated`) instead of into the empty table beside it (campus `edge_01`; found after the first v2 scoring on Tanush's boxes, so added after (a)–(d)).

*Numbers* (`experiments/2026-11-08_det_hardening/`, same pixels and camera for every row; before (e), which changes only frame-edge barrels): with the frozen v1 pixels, home train v2 **0.91 → 0.93** (orange 0.87 → 0.90, blue 0.96 unchanged, nothing on the negatives) and the campus train frames 7 → 8 right; under the hue model (probe fit) home **0.57 → 0.78**, Dave's 12 frames 0.31 → 0.40, campus 12 → 15 of 21 right, frames with an extra box 5 → 1, nothing on the four campus negatives. With the dark-gap path back on, every number is worse (home 0.67, campus 10, boxes on a negative).

### Occlusion, step 7 (2026-11-11, branch `wip/occlusion`; awaiting walkthrough; **defaults unchanged**)

What was asked: live with Dave a barrel with half its body showing got no box. What was done, in order:

1. **The reason log.** `detect`, `detect_step1`, `find_stripes` and `barrel_from_stripe` take an optional `log` list. When given, every candidate the detector drops is appended with the name of the test that dropped it (`area`, `height`, `width`, `too_far`, `body_min`, `covered`, `stripeless`, …) and the numbers it compared; kept stripes are logged as `kept`. With `log=None` (the default, and what every tool and the robot use) nothing is recorded. The answer is the same either way (tested).
2. **Attribution** (`experiments/2026-11-11_occl_attribution/`). On the 37 train frames the detector misses 5 barrels, all occluded, of 10 occluded ones. Two different causes:
   - *Hidden at the side by something that is not a barrel* (1: campus `occl_02`, behind the robot). The stripe is found, 16 px wide; the barrel is 53.6 px wide there; the width test wants 0.4 × 53.6 = 21.4 px. Refused by `stripe_w_min`. A stripe is ~15 % narrower than its barrel, so "half a barrel showing" sits right at this limit: that is the live miss.
   - *Behind a barrel of the other color* (4: home `occl_01`, `occl_02`, two in `cluster_01`). The back barrel's stripe has the front barrel's body color, the two join into one patch, and the patch is too tall to be a stripe (28–29 px against 15 expected). Refused by `height`. This is "mixed-color stacking", TODO step 9, not touched.
3. **The fix that was tried** (`experiments/2026-11-11_occl_narrow_stripe/`, rule written first in its `PREREG.md`):
   - `stripe_w_min` 0.4 → 0.25. Why a quarter: a stripe is about a quarter as tall as the barrel is wide and the finder already refuses a stripe taller than wide, so below a quarter width the width test adds nothing.
   - **(h) `occl_body_side`** (new switch, off). A narrow stripe says where the barrel is, not which side is hidden. Before: widen to a full barrel toward a barrel-colored neighbour, else centre on the stripe. With (h): first take the body-colored columns joined to the stripe above and below it (the same walk as (f)). If they already span 0.8 of a barrel, that span is the box. Otherwise widen that span by the old rule. A span wider than 1.2 barrels is a same-color neighbour and is ignored. On `occl_02` the robot's edge slants, so the blue body below the stripe shows almost the whole width: IoU with the truth 0.51 centred, 0.68 with (h).
4. **Result.** Both changes together find `occl_02` (campus 34 → 35 of 35 found), one more of Dave's barrels, and four real barrels entering at the frame edge in the recordings; no barrel lost, no false box on any home, campus or Dave frame. But they add **2 false boxes on the 25 wood frames** (where the table itself reads orange). The pre-registered rule said every added box must be a real barrel, so **nothing is adopted**: the defaults are as on `barrels`. Whether to switch them on before the wood front end is fixed is an open decision (STATUS).

### The detector given good segmentation (2026-11-18; walkthrough acknowledged, switched on and merged 2026-11-18)

What was asked: "if the segmentation is good, the detector finds the barrel", as a measured test, then fix what fails. The test (`experiments/2026-11-18_det_given_seg/run.py`) feeds the detector masks that are good by construction: the hand-made home masks (level 1), the same masks with measured damage from `degrade.py` (level 2: highlights, eaten edges, dropout, a stripe in pieces, specks; three severities), the campus frames with the adopted colors (3a) and the black-table frames with thresholds read from each frame's labels (3b). Two switches came out of it. **Both are off by default**: neither passed the pre-registered rule by the letter (below), so the detector that runs is unchanged.

**`stripe_join`: a stripe in pieces is one stripe (`_join_pieces`).** The stripe finder takes every connected piece of sandwiched stripe color and tests it alone. A pale patch 0.15 of a barrel wide across a stripe leaves two pieces: each is under the 0.4 width floor, or one passes alone as a "partly hidden" stripe and its box lands in the wrong place (level 2, that damage alone: 125 → 85 of 145 found, 35 false boxes). With the switch on, before the tests, the pieces are grouped: going left to right, a piece joins the group to its left when (1) they share rows (the overlap is at least half the height of the shorter one) and (2) together they span no more than one barrel (`body_width` 1.2 × the expected width). The reason is physical: two barrels cannot both have their stripe inside one barrel's width at the same rows, so pieces that fit there are one stripe. The joined group then takes the usual tests (area, height 0.45–1.6, at least 0.4 wide, the cut for merged stripes). One more condition, added in round 2: a piece joins only if it has a shape a stripe can have, i.e. not taller than 1.6 × the expected stripe height and not taller than wide. Without it, tall slivers of a barrel's own upper body between specks were joined into a "stripe" (4 false boxes at home in round 1). Touching same-color barrels are unaffected: their pieces span two barrels.

**`cap_behind`: a barrel standing behind a found barrel (`caps_behind`).** A barrel behind one of the other color shows its upper body, its "cap", above the front barrel. Its stripe has the front barrel's body color, the two join, and the patch is refused as too tall for a stripe: all 4 misses on the perfect home masks and 3 of the 9 on the black table. So the stripe cannot be the anchor. After the stripe barrels and the stripeless pieces are found, `caps_behind` runs once per color on that color's pixels without the boxes of the stripes already used. A connected piece is a cap when:

1. it is not a found barrel's own body (fewer than half of its pixels lie inside found boxes of its color);
2. its top row gives the distance (as in step 3), the distance gives the expected width W, height H and upper-body height (top row to the top of the stripe), and the piece is barrel-wide (0.4 to 1.2 × W) and as tall as an upper body, not more (0.5 × the upper body, to the upper body plus the usual edge tolerance). Body color running on below where the stripe must start is not a barrel top;
3. a cap that shows less than 0.8 × W must be explained: exactly one of its sides is hidden by a found barrel standing nearer (that barrel's box holds the pixel next to the cap at the cap's middle row) or by the frame edge. Hidden at neither side → not a cap. Hidden at both → no box (a wedge between two barrels: neither its width nor its place can be measured);
4. what hides the rest is a barrel: at least half of the predicted box below the cap lies inside boxes of found barrels whose bottom edge is lower (they stand nearer).

The box: top = the cap's top row. Sides = the cap's columns, widened to W toward the hidden side for a narrow cap. Bottom = top + the height of a barrel as wide as the cap when the whole width shows, else the bottom the top row predicts; and, as in step 3, if the cap's own color is seen in the cap's columns ending near that estimate (within 15 % of H, at least 4 px), the seen bottom is used. Flags `occluded`, `bottom_inferred`; `source` is `"det3"`. No new threshold: every number is one the detector already had.

*`occl_01` (home mask):* the blue cap is x 386–430, rows 115–135. Top row 115 → W 46.7, H 50.7, upper body 22.6. It is 44 wide (0.94 W, whole width) and 20 tall: a cap. A barrel 44 wide is 47.8 tall → bottom 163; blue is seen in those columns ending at row 160 (the sliver of lower body beside the front barrel), 3 px from the estimate → bottom 160. Everything below the cap is inside the found orange box. Box (386, 115, 430, 160); the hand-drawn one is (386, 115, 430, 161). *`cluster_01`:* the left orange cap is 37 wide where W is 49.3 (0.75 W, narrow); the found blue barrel's box holds the pixel to its right → widened right to 49; bottom from the top row 170.5, orange seen ending at 164 → 164. The middle orange barrel shows only a wedge between two blue barrels: both sides hidden, no box; it stays the one miss on the perfect masks.

*Why height from width and not from the top row.* The size check of this experiment (`size_check.md`): hand-drawn boxes agree with the camera model's size at their bottom row (median width ratio 0.95–1.03, height 0.96–0.99), but the bottom predicted from the **top row** is 4–7 px too low at mid and far range (home +4.4, campus +5.4, wood +7.1), while "top row + the height of a barrel as wide as the box" is off by a median 0.6–2.1 px. A tilt of about 21.8° instead of 22.25° (horizon row 80 instead of 76) would remove the bias on home and campus; that is the parked ruler calibration, not changed here.

### Occlusion: one idea, and the rules in the order the data flows (2026-11-18 to 2026-11-20; the state of branch `wip/occl-combined`, awaiting the walkthrough)

This section replaces five that were written branch by branch (side occlusion, hidden bottoms, the scorecard, up and down, the outline). The history, round by round, is in the experiments' result folders: `results/2026-11-19_det_side`, `2026-11-19_det_bottom`, `2026-11-19_det_occl`, `2026-11-20_occl_vertical`, `2026-11-20_occl_pin`, `2026-11-19_damage_realism`, `2026-11-20_stencil`, and the combination `2026-11-20_occl_combined` (`verdict.md`, `scorecard.md`, and `walkthrough.md`, the same logic in short sentences).

**What is on.** On by default: `stripe_join`, `cap_behind` (2026-11-18, merged), `side_hidden`, `seam_height`, `cap_seen_bottom`, `edge_check`, `cap_same` (2026-11-19), `half_stripe`, `half_thin`, `post_pair`, `stripe_wide`, `cap_low`, `pin_outline`, `pin_thin` (2026-11-20), `thin_span` (2026-11-20, A8, branch `wip/occl-sliver`). Built and **off**: `slice_behind`, `slice_box`, `cover_unpinned`, `stripe_two`, `occl_body_side`, `stripe_bridge`, `thin_frame`, `own_close` (A8). `stencil.py` (a stencil-matching second opinion) is not part of the detector.

**The one idea.** *What is seen fixes the distance; the distance fixes the full size; the box is the full barrel, placed where the seen part allows; a rule acts only when what is seen could not be anything else.* Every rule has three parts: a **reading** that gives the distance (the stripe's row, a seam, a top row), a **second reading that agrees** (a seen edge where the first predicts it), and a **place** (where the seen part pins a box W wide and H tall). No second reading, or nothing that pins the place: no box. A miss is allowed; a box in the wrong place is not.

**How it is measured: the scorecard (`occlude.py`, `experiments/2026-11-19_det_occl/run.py`).** There are too few real occluded frames to measure anything, so the test makes them. Each of the 50 barrels of the train frames that stands free (12 from the HOME hand masks, 12 from the same frames segmented as in the eval table, 26 from CAMPUS) is cut out of its mask and put alone on an empty mask at its own place, so its row and size are real. Then one thing hides 25, 50 or 75 % of it: the frame edge, a straight edge, a slanted edge, both sides, from above, from below, a post (pixels erased); a real barrel's pixels pasted in front, of the other or the same body color, scaled for where it stands (beside, beside and lower, straight in front); a plain block of barrel color. 3888 scenes, scored against the barrel's **full** box under the v2 rule. What it cannot show: a real occluder also throws a shadow, reflects and bleeds color at the seam. Next to it: the damaged masks (`degrade.py`: the three made-up levels light / medium / heavy, and since 2026-11-19 two levels measured on real white-table segmentation, "typical" and "bad day"; real masks are close to "typical", and "medium" is far harsher than anything measured), the eval table, and the 337 recorded frames by eye.

**The order in `detect`.** Stripe finder → stripe step (stripes seen whole first, then the partly seen ones, nearest first) → stripeless pieces → `caps_behind` → `post_pairs` → (`slices_behind`, off) → `half_stripes`. A rule runs after everything it leans on: a cap leans on found barrels; the left-over rules take only pieces that gave no box, and each adds a box only where no box of that color already is (overlap over half), so nothing found earlier changes.

#### In the stripe finder

- **`stripe_join`: a stripe in pieces is one stripe (`_join_pieces`).** Pieces of sandwiched stripe color that share rows (half the height of the shorter) and together span at most `body_width` (1.2) × W are one stripe, because two barrels cannot both have their stripe inside one barrel's width. A piece joins only if it is stripe-shaped (not taller than 1.6 stripe heights, not taller than wide). A pale patch 0.15 W wide across every stripe: 85 → 125 of 145 found.
- **`seam_height`: a stripe eaten thin at its seams.** Mixed pixels at the two seams are neither color, so a far stripe 8 px tall can be 4 px in the mask and fail the height floor (0.45). The body loses the same pixels, so the gap between the two body parts is taller by what the stripe lost. A stripe too thin by its own rows, and seen whole (0.8 W), is read again as the mean of its height and of that gap. *HOME `far_02` with 2 px eaten:* stripe rows 120–123 (4), gap rows 116–126 (11), reading 7.5 against 8.3 expected → a stripe.
- **`stripe_wide`: a stripe wider than a barrel is two barrels.** A stripe wider than 1.2 × W, with body color seen right above and right below it, is cut into max(2, round(width / W)) parts; each part is hidden where it was cut, so its box is W from its own outer edge. W here is the larger of the row's width and the width of a barrel as tall as the body seen. (`stripe_two`, off, is the same without the "body right above and below" guard: it cut one large near barrel in two.) *HOME `core_02`, orange (368, 120, 418, 173) half hidden by an orange barrel at (340, 124, 393, 178):* stripe columns 342–416, 74 px, W 50. Before: one box (348, 120, 410, 178). Now (342, 121, 396, 178) and (367, 120, 418, 178).
- **The width floor.** A stripe piece must be at least `stripe_h_min` (0.45) of a stripe height wide (since `pin_thin`; one stripe height with `side_hidden` alone; 0.4 W before). A piece under one stripe height wide, or taller than wide, is "thin" (`Stripe.small`) and is a barrel only by `pin_thin` below. Pieces under the old floor are also handed on as `narrow` pieces for `post_pairs` and `slices_behind`.

#### In the stripe step (`barrel_from_stripe`, `_partly_seen`)

The stripe's row gives the distance, hence W, H, the top and the bottom. Seen body edges are used when they agree with the prediction (15 % of H, at least 4 px).

- **`edge_check`: an edge is seen only against something that is not a barrel.** (a) A candidate with neither its top nor its bottom where its row predicts them, whose top ends against the other barrel color (that color in half of its columns in the 4 rows beyond), is refused: a thing right above a top, inside the box, stands behind and cannot hide it; such a band is a strip of a back barrel between its own stripe and the front barrel. (b) A kept seen bottom with the other barrel color right under it becomes the lower of seen and predicted. *HOME `core_01`, orange (292, 121, 342, 176) with a blue barrel pasted in front:* before, a wrong blue box (287, 124, 346, 188) and an orange box 9 px short; now the band is refused and the orange box is (293, 121, 340, 174), overlap 0.91.
- **`side_hidden`: a stripe narrower than 0.8 W is placed by what is seen.** What is seen = the stripe's columns joined with the columns holding body color above or below it (`_body_span`; if that is wider than 1.2 W a same-colored neighbour is in it and the body is not trusted). Seen span ≥ 0.8 W: that span is the box. Otherwise a side counts as hidden only for a reason: (a) the frame edge; (b) a found barrel standing nearer (its box holds one of the 3 pixels beside the seen span at the stripe's row and its bottom is lower than this barrel's predicted bottom); (c) the body and the stripe end at different columns there, further apart than the edge tolerance (at a barrel's own side they end together; a part cut from a joined stripe is hidden where it was cut). Exactly one side hidden: the box is W wide from the seen edge of the other. No side, or both: next rule. *CAMPUS `occl_02` (the live miss with Dave):* stripe columns 278–294, 16 px; row 154 → 188 mm, W 54, H 59. Body seen 277–311 (0.63 W). Left: body and stripe 1 px apart, the barrel's own edge. Right: 17 px apart (tolerance 8), cut. Box (277, 127, 331, 178); hand-drawn (275, 123, 328, 180); overlap 0.82.
- **`pin_outline`: with no reason on either side, the barrel's own corner pins it (`Outline`, `_seen_lines`, `_end_costs`, `_outline_place`).** The distance gives the whole outline as the camera sees it: top line, stripe line, bottom line, a row per column. Its arcs are 1 to 2 px and useless; its corners are large: in the last 8 % before a barrel's own side the top is 10 to 13 px lower and the bottom 13 to 17 px higher than in the middle (55 px barrel); a cut side has square corners. Each end of the seen part is asked by itself: with the outline's side put on that end, each of the three lines is compared with the seen one over half a lid's width (0.353 W); the end's cost is the **largest** of the three mean distances (after taking out each line's own up-down offset), in barrel heights. An end costing at most `pin_good` (0.05) is the barrel's own side. **Exactly one such end: the box is W wide from it.** Neither or both: as before (centred on what is seen if the stripe is at least 0.4 W, else no box). A box placed this way never replaces a found barrel's box. Why each end alone and the largest line: a slanted cut imitates one rounded corner and never both. *HOME `core_02`, left half behind a straight edge:* W 50, H 54, limit 2.7 rows; the cut end costs 4.6, the own end 1.8 → box (368, 120, 418, 172), overlap 0.98; before, centred: (380, 120, 431, 172), overlap 0.59.
- **`pin_thin`: a thin slice with one own corner.** A thin piece (three quarters hidden leaves a piece narrower than a stripe is tall) is a barrel only when it is a **full slice** (neither its top nor its bottom inferred: the body above and below ends where the piece's row predicts) **and** the outline finds exactly one own end; the box is W wide from that end. A thin piece inside a found barrel's box at its stripe's rows is that stripe's; thin pieces never change another barrel's box. *HOME `close_01`, orange (302, 143, 390, 235), three quarters behind a straight edge:* piece columns 368–385; row 194.5 → 124 mm, W 82, H 88; seen 0.26 W; limit 4.4 rows; left end 17.7, right end 3.1 → box (307, 145, 389, 231), overlap 0.87.

#### After the stripe step

- **`cap_behind`, `cap_seen_bottom`, `cap_same`, `cap_low`: a top behind a found barrel (`caps_behind`).** A barrel behind another shows its upper body; its stripe has melted into the front barrel. Per color, on that color's pixels without the boxes of the used stripes, a connected piece is a cap when (1) its top row gives the distance, and the piece is barrel-wide (0.4 to 1.2 W) and as tall as an upper body, not more (from 0.4 of an upper body with `cap_low`, 0.5 before, to the upper body plus the edge tolerance); (2) a cap narrower than 0.8 W has exactly one side hidden by a found nearer barrel or the frame edge; (3) at least half of the predicted box below the cap lies inside found barrels standing nearer. `cap_same`: a piece half inside found boxes of its own color is not skipped; its parts outside those boxes are tested (a barrel behind one of its own color is one blob with it). The box: top seen; sides the cap's columns (a narrow one widened to W toward its hidden side); bottom = top + the height of a barrel as wide as the cap when the whole width shows (a width does not depend on the tilt; the top row alone puts bottoms 4–7 px low with this camera), and the cap's own color seen ending near that estimate replaces it. `cap_seen_bottom`: for a narrow cap whose seen end is refused, the cap's color is read beside the front barrel; seen ending between the bottom of a barrel only as wide as the seen cap and the top-row bottom plus the tolerance, that end is the bottom. *HOME `occl_01`:* blue cap columns 386–430, rows 115–135; W 46.7, upper body 22.6; 44 wide, 20 tall; bottom 163 from the width, blue seen ending at 160 → (386, 115, 430, 160); hand-drawn (386, 115, 430, 161). *HOME `cluster_01` (`cap_seen_bottom`):* cap 32 px wide, seen end 162 against a top-row bottom of 173 (tolerance 8.25); limits 152.9 and 181 → bottom 162, box (315, 118, 366, 162), overlap 0.71: HOME 27 → 28 of 29. *HOME `core_01` (`cap_low`), blue (239, 123, 290, 178) three quarters behind an orange barrel:* top row 123 → W 57.3, upper body 28.5; the cap is 46 wide and 13 tall (0.46 of an upper body) → (241, 123, 287, 173), overlap 0.82.
- **`post_pair`: two slices behind a post are one barrel (`post_pairs`, `_full_slice`).** Among the pieces that gave no box (`narrow` pieces, and stripes left without a box because nothing places them), two are one barrel when each is under 0.4 W wide, they have one body color and share rows, each is a full slice (its body ends where its row predicts the top and the bottom, and not against the other barrel color), and their outer body edges are 0.8 to 1.2 W apart. Two barrels at one distance cannot have their outer edges within 1.2 W. The box runs from outer edge to outer edge. *HOME `core_02`, a post over the middle half:* pieces at columns 368–380 and 405–416, W 49.1, outer edges 50 px apart → (368, 120, 418, 173), the hand-drawn box.
- **`half_stripe`, `half_thin`: a stripe with body on one side only (`half_stripes`).** A barrel hidden from above shows its stripe and lower body; from below, its upper body and stripe; the finder wants body on both sides. A connected band of one barrel color with the other color right under it in at least 0.8 of its columns and right above it in at most 0.2 is "top hidden" (the reverse: "bottom hidden"). The **seam** between band and body is the reading: the stripe's bottom edge is 9 mm above the table, its top edge 15 mm, so the seam's row gives the distance. The band is a barrel's stripe when: it is not taller than a stripe (1.6 ×; `half_thin` drops the 0.45 floor, since half hidden from below leaves 2 to 6 rows of stripe); it is 0.8 to 1.2 W wide; **the body's far edge is where the seam predicts it** (the second reading) and inside the frame; the body joined to it is 0.8 to 1.2 W wide; the open side is clear of barrel color; the far edge ends against something that is not barrel color; it is not a found barrel's; and it is thinner than the body beside it (a stripe is 6 mm, a body part 9 mm or more). The hidden edge is drawn where the seam's distance puts it. A box the stripe step made from a corner of the same band is replaced. *HOME `core_02`, upper half hidden:* band columns 368–417, rows 146–154; seam 154 → 205.9 mm, W 49.5, top 117.1, bottom 170.9; orange ends at 173 (2.1 px off, allowed 8.1) → (368, 117, 417, 173), overlap 0.93. *Lower half hidden:* 5 rows of stripe, seam 142, top predicted 117.6 and seen 120 → (368, 120, 418, 172), overlap 0.98.

### The verify step: two facts checked on every rule's box (2026-11-24, branch `wip/occl-strict`, on there; awaiting the walkthrough)

What was asked (Tanush): the occlusion rules may be too lenient; strengthen them everywhere against random boxes without
breaking real cases, by logic. `experiments/2026-11-24_occl_strict/PREREG.md` lists, for every rule that can create a box,
what it accepts and which facts of a real barrel it never checks; `results/2026-11-24_occl_strict/verdict.md` has the
numbers. The facts: the layout (body, stripe, body), one distance for every seen size, every edge explained, no body past
the barrel's end, **a barrel is not seen through a nearer barrel**, the barrel stands on the table. Two of them separate the
random boxes from the real ones on every test bed; the others remove real barrels (a dark ottoman barrel shows neither
its top nor its bottom; clutter behind a barrel touches it) and are not used. The two run at the end of `detect`, on every
box, whatever rule drew it; each is a switch.

- **`verify_front`: a barrel cannot be seen through a nearer barrel (`verify_front`, `outline_layout`, `_evidence`).**
  Inside the outline of a barrel standing nearer (its bottom edge lower in the picture), every pixel is that barrel's own
  picture: its body color in its body rows, its stripe color in its stripe rows. The boxes are taken nearest first. For
  each, its *evidence* is what it was read from (the stripe, the cap piece, the band, the two slices, the stripeless piece;
  taken from the reason log). Evidence pixels that lie inside a kept nearer barrel's outline (between its seen top and
  bottom; the outline's rounded corners from `Outline`) and have the color that barrel's layout puts there (within half a
  stripe height of a seam, either color) are the nearer barrel's; the rest are the box's own. **Fewer than `min_area_px`
  (20) own pixels: the box was read from the nearer barrel's picture, dropped.** A real barrel behind another always has
  its visible part outside the nearer one's outline. It runs twice: after the stripe step and the stripeless pieces (so
  the caps, pairs and half stripes lean only on boxes that passed, and a cap can still find a barrel whose wrong stripe
  box was dropped), and at the end on every box. *ABP white `occlx_17`:* the far blue barrel (144, 117, 185, 160) has
  orange clutter right above its top; its blue upper body between that clutter and its own orange stripe was read as the
  blue stripe of a smaller orange barrel (161, 105, 196, 143). That "stripe" (166, 117, 183, 134) lies wholly inside the
  blue barrel, at its upper-body rows, blue: fewer than 20 pixels of its own → dropped. Same on gray `clutter_01` and on 9
  frames of the recordings (orange boxes on a hand above a blue barrel).
- **`verify_readings`: a box needs a second reading (`verify_readings`).** The stripe step reads the distance off the
  stripe's row; the second reading is a seen edge where that distance puts it: the top, the bottom, or the whole width (the
  body joined to the stripe seen at least `stripe_full` W wide). A partly seen stripe (flag `occluded`) placed W wide
  from a guessed side, with its top AND bottom both inferred, has only the stripe's row: dropped. Every other rule's box
  has a seen edge by construction (a cap its top, a half stripe its far edge, pairs and thin pieces top and bottom).
  *Glass `lampmid3_glass_neg_02`:* a tin with an orange lid and a blue label; a piece of the label (560, 144, 580, 155),
  0.4 W wide, made a box W wide from its left end, top and bottom both inferred → dropped. The dark ottoman barrels
  (`lampmid6_ottoman` core_01, clutter_01) also have both edges inferred, but their orange body shows the whole width:
  kept.

### How to run and how to score

```
python barrels/tools/detect_image.py barrels/data/raw/4A8B_window_light_touch_02.png
```

writes `barrels/results/detect_image/4A8B_window_light_touch_02_boxes.png` and prints one line per barrel: color, box, and how its edges were decided — "bottom seen" or "bottom inferred", "top inferred", "partly hidden, full box drawn", "cut by frame", and whether it came "from its stripe" or was a stripeless piece ("no stripe seen"). `--step1` runs steps 1–3 alone. `--json` also writes `<stem>_det.json` (the `Barrel` records) so a folder of them can be scored with `tools/eval_boxes.py --pred DIR [--rule v2]`. The experiment that produced the numbers below is `python barrels/experiments/2026-11-03_det_step2/run.py [--rule v2]`; it writes `results/2026-11-03_det_step2[_v2]/` with one sheet per frame (our boxes yellow/green, the hand-drawn truth in thin white, the stripes found in magenta) and the score tables for the floor, steps 1–3 and the full detector.

### What went wrong with steps 1–3 — and what step 4 (the stripe) fixed

- **Same-color occlusion.** A barrel partly behind another of the same color merges into one blob with it, and the width rule cannot tell "one barrel in front of another" from "one wide blob": `occl_01` and `occl_02` are missed. Four barrels bunched together (`cluster_01`) come out as two.
- **Anything orange or blue of about the right size gets a box.** The shape checks only ask "is this barrel-sized for its row", not "is this a barrel". A hand that *touches* a barrel is worse: in `far_02` the fingers merge with the right blue barrel into one blob whose top row is at the hand, so the geometry thinks the barrel is 2.5 m away and throws it out — a real barrel lost because of a hand.
- **Far range.** At 30 cm the tolerance (6 px) is about the size of the camera error (5 px), so the geometry can overrule a seen bottom that was right (`far_02`, 7 px). The calibration session will shrink the camera error; the tolerance may also need to be looser far away.

Step 4 (the stripe) attacks the first two directly and fixed them on the train frames (which is why it is the default): a band of one color sandwiched between the other color above and below, at the height the geometry expects, proves "this is a barrel", gives one barrel per stripe even inside a merged blob, and refuses hands and other orange or blue objects. What remains is a barrel whose stripe is entirely hidden behind another barrel.
