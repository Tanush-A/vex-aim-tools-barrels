# HOW-IT-WORKS, the short version — start here

One page to understand (or re-explain) the detector. The long form with worked numbers is `HOW-IT-WORKS.md`; the diagrams of how the files fit are `ARCHITECTURE.md`. State as of 2026-11-26 (the hs color model is the front end; the detector is ten stages with no on/off switches, collapsed on that day: `DETECTOR-MAP.md`).

**Naming note.** `EXPERIMENTS.md`, `DECISIONS.md` and `TODO.md` call the stripe step "detector step 2" (it was the second thing built, after "step 1" = blobs → count → bottom edge). Since the collapse of 2026-11-26 the detector is ten named stages (section 3 below, `DETECTOR-MAP.md`): "step 1" is stage 1, "step 2" is stages 2 and 3.

## The chain

**Photo (640 × 480) → class mask → barrels → photo with boxes.**

```
python barrels/tools/detect_image.py barrels/data/raw/4A8B_window_light_touch_02.png            # the detector
```

Nothing learns from the photo it is looking at. The only learned numbers are two per color — a hue range and a saturation floor — fit once from labeled frames. Everything else is physics: barrel size, camera height, tilt.

## 1. Color segmentation (`pipeline.py`, the hs model since 2026-11-09)

Every pixel becomes orange (1), blue (2) or other (0). A pixel is a barrel color when it is below row 50 (above the horizon nothing is a barrel), brighter than 40, its **hue** is inside the class's range and its **HSV saturation** ((max − min)/max: a shaded navy face keeps it high, a gray wall never reaches it) is above the class's floor. Orange: hue 3–13, S ≥ 140. Blue: hue 103–119, S ≥ 113. Those four numbers were read off the 16 labeled home frames once; nothing is estimated from the image being looked at. Scores: campus 0.99 vs 0.41 for the old front end, home 0.93 unchanged, nothing on any empty frame.

**What it replaced (v1, until 2026-11-09):** a saturation floor and a wall color estimated **per image** from the top 50 rows, then fixed RGB boxes. That assumed the top rows are a plain wall; in a real room (Dave's office, the campus study room) they are clutter, the floor jumped to 96–120, and the barrel bodies themselves were thrown away. Kept as `--model results/2026-10-20_v1_test_adaptive/config.json`; `HOW-IT-WORKS.md` §1, second half.

**Open question (TODO steps 8 and 10):** the thresholds are fixed, so a strong color cast can move orange out of its hue range. The plan is to look at hue/saturation histograms across light settings first, then decide what an adaptive version keys on.

**The adaptive rule (2026-11-23, branch `wip/adaptive`, OFF by default, not adopted).** A switch, `Config.hs_adapt`: per frame, a threshold moves along a line through the home point, `fixed + b · (feature − home's feature)`, clipped, where the feature is one number from that frame's own histogram (pixels never changed, nothing from other frames). Pre-registered: the two orange hue ends follow `orange_peak_h`, the blue saturation floor follows `blue_s_p90`; `b` fit on Tanush's slider labels with every place weighted equally (`src/barrels/adaptive.py`, places in `data/conditions.json`). Judged leave one *place* out by `python barrels/experiments/2026-11-24_adaptive/run.py` (exit 0 = passes its PREREG). Tonight: fails (3 labelled places, a new box on an empty frame, the ottoman worse). `HOW-IT-WORKS.md` §1, "The adaptive rule".

**The blue box follows the light (2026-11-25, branch `wip/color`, OFF by default, not adopted).** A switch, `Config.hs_light` (`src/barrels/lightcolor.py`): the light's warmth is read from the frame's brightest pixels ((R − B) / (R + G + B), the "white patch"), and the more yellow it is the lower the blue saturation floor (113 → 40) and the blue hue low end (103 → 92); orange and the pixels are untouched. It finds the washed-out blue under the bathroom lamp (bathroom middle and dim 2 → 18 of 30 barrels) but, judged leave one place out, adds false boxes and one box on an empty glass frame: it failed its pre-registered test in both rounds, as did a re-centred constant and the two together. `HOW-IT-WORKS.md` §1, "The blue box follows the light"; `results/2026-11-25_color/test/summary.md`.

## 2. Camera geometry (`geometry.py`)

Pinhole camera 43.47 mm above the table, tilted down 22.25°, focal length 400 px. Two formulas: a point at distance d and height z lands on row `240 + f·tan(atan((h − z)/d) − tilt)`, and backwards a row gives a distance. The barrel is 24 mm tall, 25.5 mm wide at the bulge, stripe 6 mm at mid-height. So for a barrel at any distance the geometry says its top row, stripe rows, bottom row and width (`barrel_rows`); and seeing any one of those tells you the distance and therefore all the others. At 20 cm: 51 px wide, 55 tall, bottom at row 174. Horizon row 76.

The tilt was corrected on 2026-11-03: the October estimate (23.3°) put every predicted bottom ≈ 20 px too low; fitting from 24 labeled barrels gives 22.25°. Labeled boxes pin the horizon row, not f and tilt separately; the known-distance calibration frames will do that.

## 3. The detector (`detector.py`)

**Now (2026-11-27):** ten stages in a fixed order, 18 numbers, **no on/off switches**. The detector was collapsed on 2026-11-26 and drew exactly the boxes it drew before. Since then one rule was adopted: the second reading of a stripe at its seams (stage 2; *branch `wip/adopt-slack`, awaiting Tanush's "understood"*). **The guide to every check, with its number and the case it exists for, is `DETECTOR-MAP.md`.** The long form with worked numbers is `HOW-IT-WORKS.md` §3. When each rule was added: `archive/DETECTOR-HISTORY.md`.

One idea: *what is seen fixes the distance; the distance fixes the full size; the box is the full barrel, placed where the seen part allows.* A rule needs a reading that gives the distance, a second reading that agrees, and something that pins the place. Without those, no box: a miss is allowed, a box in the wrong place is not.

The stages, in the order `detect` runs them:

1. **Pieces** (`find_pieces`). Group touching barrel-colored pixels, orange and blue together; close 3 px gaps; drop specks under 20 px. Cut a blob that is two barrels wide at its waist. The top row gives the distance, the distance says where the base must be: keep the seen bottom if it agrees within 15 % of the height (min 4 px), else draw the predicted one. Drop a piece that is not on the table (nearer than ≈ 64 cm), has its top above the horizon, is not barrel-wide (0.4–1.6 × the width), or is far too short (under 0.5 × the height). No box yet.
2. **Stripes** (`find_stripes`). A stripe is a patch of the *other* color with body color right above *and* right below it (within 3 stripe heights, pixel by pixel). It must have stripe proportions for its row (0.45–1.6 × the expected stripe height) and 20 px. A stripe a highlight cut in pieces is joined; a stripe wider than a barrel is cut in two. **A stripe too thin or too small is measured again from the seams** (`read_at_seams`): mixed pixels at the two seams belong to neither color, so the gap between the body above and below tells how many pixels were eaten at each edge, and the stripe's height is the mean of its own height and that gap. Asked only when the barrel is seen whole: a thin stripe by its width (eaten pixels given back, 0.8 W) or by the rest of the barrel; a small one (under 20 px) only by the rest of the barrel (`rest_of_barrel_agrees`: the top and bottom where the stripe's row puts them, not against the other color, and one side where body and stripe end together).
3. **One box per stripe** (`boxes_from_stripes`). The stripe's row gives the distance, the distance the full box. Seen edges are used when they agree. A stripe at least 0.8 of a barrel wide: the box is as wide as the body joined to it. A narrower one is **partly seen**: the box comes from what is seen (stripe plus body); a side counts as hidden only for a reason (the frame edge, a found nearer barrel, body and stripe ending apart); else the barrel's outline says which end is its own; else the box is centred, or there is none. The box must hold body color.
4. **Close-ups** (`closeup_pieces`). A piece with no stripe is kept only when its stripe is below the frame.
5. **Verify** (`drop_seen_through`). A barrel cannot be seen through a nearer barrel: a box read from a nearer barrel's own picture is dropped.
6. **Caps** (`caps_behind`). A barrel-wide top showing above a found nearer barrel is a barrel behind it.
7. **Pairs** (`post_pairs`). Two thin slices at the same rows, one barrel apart, are one barrel behind a post.
8. **Half stripes** (`half_stripes`). A stripe with body on one side only is a barrel hidden from above or from below.
9. **Verify again** (`drop_seen_through`), on every box.
10. **Verify the readings** (`drop_single_reading`). A box placed from a guessed side, with neither its top nor its bottom seen, is dropped.

`detect(..., log=[])` lists every candidate dropped and the check that dropped it; it never changes the answer. `report.py --cand-det name=value` changes one of the 18 numbers. Experiments dated before 2026-11-26 name switches that no longer exist: they run at the git tag `pre-collapse-2026-11-26` (`experiments/README.md`).


**Numbers** (v2 rule, train frames; `EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-08 later, 2026-11-09): campus **0.99** (v1 front end 0.41), home **0.93**, nothing drawn on any negative of either session; median box width / true width 0.96. History: home with steps 1–3 alone 0.71, with the stripe step 0.91 (2026-11-03).

**Works:** single barrels at any distance; touching rows, same or mixed colors; bunched barrels that each show a stripe; a barrel hidden up to about a half from the side, from above, from below, by a post or by another barrel (on `wip/occl-combined`; the kinds hidden by a thing that is not a barrel are measured on simulated scenes only); a barrel behind another, from its top; close barrels with the bottom below the frame, even under a far barrel; empty frames; barrels cut by the frame side; the campus room, the laptop lid, the window behind.
**Doesn't yet:** a barrel three quarters hidden unless a found barrel or its own corner places it; a same-colored barrel straight in front hiding half; a hand or a chair right behind a barrel sometimes gets a second, wrong box (11 in the 238 recorded campus frames, before and after the occlusion work); a bright blue object at the frame edge above the table gets a box; a barrel touching the kicker (unconfirmed); the black table under glare and the orange wood table (parked as extreme).

## 4. Scoring (`eval_det.py`)

A predicted box matches a true box if same color and overlap (intersection over union) ≥ 0.5; greedy, each true barrel used once. Precision = hits ÷ boxes drawn; recall = hits ÷ barrels; F1 blends them; the headline is the mean of the orange and blue F1. The v2 rule (`--rule v2`) adds "bottom edge within a tenth of the box height" and counts every barrel, occluded ones included; a box on a clean empty frame disqualifies.

**The one table (`report.py`, `tools/report.py`, 2026-11-11).** `python barrels/tools/report.py` answers "what is better where" on one screen. Rows: each data set (HOME, CAMPUS, DAVE\*, and any new `{robot}_{light}_{surface}` session that has box labels, by itself) broken down by scenario tag, with a subtotal per set. Columns, for a **candidate** and a **baseline** side by side and then their difference: barrels found / missed, false boxes, median box width ÷ true width, median bottom-edge error in px; on the subtotal rows the headline score and the false boxes on empty frames (`DQ` = on a clean one). An arm is a color-model file plus `DetConfig` numbers (there are no switches since 2026-11-26), so a change can be compared with today: `--cand-det stripe_merge=1.68 --base-model default`. Defaults: today (hs + `DetConfig()`) vs the v1 front end with the same detector. It only presents — every number is `eval_det`'s v2 rule. Train frames only; it refuses test frames unless `--split test` is typed. HOME is scored leave-one-scene-out (the color model learned from those frames, so each scene is segmented by a model re-fit without it); the other sets use the model file as it is. A full `pytest` run prints the same table at the end (`BARRELS_NO_REPORT=1` skips it). Note: v1 + today's detector scores 0.39 on campus; the published 0.41 is v1 + the 2026-11-05 detector (`experiments/2026-11-11_eval_report/run.py` prints both).

**The vendor next to ours (`report.py --vendor`, 2026-11-18, branch `wip/eval-vendor-gate`, awaiting walkthrough; the default table is unchanged).** `tm save` stores what the robot's own detector reported with every frame (`data/vendor/<stem>_vendor.json`). `--vendor` makes the vendor the baseline arm of the one table: its boxes are read from those files and scored by the same v2 rule against the same labels, so the delta block is "ours minus the vendor". Both arms are scored only on the frames that have a vendor file (the footer says how many of each set); a file that says `available: false` means "not measured" and the frame is left out, never scored as "saw nothing"; balls, robots and tags are ignored and counted; a vendor box on an empty table is a false box. Because the vendor may not draw boxes the way our labels are drawn, two looser counts are printed under the table for both arms: **overlap** (same color, IoU ≥ 0.5, bottom edge not checked) and **place** (same color, box centers at most half the true box's width apart). They are reporting only; the score stays the v2 rule. Checked on the real files 2026-11-23 (`experiments/2026-11-24_vendor/run.py`): the ×2 conversion is right; the vendor's boxes are a little larger than the hand boxes (median IoU 0.82 on free-standing barrels) and cover only the visible part of a hidden barrel.

**Counting per placement, and a misplaced box once (`counting.py`, `report.py --places`, 2026-11-26, branch `wip/measure`, awaiting walkthrough).** The v2 rule is unchanged and is called *strict*. Two things around it changed. (1) A *placement* is one barrel in one arrangement; shot under five lamp settings it is still one barrel, and it counts the share of its lights in which it is found. Tables are by *place* (table and room, `data/conditions.json`) and say how many placements they rest on: the 243 train barrels of the new ordinary conditions are 108 placements at 6 places. (2) A box that sits on a real barrel of the right color but fails the strict rule used to be a miss plus a false box. Now the barrel is *found, box off* and the box is not a false box. A box is *on* a barrel when at least half of the smaller of the two boxes lies inside the other. The boxes that remain are a wrong color, a second box on a barrel, or on nothing. In the vendor comparison the two white tables without vendor files get a row marked ASSUMED (the vendor counts as finding every barrel there; Tanush 2026-11-26), never part of a measured total. Per placement on the measured places: ours 70.8, the vendor 83.3 of 108; the vendor's 30 "false boxes" are 23 boxes on a barrel and 7 on nothing. `HOW-IT-WORKS.md` §4, "Counting per placement".

**The never-regress check, with a margin (`gate.py`, `report.py --gate`, 2026-11-26, same branch).** A principle, not a strict rule (Tanush, 2026-11-26): a change may not make the protected sets worse by more than a change that means nothing moves them. `--gate` runs the detector and compares it with `results/baseline/baseline.json` (one line per frame, made by `--save-baseline`). A protected set is judged on its total: it fails when found (strict, per placement) is lower by more than its margin, or the boxes that are not on their barrel are higher by more than its margin. Protected (`results/baseline/protected.json`): HOME, CAMPUS, and new: the glass table, pale wood, the ABP white occlusion set, the bathroom under the cool lamp. Margins (`results/baseline/margins.json`): one placement and one box, except the cool-lamp bathroom, 5 placements. They are measured: the largest change that one step of one color threshold (and 12 joint moves) produced in that set (`experiments/2026-11-26_noise_floor`). One hard line stays: a new box on a clean empty frame fails in any set. Every barrel and box that changed is listed, pass or fail. `--save-baseline` refuses a run that is lower anywhere, even inside a margin, unless `--force`. On today's defaults: PASS.

**The standard pass rule (`judge.py`, `tools/judge.py`, 2026-11-26, same branch).** One rule for every experiment. A candidate is any function `detect_frame(stem, frame) -> list[Barrel]`, or a color-model file plus `DetConfig` fields. It and the baseline (today's default) are run on the ordinary train conditions (154 frames, 172 placements, 8 places); test frames are refused. Four clauses with measured margins: found over all places (better above +4.00 placements, worse below −10.92), found at each place (worse below that place's margin), boxes that are not on their barrel (worse above +1.85), boxes on empty frames (none new on a clean one; with clutter +1.00). FAIL = any clause worse. PASS = none worse and found better. NO CLEAR DIFFERENCE = neither. A candidate with fitted numbers is given as `fit(train_stems, held_place)` and judged leave one place out. `python barrels/tools/judge.py --cand-det field=value` (or `--cand-model`, `--cand-callable file.py:name`, `--cand-fit file.py:name`), about 25 s. On known cases: today against itself, nothing changed; the hand thresholds PASS (133.8 → 156.8 placements); "blue follows the light" leave one place out, PASS for the whole-frame reading (+5.9, close to the margin), no clear difference for the other. `EVAL.md`, "The standard pass rule (2026-11-26)".

## 5. On the robot (`live/`, `worldmap_bridge.py`, `tracker.py`)

Two small aim_fsm state machines on one base (`live/barrel_live.py`). **`BarrelViewer`**: once a second, take `robot.camera_image`, hand it to `BarrelDetectorHook.view`, show the result with Dave's `imshow`; every frame is answered on its own, exactly as a photo is. **`BarrelTracker`**: five times a second, the same detector, but a barrel is shown only once it has been seen in 3 of the last 4 frames, stays through a missed frame and is forgotten after 4 misses in a row (`tracker.py`; the box drawn is the median of the recent ones), so one-frame blips never show and a barrel lost for a frame does not blink. The hook converts RGB → BGR, runs sections 1 and 3, draws with `detector.draw`, converts back. At the `C>` prompt: `tm seg` / `tm boxes` / `tm both` switch the view. `tm seg` (since 2026-11-11): the main window dims every pixel that is not classified orange or blue to 30 % brightness (barrel-colored pixels stay as the camera gave them), and a second window, `<title> seg`, shows only the classified pixels, in their class color on black; `tm both` is the same with the boxes on the main window; `tm boxes` closes the second window (`wb.seg_views`, `hook.seg_only`, `BarrelLive.show`). Also: `tm save` / `tm rec` write raw frames to `live/snapshots/` for importing, `tm raw` (tracker only) overlays the per-frame boxes in white. Run: `cd barrels/live && simple_cli BarrelViewer` or `simple_cli BarrelTracker` (`RUNBOOK.md`). `BARRELS_MODEL=<results config.json>` in the environment swaps the front end (e.g. v1 for a side-by-side).
