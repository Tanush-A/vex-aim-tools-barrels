# HOW-IT-WORKS.md — the main code, in plain English

Written for Tanush, so he can explain the detector to Dave without help. One section per **main** module: color segmentation, camera geometry, the detector, the detection eval, plus the live feed. Side tools (labelers, importers) are not covered — they are allowed to be black boxes. The one-page version is `HOW-IT-WORKS-SHORT.md`; the file-level diagrams are `ARCHITECTURE.md`. Naming: what the lab notebook calls "detector step 2" (the stripe) is **stages 2 and 3** of section 3 here; the detector's own guide, check by check, is `DETECTOR-MAP.md`. Rule since 2026-11-02 (`DECISIONS.md`): every commit that changes main code updates its section here and gets a walkthrough in chat first.

Every number in this file is a real number from the code or from a real frame, so you can check it: `4A8B_window_light_touch_02` (three touching barrels, orange–blue–orange, about 18 cm away) is the worked example throughout.

## 0. The whole thing in one paragraph

A photo comes in (640 × 480). **Section 1** looks at every pixel and says "barrel orange", "barrel blue" or "other", producing a *class mask* — an image the same size holding 0, 1 or 2 per pixel. **Section 3** takes that mask, groups the barrel pixels into blobs, works out how many barrels each blob is and where each barrel's edges are, using the camera geometry from **section 2** whenever an edge cannot be seen. **Section 3b** (since 2026-11-27) then looks at the photo itself for the barrels the colors of section 1 missed: a stripe bluer or redder than the body above and below it; it paints that barrel in its own colors and adds its box. Out comes a list of barrels, each with a color and a box, and the photo is written back with the boxes drawn on it. **Section 4** is how we score that against hand-drawn truth. One function does 1 → 3b, `pipeline.detect_frame(frame)`, and the one command that runs it on a photo:

```
python barrels/tools/detect_image.py barrels/data/raw/4A8B_window_light_touch_02.png
```

Nothing in this chain learns anything from the photo it is looking at. The only "learned" numbers are two color boxes in section 1, fit once from labeled frames; everything else is either computed fresh from the image (the saturation floor, the wall model) or is physics (barrel size, camera height, tilt).

## 1. Color segmentation — `src/barrels/pipeline.py` (the hs color model, config `eb863d84`, the front end since 2026-11-09; v1 `afd85483` kept below as history)

**What it does.** Takes one image and labels every pixel as barrel-orange (1), barrel-blue (2), or other (0). (The same file holds `detect_frame`, the whole default path, photo in, boxes out: this step, the detector of section 3, the stripe by contrast of section 3b.) This is the front end of everything after it; the detector works on these pixel labels, not on the raw image. (A fourth value, 255 = "ignore", exists only in hand-painted label masks for glare we did not want to score.)

### The "hs" color model — the front end (built 2026-11-08, adopted 2026-11-09)

**Why.** On the campus frames (2026-11-07) tests 2 and 3 above removed the barrels themselves: with a dark, cluttered room in rows 0–50 the floor came out at 67–120 and the wall model's spread at 35–52 per channel, so navy-blue bodies (saturation 52–84) and shaded orange faces were rejected as "background" — blue bodies 0–7 % labeled in nine frames (`EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-07, attribution). Every adaptive part assumed the top rows are a plain wall and the barrels the only colorful thing in the picture.

**The idea.** Describe a barrel color by two numbers that do not change with brightness, and read both off the labeled frames once:

- **Hue** (the position on the color circle, OpenCV units 0–179): orange barrels sit at about 5–12, blue ones at 104–118, in the home frames and on campus alike. Hue is computed from the ratios of R, G and B, so a lit face and a shaded face of the same barrel have the same hue. Orange sits next to the wrap at 0/180, so its hue is rotated by +30 before taking percentiles (a sector 3–13 would otherwise read as "3 to 168").
- **HSV saturation**, S = (max − min) / max, 0–255: how far from gray a color is *relative to its brightness*. A navy-blue shaded face (RGB 12, 45, 85) has S = 219 even though v1's max − min is only 73; a bluish-gray wall (160, 150, 140 or so) stays under 110. This is the number that separates a dark barrel from a gray wall, which v1's max − min could not.

Per class: the S floor = the `hs_s_pct` percentile of the labeled pixels' S; the hue sector = the `hs_h_pct` percentiles of the labeled pixels above that floor, widened by `hs_h_margin` on each side. Then a pixel is that class when it is below row 50, brighter than 40, inside the sector and above the floor; in both → the redder one is orange (the same tie rule). **No per-image floor, no wall model, no table model**: nothing is estimated from the image being segmented.

**The adopted numbers** (fit once on the 16 home train frames, `hs_s_pct=(5, 15), hs_h_pct=(5, 95), hs_h_margin=2`, config `eb863d84`, saved in `results/2026-11-09_hs_candidate/config.json`): **orange hue 3–13, S ≥ 140; blue hue 103–119, S ≥ 113.** Two floors because orange wants a low one (the small dull orange stripe of a far blue barrel, `room_01`) and blue a higher one (the bluish home wall enters the blue class below S ≈ 110). The campus `room_01` blue body (RGB ≈ 12, 45, 85; hue 108, S 219) passes; the home wall in the blue hue sector (S ≤ 110) does not; skin on the campus hand (hue 13, S ≈ 149) passes the orange floor of 140 in places and is kept out of the boxes by the detector's stripe requirement; the orange glue-stick cap (hue 9, S 222) passes and is refused by the detector because nothing sandwiches a band in it.

**Where it lives.** `worldmap_bridge.DEFAULT_MODEL` → the saved model file (what `detect_image.py`, `segment_image.py` and both live programs load; `BARRELS_MODEL` in the environment overrides it, `worldmap_bridge.V1_MODEL` is the old one). `Config(color_model="hs", hs_s_pct, hs_h_pct, hs_h_margin)`; `Frame.hs(shift)` (the hue/saturation planes, cached per shift); `fit_hs` (called by `fit`); the `"hs"` branch at the top of `segment`. The fields are omitted from the config hash at their defaults, so every v1 hash (`afd85483` included) is unchanged. `pipeline.HUE_SHIFT` = {orange: 30, blue: 0}.

**How it was chosen** (`EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-08 and 2026-11-08 later; `DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-09). 18 configurations (orange percentile 5/10/15 × blue 10/15/20 × margin 2/4), scored by a rule written before the run: home leave-one-scene-out v2 ≥ 0.91, zero false detections on every negative frame of both sessions, then the campus v2 score on Tanush's boxes. Winner `o5 b15 h5-95 m2`: **campus train 0.99** (v1: 0.41), home 0.93 (= v1), nothing on any negative, Dave's office 0.47 (indicative). An earlier count-level sweep's rule had picked a home-regressing config; that rule flaw is recorded, and the per-class floor was the fix.

**What can go wrong.** Hue is noise where S is low, so dark low-saturation pixels are excluded by the floor rather than by hue; the orange sector (3–13) touches skin and wood hue — the S floor (140) removes most skin and the detector's stripe requirement the rest; wood tables (in scope since 2026-11-09) will segment as orange and are the detector's problem; a bright blue object in the room (a jacket, a chair) is labeled blue, and only the detector's stripe and size checks keep it from becoming a barrel.

### The adaptive rule — built 2026-11-23 on `wip/adaptive`, OFF by default (`Config.hs_adapt`; not adopted)

**Why.** The six numbers above were fit at home. On the black ottoman under a lamp the fixed model finds 0–6 of 15 barrels while Tanush's hand-set numbers find 12–15; on the ABP gray table 10 against 15 of 16. The plan with Dave: if something in the frame's own histogram predicts the hand-set numbers, that becomes the rule.

**The idea, in one line.** `threshold(frame) = fixed + b · (feature(frame) − home's feature)`, clipped to ± a limit. The feature is one number read off **this frame's own** hue × saturation histogram (the pixels below row 50 with V ≥ 40; no label, nothing from earlier frames). At home the feature equals home's mean, so the rule gives the adopted model; elsewhere the threshold moves in proportion to how far the frame's histogram is from home's. Pixels are never changed (no white balance): only the two boxes move, per frame.

**The rule as pre-registered** (`experiments/2026-11-24_adaptive/PREREG.md`): three terms, the three thresholds that change what is found on today's frames: the orange hue ends (`orange_h_lo`, `orange_h_hi`, clip ± 10) on `orange_peak_h` (the tallest hue in −10..30 among pixels with S ≥ 40) and the blue floor (`blue_s_lo`, clip ± 60) on `blue_s_p90` (the 90th percentile of S in the blue hue window). The other three stay fixed. `b` is the only fitted number per term: least squares through the home point on the slider labels, **every place weighted equally** (the four ottoman lamps are one place). A frame with too few pixels in the window has no reading and keeps the fixed value.

**Worked example.** Blue term with b = 1, home's `blue_s_p90` = 100, clip 60: a frame whose most saturated blues read p90 = 151 gets a blue floor of 113 + 51 = 164; a dull frame reading 71 gets 113 − 29 = 84. (The real fitted slopes are much smaller: tonight b ≈ +0.04 for the blue floor, +0.2 for the orange ends.)

**Where it lives.** `src/barrels/adaptive.py`: `Term`, `frame_feature_values` (calls `hsrel.gated_hist` and `hsrel.frame_features` with no reference), `adapted_boxes` (moves the boxes; an orange range that comes out empty keeps the fixed box), `fit_slope`, `place_weights`. `pipeline.Config.hs_adapt` holds the terms as plain tuples; empty (the default) = off and left out of the config hash, so `eb863d84` is unchanged. In `segment`, the `"hs"` branch calls `adaptive.adapted_boxes` only when `hs_adapt` is set; with the home leave-one-scene-out re-fit, the rule moves the re-fit model's own numbers. A model file with the rule on: `results/2026-11-24_adaptive/model_adaptive.json` (`tools/report.py --gate --cand-model <file>` takes it). Places and light kinds per condition: `data/conditions.json` (`src/barrels/conditions.py`).

**How it is judged.** `python barrels/experiments/2026-11-24_adaptive/run.py`: leave one *place* out (fit without the place, detect on its frames), the never-regress gate with the rule on, and a copy of the rule frozen on 2026-11-23 scored on places that are new since. Exit 0 only if every clause of the PREREG holds (at least 5 hand-labelled places, ≥ 3 more barrels found, ≤ 2 more false boxes, no new box on an empty frame, no condition losing > 0.02, no new gate failure, the frozen rule holding on new places). **2026-11-23: FAIL** (3 hand-labelled places; ABP gray 10 → 16 of 16 but one new box on an empty frame; the ottoman gets worse, 9 → 4 of 45, because its orange peak sits below home's and the fitted slope narrows the range where the hand labels widen it). Off; nothing changes unless Tanush adopts it after a walkthrough.

### The blue box follows the light — built 2026-11-25 on `wip/color`, OFF by default (`Config.hs_light`; not adopted)

**Why.** Under a yellow lamp the blue paint loses its saturation. On the bathroom floor at the lamp's middle setting a blue body reads hue 98, S 78 (RGB 43, 56, 61) against the adopted floor of 113, so it is never painted and the orange barrels lose their stripe: the fixed model finds 1 of 15 barrels there, the vendor 8-9. Under the warm setting the blue paint reads gray-olive (hue 26-44, S 44): no blue box can reach it without the floor (`results/2026-11-25_color/gates.md`). Under daylight the opposite holds: white tables and gray walls read slightly blue, which is why the floor is 113.

**The idea, in one line** (Tanush's picture: "if the whole image is more yellow, shift the thresholds that way"): read how yellow the *light* is from the frame itself, and lower the blue saturation floor and the low end of the blue hue range the more yellow it is. Pixels are never changed; only the blue box moves, per frame.

**How** (`src/barrels/lightcolor.py`). The light's warmth = the median of (R − B) / (R + G + B + 1) over the brightest pixels (the "white patch": white and pale things take the light's color). Share t = clip((warmth − w0) / (w1 − w0), 0, 1); blue S floor = 113 − t · (113 − s_warm), blue hue low = 103 − t · (103 − h_warm); only ever lowered, orange untouched. `Config.hs_light = (w0, w1, s_warm, h_warm)` reads the brightest 5 % of the whole frame; the six-number form `(…, rows, top)` reads the brightest `top` % of the rows above `rows` only. Empty = off, and then the config hash is unchanged (`eb863d84`).

**Worked example.** Bathroom, lamp middle setting, `core_01`: the brightest 5 % of the frame have median RGB (222, 186, 96), warmth (222 − 96) / 505 = 0.25. With (w0, w1) = (0.08, 0.20) the share is 1: the blue floor drops from 113 to 40 and the hue low end from 103 to 92, so the blue body (hue 98, S 78) is painted. Home white table: the brightest pixels are (175, 180, 184), warmth −0.02, share 0: the adopted model, unchanged.

**How it was judged** (`experiments/2026-11-25_color/PREREG.md`, `test.py`; leave one *place* out over 8 places, 154 ordinary train frames). Round 1 (whole-frame reading): +22 barrels found (bathroom middle and dim 2 → 18, ottoman warm 0 → 5), but +6 false boxes, one new box on an empty glass frame and the glass mid lamp −0.023: **fails** the written acceptance. The cause: where an orange barrel or yellow clutter is among the brightest things (close-ups, the black ottoman) the "light" reads warm under white light. Round 2 read the light above the horizon gate only (rows 0–49, where no barrel can be): steadier, but it puts the bathroom's floor around 55–65, where the blue body is painted in pieces and the detector loses more than with no change (bathroom middle 4 of 15, 5 false): **fails**. Not adopted. The re-centred constant (orange 2..16, S ≥ 160) and the two together failed as well: the ottoman's orange shift (hue 15) is not predictable from the other places. `results/2026-11-25_color/test/summary.md`.

**What can go wrong.** The warmth reads the brightest things in view; if those are orange barrels or a yellow notepad under white light, it lowers the blue floor where it should not. A floor halfway down paints a washed-out blue body in pieces, and the detector is not monotone in that (`results/2026-11-25_color/sweep.md`).

### v1 — the `rgb_box` model, the front end until 2026-11-09 (config `afd85483`; kept for the record, selectable with `--model results/2026-10-20_v1_test_adaptive/config.json`)

**The idea.** A pixel has to survive a chain of cheap tests to be called barrel. Each test throws out pixels that *cannot* be barrel; the last test decides which color the survivors are.

1. **Horizon gate.** Nothing above row 50 can be a barrel. The camera is 43 mm above the table and a barrel is 24 mm tall, so every barrel standing on the table appears below the horizon line, which for our robot is around row 76 (section 2). Row 50 is a safe margin above that. Those top rows are therefore *guaranteed background*, which the next two tests exploit.
2. **Saturation floor.** Barrels are vivid; tables, walls and glare are grayish. "Saturation" here is simply max(R, G, B) − min(R, G, B), a number from 0 to 255. The floor is not a fixed number: the program takes rows 0–50 (guaranteed background), finds the 99.5th percentile of their saturation (i.e. "how colorful is the most colorful 0.5 % of the background"), adds a margin of 10, and clamps the result to between 20 and 120. Any pixel below that floor is out. On `touch_02` the floor comes out at 48; on `core_01`, 55. A warm-lit room with a yellowish wall gets a higher floor by itself, with nothing changed in the code. This is what "adaptive thresholds, no image correction" means in practice.
3. **Wall model.** From the same background rows, a robust estimate (median and spread, per channel) of the background color, fit on the low-saturation background pixels; any pixel within 2.5 spreads of that color is rejected. Also per image. This catches bluish or warm walls that pass the floor.
4. **Brightness gate.** Any pixel whose brightest channel is below 40 is out: in near-black pixels the color is noise. This changes nothing on a light table; it exists for dark ones.
5. **Color boxes — the only learned part.** For each class, a box in RGB space: the 1st-to-99th percentile range of hand-labeled barrel pixels that survived the gates above, fit once on the 16 training frames. The frozen numbers are orange = R 74–211, G 14–121, B 4–104 and blue = R 0–127, G 11–110, B 36–185. A surviving pixel inside the orange box is orange, inside the blue box is blue; if it is inside both, the redder one (R > B) is orange. Outside both, it is other.

A "table model" built from the bottom rows, mirroring the wall model, exists as a switch but is **off**: a close barrel's shadow sits in those rows and fools it.

**Worked example.** Take a pixel on the shaded lower face of the blue barrel in `touch_02`, say RGB (30, 45, 110). Row ≈ 160, below 50: passes the horizon gate. Saturation 110 − 30 = 80 ≥ 48: passes the floor. Nothing like the gray-blue wall (the wall median is around (120, 125, 135) with a spread of a few units): passes the wall model. Brightest channel 110 ≥ 40: passes. Inside the blue box (R 0–127, G 11–110, B 36–185) and outside the orange box (B 110 > 104): **blue**. Now a slightly darker pixel on the same face, (25, 30, 60): saturation 35 < 48 — thrown out by the floor. That is the main v1 miss, and it is why the detector (section 3) must be able to fill in a bottom edge the mask does not show.

**Why the boxes alone don't work.** Barrel pixels in RGB form a long smear along the brightness axis (bright lit face, dark shaded face), so a box wide enough to include the shadowed faces also includes a lot of table and wall. The floor and the wall model do the separating; the boxes only decide orange vs blue among the survivors.

**Where it lives.** `Config` (every knob; defaults = the frozen v1 config; `horizon_row=50, s_min_mode="band", s_band_q=99.5, s_band_margin=10, s_min_lo=20, s_min_hi=120, v_min=40, bg_prior=True, k_bg=2.5, table_prior=False, box_space="rgb", box_pct=(1, 99), tie="rb_sign"`). `Frame.from_image(bgr)` (the image plus its saturation and brightness planes). `effective_s_min(frame, cfg)` (the per-image floor). `fit(frames, masks, cfg)` → `Model` (learns the two boxes; done once, saved in `results/2026-10-20_v1_test_adaptive/config.json`). `segment(frame, model)` → the mask + an info dict (`s_min_eff` is the floor it used).

**How to run it.** `python barrels/tools/segment_image.py image.png` writes the orange, blue, class-mask and overlay images to `results/segment_image/`.

**What can go wrong.** Shadowed lower faces of blue barrels fall below the saturation floor (the main miss; `far_02`'s middle orange barrel loses its lower face the same way). Skin lands inside the orange box — hands are orange to this program. Under a strong color cast the fixed RGB boxes can miss while the floor and wall model adapt; v2 allows one re-fit of the boxes on more frames if that happens.

**Numbers.** Held-out test: orange IoU 0.84, blue 0.81, zero false pixels on the empty frame (`EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-10-20). (IoU for pixels = correct barrel pixels ÷ (correct + missed + falsely added).)

## 2. Camera geometry — `src/barrels/geometry.py` (v1.5)

**What it does.** Converts between image rows and real-world distances for this robot's camera, and says how big a barrel should look at any place in the image.

**The idea.** The camera is a pinhole at a known height above the table (h = 43.47 mm), tilted down by an angle θ, with a focal length f that says how many pixels one radian of view is (≈ 400 px). A point on the table at horizontal distance d in front of the camera is seen at an angle atan(h / d) below horizontal; subtract the tilt, and the row it lands on is

    row = 240 + f · tan( atan(h / d) − θ )

(240 is the image center row.) Run it backwards and a row gives a distance:

    d = h / tan( atan((row − 240) / f) + θ )

A point that is not on the table but at height z (the top of a barrel) uses h − z instead of h. Two consequences worth holding in your head: rows near the horizon are very far away and a one-row change there is many millimetres, while rows near the bottom of the frame are close and a row is a fraction of a millimetre; and the **horizon row** — where a point infinitely far away lands — is 240 − f · tan θ, which for our numbers is **row 76**. Nothing on the table can appear above it.

**The barrel.** 24 mm tall, 25.5 mm across at the bulge, 18 mm across at the ends, stripe 6 mm tall centred at mid-height. The box's top is the *far* edge of the lid (9 mm beyond the barrel's axis), its bottom is the *near* edge of the base (9 mm in front of the axis), so top and bottom are at slightly different distances. `barrel_rows(d_axis)` applies all of that and returns the top row, the stripe's rows, the bottom row and the width in pixels for a barrel whose axis is d_axis away. With the current camera:

| barrel axis distance | top row | stripe rows | bottom row | width px | height px |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 mm | 212 | 348–390 | 422 | 204 | 210 |
| 100 mm | 154 | 211–235 | 263 | 102 | 109 |
| 150 mm | 131 | 166–183 | 204 | 68 | 73 |
| 200 mm | 118 | 143–156 | 174 | 51 | 55 |
| 300 mm | 105 | 121–130 | 142 | 34 | 37 |
| 400 mm | 98 | 110–116 | 126 | 26 | 28 |

So a barrel at 20 cm is about 51 px wide and 55 px tall with its bottom at row 174; seeing *any one* of those — the top row, the stripe, the width, the bottom — tells you the distance, and from the distance every other number in that row of the table. That is the whole trick the detector uses. The inverses are `bottom_row_from_top_row`, `bottom_row_from_stripe_row`, `bottom_row_from_width`, `expected_barrel_width_px(bottom_row=…)`, `expected_barrel_height_px(bottom_row=…)`.

**Worked example.** In `touch_02` the left orange barrel's top row is 122. Backwards through the formula with z = 24 mm: the lid's far edge is 191 mm from the camera, so the axis is 182 mm away, so the base's near edge is 173 mm away, which lands on row 182.6. The segmentation saw the bottom at row 175: 7.6 px higher than predicted, which is within what we expect from the camera numbers (next paragraph), so the seen bottom is believed.

**Where it lives.** `CameraModel(f, tilt_deg, height_mm)` with `horizon_row()`, `row_to_ground_distance(row)`, `point_row(d, z)`, `barrel_rows(d_axis)`, the inverses above, and `image_to_ground(u, v)` (reproduces the robot's own ground projection; kept for the world map later, not used in v2). Presets: `AIM_4A8B6118_BOXFIT` (the current default, f 400 / tilt 22.25°), `AIM_4A8B6118` (the October measurement, f 404 / tilt 23.3°), `NOMINAL` (Dave's 23° / 400), and the honest range `AIM_4A8B6118_RANGE`.

**How to run it.** `python -m barrels.geometry --tilt 22.25 --f 400` prints the table in `constraints.md` for any camera numbers.

**What can go wrong.** The tilt and focal length are only known to a few percent, and close to the robot small tilt errors move rows a lot (at 5 cm, 1° of tilt is ≈ 7 px). The known-distance `calib` frames (the calibration session, `TODO.md` P1) will measure both properly.

**Update 2026-11-03 — the preset was off.** On the labeled frames the October camera (f 404, tilt 23.3°, horizon row 66) put every predicted bottom edge about 20 px too low: the top row said "this barrel is closer than it is". Re-fitting the tilt from 24 labeled, fully visible barrels on the train frames (f held at 400, the known barrel height and width as the ruler) gives tilt 22.25°, horizon row 76 — inside the band measured in October. With it, predicted bottoms land within about 5 px of the truth. That is the provisional preset `AIM_4A8B6118_BOXFIT`, now the detector's default, until the known-distance `calib` session measures it properly. Note what the boxes actually pin down: the horizon row, not f and tilt separately (they trade off along a ridge: f 356 with tilt 24.75° fits exactly as well as f 400 with 22.25°). `experiments/2026-11-03_geo_boxfit/`, `EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-03.

**Numbers to remember.** Horizon row ≈ 76. A barrel at 20 cm is ≈ 51 px wide. The bottom edge leaves the frame when the barrel's base is within ≈ 28 mm of the bumper; in that zone the top edge moves ≈ 13 px per mm.

## 3. The detector — `src/barrels/detector.py` (v2; collapsed 2026-11-26: ten stages, 18 numbers, no switches)

**Now (2026-11-27).** After the detector, the stripe by contrast runs on the photo and adds the barrels the fixed colors missed (section 3b; "Stage 11" in `DETECTOR-MAP.md`); `detect(mask)` itself is not changed by it. One rule was adopted after the collapse: the second reading of a stripe at its seams (stage 2 below; it changes the boxes of 54 of the 5435 masks of the collapse proof, `DETECTOR-MAP.md` section 5, last part; *branch `wip/adopt-slack`, awaiting Tanush's "understood"*). **2026-11-26.** The detector was collapsed on branch `wip/collapse`: the 30 on/off switches are gone (the ones that were on are plain code, the ones that were off are deleted), and `detect` is a list of ten named stages. It draws exactly the boxes it drew before (`experiments/2026-11-26_collapse/`). **Every check, with its number and the case it exists for, is in `DETECTOR-MAP.md`.** This section gives the idea of each stage and one worked example. The section as it stood before the collapse, with every switch and every experiment's numbers, is `archive/HOW-IT-WORKS-detector-before-collapse-2026-11-26.md`. When each rule was added: `archive/DETECTOR-HISTORY.md`.

**What it does.** Takes the class mask from section 1 and returns a list of barrels. Each barrel is a `Barrel` record (`detect_types.py`): `color` (orange or blue), `bbox` = `(x0, y0, x1, y1)` in pixels, half-open (x1 and y1 are one past the last pixel), `confidence` (0–1), `flags` (`bottom_inferred` / `top_inferred` when that edge was computed rather than seen; `occluded` when the barrel is partly hidden and the full box is drawn anyway; `truncated` when the box reaches a frame edge), `area` and `source` (`"det2"` for a barrel found from its stripe, `"det1"` for a close-up with no stripe in view, `"det3"` for a barrel found from its top behind another). `detect(mask, cfg, cam)` is the one entry point: mask in, barrels out, left to right. `draw(image, barrels)` paints a 1-px rectangle per barrel, yellow for an orange barrel and green for a blue one, and nothing else.

Nothing is remembered between images and nothing is fit per image. `DetConfig` holds 18 numbers and no switch: every rule always runs.

**The one idea.** *What is seen fixes the distance; the distance fixes the full size; the box is the full barrel, placed where the seen part allows; a rule acts only when what is seen could not be anything else.* Every rule has three parts: a **reading** that gives the distance (the stripe's row, a seam, a top row), a **second reading that agrees** (a seen edge where the first predicts it), and a **place** (where the seen part pins a box W wide and H tall). No second reading, or nothing that pins the place: no box. A miss is allowed; a box in the wrong place is not.

### The ruler (`BarrelAt`, `edge_tol`)

Section 2 says: a row gives a distance, and the distance gives every size. `BarrelAt` is that as one record: the distance, the rows (top, stripe top, stripe bottom, bottom), the width W, the height H, the stripe's height. Three readings can give the distance: the stripe's row (`from_stripe_row`), the top row (`from_top_row`), the seam of a half stripe (`from_seam_row`). Each stripe carries the one for its own row.

"A seen edge agrees with the predicted one" always means the same: within 15 % of the height, at least 4 px (`edge_tol`). *A barrel 20 cm away is 55 px tall: 8.3 px. At 40 cm, 28 px tall: 4.2 px.*

### The ten stages (`detect`)

```
pieces            = find_pieces(mask)                    # 1  blobs of barrel color, barrel-shaped. No box yet
stripes, leftover = find_stripes(mask)                   # 2  one color with the other right above and below
found, unplaced, lost = boxes_from_stripes(stripes)      # 3  one box per stripe
found += closeup_pieces(pieces, found)                   # 4  a piece with no stripe: only as a close-up
found  = drop_seen_through(found)                        # 5  verify: not read from a nearer barrel's picture
found += caps_behind(found, lost)                        # 6  a top showing above a found nearer barrel
found += post_pairs(leftover + unplaced, found)          # 7  two thin slices, one barrel apart
found  = half_stripes(found)                             # 8  a stripe with body on one side only
found  = drop_seen_through(found)                        # 9  verify again
found  = drop_single_reading(found)                      # 10 a guessed side needs a seen edge
```

Stripes come first: the first boxes are drawn in stage 3. Later stages only add boxes, except that the verify stages delete and a half stripe may replace a stage-3 box. On the 261 real train frames, 201 of the 239 boxes come from stage 3, 28 from stage 4, 6 are caps, 4 half stripes.

### Stage 1 — pieces (`find_pieces`: `find_blobs`, `split_blob`, `resolve_bottom`)

Take every pixel that is orange *or* blue (both at once, so a barrel's body and its stripe land in the same blob). Close gaps up to 3 px. Find the 8-connected blobs; drop those under 20 px.

*`touch_02`:* the mask has 3409 orange and 2337 blue pixels. They form **two** blobs: one at x 149–237, y 122–178 (88 px wide, 1763 orange / 1884 blue: the orange *and* the blue barrel merged, because the orange barrel's blue stripe touches the blue barrel's body), and one at x 240–288 (48 wide: the right orange barrel on its own).

**How many barrels is a blob?** Its width ÷ the width one barrel has at its bottom row, rounded. Two or more: cut at the *narrowest column* near each equal cut. Barrels bulge in the middle, so two touching barrels have a waist where they meet. *`touch_02`, first blob:* bottom row 178 → one barrel is 53.4 px wide there. 88 ÷ 53.4 = 1.65 → **2** barrels, cut at x = 193: piece A, 1301 orange / 335 blue → **orange**; piece B, 462 orange / 1549 blue → **blue**.

**The bottom edge.** The top row of a piece says how far away the barrel is, and the distance says where its base must be. If the seen bottom agrees with that (edge tolerance), it is kept. If not, the predicted bottom is drawn (`bottom_inferred`). *`touch_02`, piece A:* top row 122 → axis 182 mm away → predicted bottom 182.6. Seen bottom 175. Tolerance 9.1 px, difference 7.6 → **seen bottom kept**. *`far_02`, the middle orange barrel:* predicted bottom 144.4, seen 136 (the shaded lower face was not painted), tolerance 5.8 → **bottom replaced by 144**.

Then four checks: on the table (one barrel is at least 16 px wide at its bottom row; the hand at the horizon in `far_02` fails this), its top below the horizon, barrel-wide (0.4 to 1.6 ×), not far too short (at least 0.5 × the height for its bottom row; this refuses only a short piece with its top in the lowest rows of the frame, where a real barrel would run out of the frame bottom).

**The horizon check** is new as a check of its own (2026-11-26). A barrel is 24 mm tall and the camera is 43 mm up, so the camera looks down on every lid: the top of a barrel on the table is always below the horizon (row 76). *Campus `core_01`:* a blue jacket above the table edge, 24 px wide and 45 tall, with its bottom just under the horizon, so its top is above it. Before, the height check refused it (twice as tall as a barrel at its bottom row). The height check was doing the horizon's job by accident: without it, 107 false boxes appear on 36 real frame-runs. Now the fact is asked directly. And the height check has no upper limit any more: once the horizon is asked first, no piece is too tall for its bottom row (at most 1.1 ×; `DETECTOR-MAP.md`, section 5, B13).

No box is drawn in stage 1. A barrel with a stripe gets its box in stage 3; a piece is used only in stage 4.

### Stage 2 — stripes (`find_stripes`, `sandwiches`)

A stripe is a patch of the *other* color with body color right above **and** right below it, pixel by pixel, in its own column, within 3 stripe heights. Only the stripe's own pixels are measured, so shadow around it changes nothing, and a dark gap with no stripe-colored pixel is not a stripe.

*`touch_02`:* three stripes: blue between orange at x 152–191, orange between blue at 189–235, blue between orange at 241–288. Three barrels, and no width rule was needed to separate them.

Then, per stripe: big enough (20 px), on the table, as tall as a stripe (0.45 to 1.6 × the expected stripe height), not a speck sideways, not wider than one barrel (else it is cut). Three things repair a damaged stripe:

- **A stripe in pieces is one stripe** (`_join_pieces`). A highlight cuts a stripe in two. Pieces at the same rows that together fit in one barrel's width are joined, when each is stripe-shaped or when body color bridges the gap. Two barrels cannot both have their stripe inside one barrel's width at the same rows.
- **A stripe too thin or too small is read a second time, at the seams, when the barrel is seen whole** (`read_at_seams`, `rest_of_barrel_agrees`; adopted 2026-11-27 from the experiment `2026-11-26_slack`, rule D; *awaiting Tanush's "understood"*). Mixed pixels at the two seams belong to neither color. So the stripe loses rows at its two edges and the body loses the same rows next to it. With h the stripe's own height and g the gap between the body above and the body below, each color lost e = (g − h) ÷ 4 pixels at each seam (at most 3). Given back, the stripe is h + 2e tall: the mean of h and g. This is asked only when the barrel is seen whole. A stripe with 20 px or more may show it by its width, with the eaten pixels given back (at least 0.8 W), or by the rest of the barrel. A stripe under 20 px may show it only by the rest of the barrel. "The rest of the barrel agrees" means three things: the body above and below ends where the stripe's row puts the top and the bottom (the edge tolerance); neither end lies against the other barrel color; and on at least one side the body and the stripe end together, so that side is the barrel's own edge. Then the seam height must be a stripe's height (0.45 to 1.6), and a small stripe's pixels, scaled by seam height ÷ own height, must reach 20. From then on the stripe is the band from body to body, and the verify step asks a small one for its own pixel count, not for 20.
  *Worked example, a barrel 50 cm away, two pixels eaten off every color* (the test `test_a_thin_stripe_is_read_at_the_seams_when_the_rest_of_the_barrel_agrees`). The barrel is 20.5 px wide and its stripe is expected 5.5 px tall. What is left of the stripe is 2 rows tall (the floor is 0.45 × 5.5 = 2.5) and 16 px wide, just under 0.8 × 20.5 = 16.4: before, it was refused as too thin, and the old second reading (0.8 W by its own columns) was not asked. The gap between the bodies is 10 rows, so e = (10 − 2) ÷ 4 = 2 and the seam height is (2 + 10) ÷ 2 = 6: a stripe's height. Seen whole: 16 + 2 × 2 = 20 px wide, and the rest of the barrel agrees as well. One box, (310, 90, 330, 108), on the barrel (310, 88, 330, 110).
  *Real frame where only the rest of the barrel can say it, `4A8B_lampmid6_ottoman_core_01`* (fixed colors): a blue barrel at (77, 133, 155, 208) was missed. Its orange stripe is 39 px wide of a barrel 72 px wide (0.54), and 5 rows tall where 18 are expected (the floor is 8.1). The gap between its bodies is 23 rows: e = (23 − 5) ÷ 4 = 4.5, capped at 3, and the seam height is (5 + 23) ÷ 2 = 14: a stripe's height. The width, even with 2 × 3 px given back, is 45 px, under 0.8 × 72 = 58. But the body above and below ends where the stripe's row puts the top and the bottom, and on one side body and stripe end together. Read at the seams it passes; the barrel gets the box (78, 136, 150, 203), a strict hit.
  *Real frame, `4A8B_lampcool6_bathtile_clutter_01`:* an orange barrel at (410, 108, 442, 141), missed before. Its blue stripe is 3 rows tall where 9 are expected (floor 4.0) and 26 px wide of a barrel 34 px wide (0.8 W = 27.3). Gap 8 rows: e = 1.25, seam height 5.5. Seen whole by its width with 2.5 px given back (28.5 px), and by the rest of the barrel. Box (411, 110, 441, 141), a strict hit.
  *Why a small stripe needs the rest of the barrel:* with the width alone allowed for it, four boxes appeared on nothing on the train frames with hand-set colors (bathroom tile under the mid and dim lamps, an empty bathroom frame, the warm ottoman). *Why one side must be the barrel's own:* in a campus clip a blue sliver sat between two fingers, skin above and below. The skin runs on past both ends of the sliver, so neither side is a barrel's edge: not read again.
- **A stripe wider than a barrel is two barrels.** Over 1.4 W, or over 1.2 × the width of a barrel as tall as the body seen above and below it.

A piece narrower than a stripe is tall is **thin**. It may still be a barrel (three quarters hidden), but only as a slice seen from top to bottom. Thin pieces and specks are also kept as **left-over pieces** for stage 7.

### Stage 3 — one box per stripe (`boxes_from_stripes`, `box_from_stripe`)

The stripe's middle row gives the distance, the distance the full box.

**Top and bottom** (`read_edges`). The body is walked up and down from the stripe. A seen end that agrees with the prediction is the edge; else the predicted edge is drawn and flagged. An edge is seen only against something that is not a barrel: a candidate with neither edge where its row predicts it, whose top ends against the other barrel color, is not a barrel.

**Left and right** (`place_sides`).

- *The stripe is at least 0.8 W wide:* the box is as wide as the body joined to the stripe, at most 1.2 W. The stripe's outermost pixels curve away and go dark, so the stripe alone runs about 15 % narrow (campus `core_02`).
- *Narrower: the partly seen branch* (`partly_seen`). In this order: (1) the body joined to the stripe shows the whole width → that is the box. (2) Exactly one side has a **reason** to be hidden (the frame edge, a found barrel standing nearer, or the body and the stripe ending apart there) → the box is W wide from the seen end of the other side. (3) Exactly one end of what is seen has a barrel's rounded corners (`own_end_by_outline`) → W wide from that end. (4) The stripe is at least 0.4 W wide → centred on what is seen, a guess. Else no box.
- *Thin* (`thin_slice`): the body shows the whole width; or the slice touches the frame on one side; or the outline knows its own end. Else no box.

*Campus `occl_02`, the live miss with Dave:* a blue barrel behind the robot. Its stripe is 16 px wide where a barrel is 53.6 px wide: 0.3 W. The robot's edge slants, so the blue body below the stripe shows almost the whole width, and the box is placed by what is seen: overlap with the hand box 0.82.

**The box must hold body color** (`holds_body`). *Campus `core_01`:* a blue barrel's body, lying between its own orange stripe below and an orange neighbour above, is "a blue stripe between orange". In its columns there is more blue than orange outside the stripe rows: not an orange barrel.

**Duplicates.** Two boxes of one color that overlap by more than half are one barrel; the surer stays.

### Stage 4 — close-ups (`closeup_pieces`)

A stage-1 piece that no box covers is kept only when a stripe could not be seen: its box reaches the frame bottom, or its top row puts the stripe's bottom edge below the frame.

- *`4A8B_overheaddim_palewood_close_01`:* top row 241 → stripe rows 434–483, base 508. The frame cuts the stripe, so no body shows below it and stage 2 cannot see it. Box (292, 241, 568, 477) against the label (291, 243, 566, 480).
- **A close-up that the frame's side also cuts** shows neither its width nor its stripe. It must be seen from its top row down to where that row puts the stripe. *The printed fruit on the empty frame `occlx_15`:* top row 286, it must be seen down to row 451, its pixels end at 368 → no box.

### Stages 5, 9 and 10 — verify (`drop_seen_through`, `drop_single_reading`)

Two facts are checked on the boxes, whatever rule drew them.

- **A barrel cannot be seen through a nearer barrel.** The boxes are taken nearest first. Each box knows what it was read from (its evidence: its stripe, its cap piece, its band). Evidence pixels that lie inside a nearer kept barrel's outline and have that barrel's own colors there are the nearer barrel's. Fewer than 20 own pixels: dropped (a stripe admitted at the seams with fewer than 20 pixels: fewer than its own pixel count). *ABP white `occlx_17`:* the far blue barrel (144, 117, 185, 160) has orange clutter right above it. Its blue upper body between that clutter and its own orange stripe was read as the blue stripe of a smaller orange barrel (161, 105, 196, 143). That "stripe" lies wholly inside the blue barrel: dropped. It runs before the caps (stage 5) and once more at the end (stage 9).
- **A guessed side needs a second reading.** A partly seen stripe's box with its top **and** bottom both predicted, whose seen span is under 0.8 W, rests on the stripe's row alone: dropped. *Glass `lampmid3_glass_neg_02`:* a tin with an orange lid and a blue label; a piece of the label (560, 144, 580, 155) made a box from one side → dropped.

### Stage 6 — caps (`caps_behind`)

A barrel behind one of the other color shows only its top; its stripe melts into the front barrel's body. A piece of one color that is barrel-wide and as tall as an upper body for its **top row**, with a found nearer barrel hiding the rest, is a barrel.

*`occl_01` (home mask):* the blue cap is x 386–430, rows 115–135. Top row 115 → W 46.7, H 50.7, upper body 22.6. It is 44 wide (0.94 W, the whole width) and 20 tall: a cap. A barrel 44 wide is 47.8 tall → bottom 163; blue is seen in those columns ending at row 160 → bottom 160. Box (386, 115, 430, 160); the hand-drawn one is (386, 115, 430, 161). A narrower cap needs exactly one hidden side (*`cluster_01`:* 37 wide where W is 49.3; the found blue barrel is on its right → widened right).

### Stage 7 — pairs (`post_pairs`); stage 8 — half stripes (`half_stripes`)

- **Pairs.** Two thin slices at the same rows, each seen from top to bottom, outer edges one barrel apart: one barrel behind a post. Simulated scenes only; no real train frame forms a pair.
- **Half stripes.** A stripe with body on one side only, seen in its whole width, is a barrel hidden from above or from below when the body's far edge is where the seam between stripe and body predicts it. The seam is 9 mm or 15 mm above the table, so it gives the distance.

### The reason log

`detect(mask, log=[])` appends one note per candidate: the stage, the reason it was refused (or "kept"), its box and the numbers it was compared with. With no list given, nothing is recorded. The log is diagnostics only: nothing in the detector reads it, and the boxes are the same with or without it (tested). The reason names are the words in quotes in `DETECTOR-MAP.md`.

### The numbers (`DetConfig`)

18 numbers, no switches. The table with every number, its value, its meaning and where it is used is `DETECTOR-MAP.md`, section 4. `report.py --cand-det name=value` runs the detector with one number changed; naming a removed switch stops with a message.

## 3b. The stripe by contrast — `src/barrels/contrast.py` (adopted 2026-11-27; experiment `2026-11-26_contrast`)

**What it does.** Finds the barrels the fixed colors of section 1 missed, and adds their boxes to the detector's. It runs after section 3, on the picture itself, inside `pipeline.detect_frame` (below). It never moves or drops a box of section 3. In `DETECTOR-MAP.md` it is "Stage 11", with every check and number.

**Why.** The fixed colors ask of every pixel "is this the home orange, is this the home blue?". Under the bathroom's mid lamp the blue stripe of an orange barrel is still bluer than the body, but no longer the home blue; the detector needs both colors, so it sees nothing. Fixed colors: 1 of 15 placements on that floor under the mid lamp, 3.3 of 15 on the ottoman, 11 of 16 on the gray table.

**The idea.** Ask a question that the light mostly cancels out of: *is this band bluer (or redder) than what is right above it AND right below it, by a margin, at the size its row predicts?*

1. **The channel.** Per pixel, red against blue without brightness: `log((R + 8) / (B + 8))`. Gray is 0, orange about +1.8, the home blue about −1. A warm lamp multiplies R and B by different amounts, which adds a constant to this number everywhere; the difference between two bands removes the constant. A pixel darker than 25, or with a channel at 250 or more (glare), is left out.
2. **What the row predicts** (`row_bands`). For every image row taken as a stripe's middle, the camera model (section 2) gives the barrel that would stand there: its top, its stripe rows, its bottom, its width. Three bands: the middle 0.6 of the stripe rows; the body above and the body below, each starting a quarter of a stripe height out and 0.9 stripe heights tall, never past the barrel's own top and bottom. Rows above 50, stripes thinner than 4 px and barrels narrower than 16 px are not searched. This is pure geometry, computed once per camera.
3. **The sandwich** (`band_means`, `sandwich`). In each column, the mean of the channel over each band. The score is the smaller of (above − stripe) and (below − stripe) when both have the same sign, else 0. Positive: a band bluer than both neighbours, an orange barrel's blue stripe. Negative: redder, a blue barrel's orange stripe.
4. **Candidates** (`find`). In a window 0.8 of the predicted width, at least half the columns must have a score past 0.5, and the mean score over the window (the *strength*) must be at least 1.15. The strongest first, at most 20 a frame. Each is moved to the middle of its stripe (the score is flat over a few rows, and the first of them is not the middle; touching barrels of one color share one long stripe, cut into barrel-wide slots). A weaker candidate inside a stronger one's predicted box is dropped: a barrel's own body bands are sandwiches of the other sign (lid or table, body, stripe).
5. **Already found?** A candidate whose stripe point lies in a box of section 3 is left alone.
6. **Its own colors, and two checks** (`measure`). The body color is read from the two body bands, the stripe color from the stripe band (middle 60 % of the width), as (log R/G, log B/G, half the log brightness); `d` is how far apart they are. *The absolute check:* the part that should be orange (an orange barrel's body, a blue barrel's stripe) must read at least 0.8 in the channel — a blue tin's "orange body" does not. *The extent check:* what is just above the predicted top and just below the predicted bottom must differ from the body by at least 0.15 d — a band of color that goes on past where the barrel should end is not a barrel.
7. **The paint** (`paint`). Inside the predicted box grown by 8 %, and only where the fixed colors painted nothing, a pixel becomes body when this barrel's body color is the nearest of (body, stripe, the things around the box that are clearly something else) and nearer than 0.6 d; stripe likewise, near the stripe's rows only.
8. **The boxes** (`add_boxes`). If anything was painted, the detector of section 3 runs again on the painted mask, with the same `DetConfig`. Each of its boxes that is on no box of the first run (half of the smaller box inside the other) is added, with source `"contrast"`. The rest are dropped.

**Worked example — `4A8B_lampmid6_bathtile_core_01`** (train; the bathroom floor, mid lamp; two orange and two blue barrels). The fixed colors give the detector nothing to box: no box. The contrast stage finds four candidates:

| candidate | stripe point (row, column) | strength | orange part | above / below the box, × d | painted px | box added | hand box |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| orange | 142, 246 | 2.07 | 1.81 | 0.39 / 0.44 | 377 | (218, 112, 271, 168) | (223, 115, 270, 165) |
| blue | 188, 416 | 1.81 | 1.66 | 0.74 / 0.67 | 4206 | (379, 140, 450, 225) | (374, 140, 457, 226) |
| blue | 129, 493 | 1.75 | 1.74 | 0.61 / 0.62 | 769 | (477, 111, 512, 146) | (475, 110, 513, 147) |

**Where it runs: `pipeline.detect_frame(frame, model, cam, det_cfg)`.** The one photo-in, boxes-out function: `segment` (section 1) → `detector.detect` (section 3) → `contrast.add_boxes`. The photo tool, the live hook, `report.py` (and so `--gate`) and `judge.py` all call it, so they cannot drift apart. `detector.detect(mask)` itself is unchanged. `contrast=False` stops after section 3: the default until 2026-11-27, kept only for comparisons (`judge.py --base-previous`, `report.py --cand-no-contrast / --base-no-contrast`).

**The numbers.** 23, fixed, in `ContrastConfig` (`DETECTOR-MAP.md` section 4.4). They are the setting the experiment's fold rule picked on all eight places; judged leave one place out (each place's numbers chosen on the other seven) it found 133.8 → 155.3 of 172 placements, 64 barrels gained, none lost. As adopted, on today's detector, in-sample: 135.4 → 156.6 placements (214 → 277 per frame), 63 gained, 0 lost, boxes not on their barrel 1.93 → 2.43, no box on an empty frame. The stage costs about 30 ms a frame (median 43 → 76 ms, one thread); the second detector run happens on 30 of the 154 ordinary frames.

**Why add and not merge.** Painting every candidate into the fixed mask and taking the detector's whole answer on that mask failed its judge: second boxes on barrels already found, and boxes on a jar. Adding keeps every box of section 3 as it is.

**What it cannot do.** A barrel with no visible stripe (close-ups whose stripe is below the frame, tipped barrels). Very dark scenes: on the black ottoman the pixels are under 25 and nothing is read. A thing that really is orange with a blue band at barrel size: the jar with an orange lid and a blue label on the glass table under the warm lamp gets two boxes. A barrel inside a wrong box of section 3 is left alone.

## 4. Detection eval — `src/barrels/eval_det.py`

**What it does.** Scores predicted boxes against the hand-drawn boxes: for each image, which predictions match which true barrels, and from that precision, recall and F1 per color, plus how far off the bottom edges are.

**The idea.** A prediction and a true box *match* only if they have the same color and overlap enough — intersection over union ≥ 0.5, i.e. the overlapping area is at least half the area the two boxes cover together. Matching is greedy: the most confident prediction takes the best-overlapping free truth box, then the next, so each true barrel is used at most once. What is left over is either a *false* box (a prediction with no truth) or a *miss* (a truth with no prediction). On frames with no barrels every box is false. Per color: precision = matches ÷ boxes drawn, recall = matches ÷ true barrels, F1 = their harmonic mean (2PR ÷ (P + R)), and the headline number is the mean of the orange and blue F1. That is the **v1 rule**, which also ignores true barrels flagged occluded / truncated / top-only (they count neither as hits nor as misses). The **v2 rule** (`EVAL.md`, in the code since 2026-11-03 evening, `rule="v2"`) is stricter in two ways: a match also needs the predicted bottom edge within max(3 px, a tenth of the true box height), and **every** true barrel counts — occluded, truncated, held, all of them; the flags only split the error reports (bottom error for seen vs `bottom_estimated` boxes). Matching under v2 takes the highest-overlap eligible pair first. A false box on an empty frame marked `clean` (nothing orange or blue in it) disqualifies the configuration outright. Scores are also broken down per scene tag.

**Where it lives.** `evaluate_frame(name, preds, gts, cam, rule="v1"|"v2", clean=…)` → per-frame counts and the list of matches with their IoU and bottom-row error; `bottom_tol_px` / `bottom_within_tol` (the v2 bottom condition); `match_greedy` (v1) and `match_best_first` (v2); `aggregate` pools frames per condition, per scene and overall; `write_results` writes `summary.md`, `summary.csv`, `per_image.csv` (counts per frame) and `per_barrel.csv` (every match, miss and false box with its IoU, bottom error and `width_ratio` = predicted width / true width, added 2026-11-09 because the IoU match passes a box 15 % too thin); `score` reduces a run to one dict for sweeps (primary = mean F1; `disqualified` when a clean empty frame got a box). CLI: `tools/eval_boxes.py --pred DIR --split train --gt-boxes --rule v2`.

**What can go wrong.** The true bottom edge of a hidden base is itself a human estimate, so bottom-edge error is reported separately for `bottom_estimated` boxes (`per_barrel.csv`). Five test frames hold about ten barrels, so the test number will be coarse: one miss moves recall by 10 %.

### The one table — `src/barrels/report.py`, `tools/report.py` (2026-11-11)

**What it does.** Prints one table that shows what is better where: rows are data set × scenario, columns are the same five numbers for a *candidate* and a *baseline* next to each other, then candidate − baseline. It changes nothing about how a box is judged — every count comes from `evaluate_frame(rule="v2")` and the headline from `score`, as above. Before it there were three sets, three experiment scripts and a different table from each.

**The idea, in the order the data flows.**

1. *Which frames* (`labeled_stems`, `select_frames`). Every file `data/labels/<stem>_boxes.json` names a frame with truth boxes. The stem `{robot}_{light}_{surface}_{scene}_{nn}` is cut in two: the first three fields are the **data set** (`4A8B_window_light` is shown as HOME, `4A8B_campus_light` as CAMPUS, Dave's as `DAVE*`; any other group, e.g. `4A8B_lampwarm7_wood`, shows up under its own name with no code change), the fourth is the **scenario**. Kept: frames in the train split, and Dave's (they are in no split; marked `*` = indicative, because his boxes group touching barrels). Left out: the 15 test frames, `calib` / `ruler` / `horizon` frames, and labeled frames not yet assigned to a split (they might still become test frames; the footer lists them).
2. *The test-frame guard* (`refuse_test_frames`). `run_arm` calls it before any image is opened and it raises `TestFrameError` if a test frame is in the list. The only way past is `--split test` on the command line, for a planned look.
3. *An arm* (`Arm`, `parse_det_overrides`, `run_arm`). An arm is a color-model file (a results `config.json`) plus the `DetConfig` fields changed from the default, written `field=value`. For every frame: segment with the model, run `detector.detect`, score against the box label (`score_frame`). **Home is special:** the color model's four numbers were read off the home frames, so scoring them with that same model would be marking its own homework. As in the experiment that produced the published 0.93, each home scenario is segmented by a model re-fit (same `Config`, `pipeline.fit`) on the *other* home scenarios — leave-one-scene-out. Every other set never taught the model anything, so the model file is used as it is. `--home-protocol fixed` turns the re-fit off; the footer then says the number is not the published one.
4. *One cell* (`summarize`). A list of scored frames becomes: found = true barrels with a right box; missed = without; false = boxes matching no barrel; width = the median of box width ÷ true width over the found barrels; bot = the median of |predicted bottom row − true bottom row| over the found barrels; and, for a whole set, the score (mean of the orange and blue F1), the false boxes on empty frames and the disqualifier — the last three taken from `eval_det.score` unchanged.
5. *The rows* (`build_rows`, `delta`). Per data set: one row per scenario, then the set's subtotal. `delta` is candidate minus baseline, column by column; a dot means no change, a dash means there is no number (no barrel found on one side). The last row, ALL, adds up the counts of the sets that are not indicative and has no score, because no agreed headline pools different sets.
6. *Out* (`format_text`, `format_markdown`, `write_csv`, `to_json`, `write_all`). The same rows as fixed-width text (≈ 140 columns), and with `--out` as `report.md`, `report.csv`, `report.json` in `results/<date>_<name>/`.

**How to run.** `python barrels/tools/report.py` = today (hs `eb863d84` + `DetConfig()`) against the v1 front end with the same detector. To test a detector change behind a switch against today: `--cand-det <field>=<value> --base-model default`. To test a new color model: `--cand-model results/<x>/config.json --base-model default`. `--sets CAMPUS` limits the rows. A full `python -m pytest` run prints the default table at the end (`tests/conftest.py`, ≈ 15 s; `BARRELS_NO_REPORT=1` skips it; it is skipped when only some tests are run).

**Numbers it reproduces** (2026-11-11, `experiments/2026-11-11_eval_report/`): HOME 0.93 for both front ends, CAMPUS 0.99, DAVE\* 0.47, no box on any empty frame. One thing to know: the default baseline (v1 + *today's* detector) scores 0.39 on campus; the published "v1 0.41" is v1 + the *2026-11-05* detector, which the experiment script prints as a second table (`--base-det stripe_mode=bands,body_min=0,height_max=0,body_width=0,cover_rows=false`; those switches exist only at the git tag `pre-collapse-2026-11-26`). Both were already in `EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-08.

**What can go wrong.** A scenario row is one or two frames, so one barrel moves it a lot — read the rows for *where*, the subtotals for *how much*. The width and bottom medians are over found barrels only, so an arm that finds only the easy barrels can look more exact. A new session appears only once its frames have box labels *and* a split assignment.

### The vendor next to ours — `report.py` (`vendor_arm`, `vendor_coverage`, `loose_counts`), `tools/report.py --vendor` (2026-11-18, branch `wip/eval-vendor-gate`; awaiting walkthrough; the default table prints exactly what it printed before)

**What it does.** The week's goal is a detector at least as good as the robot's own ("aivision", the vendor). Since 2026-11-18 `tm save` stores, with every frame, what the vendor detector reported at that instant (`data/vendor/<stem>_vendor.json`, `DATA.md` "Vendor detections"). `python barrels/tools/report.py --vendor` puts the vendor in the one table as the baseline arm, so the delta block reads "ours minus the vendor". Nothing about a hit changes: the vendor's boxes go through the same `evaluate_frame(rule="v2")` against the same hand-drawn labels.

**The idea, in the order the data flows.**

1. *Which frames.* As before: labeled train frames grouped into data sets; test frames refused. The refusal (`refuse_test_frames`) runs before any vendor file is read, as it runs before any image is opened.
2. *Which of those the vendor was measured on* (`vendor_coverage`). Each selected frame's vendor file is read once. The file is there and says `available: true` → **measured**, also when its object list is empty: the vendor looked and saw nothing, which is an answer, and every barrel in that frame is a miss for it. No file (frames shot before 2026-11-18), `available: false` (the robot's status could not be read at that moment) or a file that cannot be read → the frame is **left out for both arms** and counted in the footer. `available: false` means "not measured"; it is never scored as "the vendor saw nothing". A fair comparison uses the same frames on both sides, so the rows of a vendor comparison hold the measured frames only, a set with none disappears from the table, and the footer says how many frames of each set have a file ("HOME 0 of 16, …").
3. *The vendor's boxes* (`io.vendor_barrels`). Objects named `OrangeBarrel` / `BlueBarrel` become boxes in our 640×480 pixels (the vendor reports 320×240 units; times 2, done when the file was written). Balls, robots and tags are ignored and counted in the footer; a barrel object with no usable box is ignored and counted too.
4. *Scoring* (`score_boxes`, used by both arms). Boxes plus the frame's label → `evaluate_frame(rule="v2")`. A vendor box on an empty table is a false box, and on a *clean* empty table it disqualifies, exactly as for us. Our arm on these frames is what it always is; a home frame is still segmented by the model re-fit without its scene, and that re-fit learns from every other home scene whether or not those have a vendor file (`run_arm(..., only=)`), so a frame's score does not depend on which frames the vendor was measured on.
5. *The two looser counts* (`loose_counts`), printed as a small second table under the footer, for the vendor and for us alike. The v2 rule wants IoU ≥ 0.5 **and** the bottom edge within a tenth of the height. Our labels are the whole barrel, lid to base, drawn through occluders; the vendor may draw tighter, or only what it sees. Then the strict rule punishes it for how it draws, not for what it finds. So next to *strict* (the v2 rule): **overlap** = same color and IoU ≥ 0.5, the bottom edge not checked; **place** = same color and the two box centers at most half the true box's width apart (for a barrel 50 px wide: within 25 px), nearest pair first. The place count does not depend on how tight a box is; it only asks "is there a box of the right color on this barrel". Each barrel and each box is used once; `false` = the boxes left over under that count. Worked example (the unit test): the true box is 50 × 60. A vendor box 30 × 40 around the same center has IoU 0.4: strict miss, overlap miss, place found. A vendor box over the top two thirds has IoU 0.67 but its bottom edge is 20 px high (the tolerance is 6): strict miss, overlap found, place found. **These counts are reporting only**: found / missed / false and the score in the table stay the v2 rule.

**How to run.** `python barrels/tools/report.py --vendor` (= `--base-model vendor`): today's detector against the vendor. `--cand-det …` / `--cand-model …` as always, to compare a candidate with the vendor. `--cand-model vendor --base-model default` turns the two around. `--loose` prints the looser counts for any two arms without the vendor (today: home 27 strict / 27 overlap / 27 place of 29, campus 34 / 34 / 34 of 35). `--out` writes them too: `report.json` gets a `loose` dict in every cell and a `vendor` block (frames per set, what was left out, what was ignored), `report.csv` eight more columns, `report.md` a second table. Without `--vendor` or `--loose` every output is byte-for-byte what it was.

**What can go wrong.** Until 2026-11-18 everything was tested on files the tests write themselves. **Checked on the real files 2026-11-23** (`experiments/2026-11-24_vendor/run.py`, 60 train frames, 106 vendor boxes paired with hand boxes; no code changed): the conversion is right (the hand box centers against the vendor's own 320×240 centers fit a line of slope 1.99 across and 2.03 down, offset about 1 px; reading the corner as a center would give a median IoU of 0.12 instead of 0.81); on free-standing barrels the vendor's box is a little larger than the hand box (median IoU 0.82, width 1.07 ×, height 1.09 ×, top about 6 px higher, bottom 2 px lower), and on hidden barrels it boxes only the part it sees (narrower, or its bottom 8–16 px high), which is where the strict rule is harsh on it; no file came back `available: false`. Not seen yet: whether its list lags a moving barrel (every frame was still). The place count passes a box of the right color sitting on the barrel even when it is a quarter of the barrel's size, and for a barrel cut by the frame edge the true width is the clipped one, so the tolerance shrinks. With today's data the command prints "no frame to compare" and the per-set "0 of N" line, not a table of zeros.

### Counting per placement, and a misplaced box once — `src/barrels/counting.py`, `report.py` (`place_tables`), `tools/report.py --places` (2026-11-26, branch `wip/measure`; awaiting walkthrough; the table above prints exactly what it printed before)

**What it does.** Two things about how results were counted were wrong, and this fixes both without touching the rule for a hit. (1) One arrangement of barrels shot under five lamp settings counted as five times as many barrels. (2) A box that sits on a real barrel of the right color but fails the rule, usually by its bottom edge, was charged twice: the barrel was a miss and the box was a false box. The v2 rule is unchanged and is called **strict** from here on.

**The idea, in the order the data flows.**

1. *One frame* (`classify_frame`). Input: the boxes drawn on a frame and its true boxes. First the strict hits are taken, by the same call the eval uses (`match_best_first` with the bottom-edge test), so "found" is exactly the old number. Then, among the boxes and barrels left over: a box of the same color that is **on** a barrel takes it, highest overlap first, one box per barrel. That barrel is **found, box off**; before, it was a miss, and its box a false box. Every box still left is looked at once more: if it is on some barrel, it is a **second box** (that barrel has the box's color and already has a box) or a **wrong color**; if it is on no barrel, it is **on nothing**. Barrels still left are **missed**. Output: a `FrameCount`, one state per true barrel and one per box.
2. *On a barrel* (`is_on`, `on_share`). At least half of the smaller of the two boxes lies inside the other: intersection area ÷ the smaller area ≥ 0.5. A box around the visible half of a hidden barrel is on it (the small box lies inside the big one). So is a box a little too large, or 10 px too low. A box beside the barrel is not. Any pair with IoU ≥ 0.5 passes, so strict hit, on the barrel, and not on it are nested.
   *Worked example, real:* the glass table, `occl_02`, cool lamp. The true box is 46 × 49 at (254, 114)–(300, 163). The vendor's box is (246, 102)–(308, 172). It holds the whole true box and the overlap is 0.52, but its bottom edge is 9 px low and the tolerance is 4.9 px. Strict: one miss and one false box. Second accounting: found, box off; no false box.
3. *Which barrel is which* (`build_placements`). A **place** is a table in a room (`data/conditions.json`). An **arrangement** is one scene at one place: the frames `{scene}_{nn}` of that place, one per light. A **placement** is one barrel in one arrangement. The frames are grouped by arrangement. The barrels of the first frame are numbered as they come; in every other light each box takes the number of the same-colored box it overlaps most in that first frame (the boxes were copied across lights, so the overlap is near 1). If two lights of one arrangement do not hold the same barrels, it raises `ArrangementMismatch` and names the two frames: a label is wrong, and no count that leans on them would be right.
4. *The tally* (`tally`). A group of frames becomes one `Tally`. The raw counts are sums over frames, as before. The weighted counts take every placement once: a placement that is in k of the frames and found in j of them adds j / k to `found_w`. A box that is not on its barrel adds 1 / k, where k is the number of these frames that show its arrangement. *Worked example:* the glass table has 15 placements under 5 lights, 75 barrels per frame. We find 74 of the 75. Per placement that is 14.8 of 15: one barrel is missed under one of its five lights, and counts 0.8.
5. *The rows* (`group_stems`, `report.place_tables`, `format_places`). One row per place over the ordinary conditions, with lights, frames, barrels and placements, and a total. Then one row per condition (kept; one light each, so per placement = per frame there). Extreme and unlisted conditions are in the by-condition table only, marked.
6. *The vendor where it was not measured* (`report.assumed_vendor_stems`, `counting.assumed_perfect`). The home and campus frames have no vendor file. Tanush's decision of 2026-11-26: on a white table under window or overhead light the vendor counts as finding every barrel. Those conditions carry `vendor_assumed` in `data/conditions.json`. In a vendor comparison they get rows of their own, marked ASSUMED, every assumed number starred, the vendor's box columns empty. The measured total never holds them; a second total that does says ASSUMED in its name.

**How to run.** `python barrels/tools/report.py --places` adds the by-place view under the one table for any two arms. `--vendor` always prints it. `--out` then also writes `places.md` and `places.json`. The headline table of 2026-11-26 with its checks: `python barrels/experiments/2026-11-26_measure/run.py` → `results/2026-11-26_measure/summary.md`.

**The numbers** (train frames, 2026-11-26). The new ordinary conditions with vendor files are 243 barrels per frame: 108 placements in 47 arrangements at 6 places. Per placement: ours 70.8 found and 1.2 with the box off; the vendor 83.3 and 8.2. Per frame, the old way: 148 and 177 of 243. The vendor's 30 "false boxes" are 23 boxes on a barrel that fail the strict rule and 7 on nothing, all 7 on empty frames; ours are 2 and 5. By place, ours against the vendor: ABP gray 10.0 against 16.0 of 16; bathroom floor 4.0 against 7.0 of 15; glass 14.8 against 12.0 of 15 (the vendor has 2.8 more with the box off); ottoman 3.0 against 12.3 of 15; pale wood 13.0 against 12.0 of 15; ABP white 26.0 against 24.0 of 32. With the two assumed white tables: ours 133.8 of 172, the vendor 147.3 of 172, 64 of them assumed.

**What can go wrong.** A small box that lies inside a barrel's box counts as on it wherever it sits; only one box per barrel gets that credit. The share of lights treats lights as equal, but a table shot under four lamp settings and never by daylight says nothing about daylight. The assumed rows are an assumption: the only white table with vendor files, the ABP occlusion set, has the vendor at 24 of 32. A place is still one table: 15 placements move by 0.07 of the place per barrel.

### The noise floor — `experiments/2026-11-26_noise_floor/run.py` → `results/baseline/margins.json` (2026-11-26, same branch)

**What it does.** Measures how much the counts move under a change that means nothing, so that the two checks below can tell a real difference from one hue unit.

**The idea.** The fixed color model has seven numbers: two hue edges and a saturation floor per color, and the brightness floor. Set A moves one of them one step (one hue unit, three saturation units, two brightness units): 14 neighbours. Set B moves several at once: 12 seeded draws. Each neighbour is run on every train frame with today's detector, and compared with the fixed model at four levels: one condition, one place, one protected set, all ordinary conditions together.

**The numbers.** At one condition most neighbours change nothing; the worst case is 6 barrels. At one place: median 0, 90th percentile 0.33 placements for one step, worst case 6 (the ABP gray table: its orange sits on the hue edge, so orange hue low 3 → 4 costs 6 of 16 and 3 → 2 gains 4). Over the ordinary conditions together, one step moves found by 0.67 at the median and 6.2 at most; joint moves by 4.6 at the median and 10.9 at most. It is one-sided: the largest loss is 10.92 placements, the largest gain 4.00, because the fixed model was picked for scoring well. No neighbour puts a box on a clean empty frame.

**The margins.** Each margin is the largest change, in that direction, that any of the 26 neighbours produced at that level, and never less than one whole unit. The script writes them to `results/baseline/margins.json`: a `judge` block (found up 4.00 / down 10.92; boxes up 1.85 / down 1.00; a drop per place) and a `gate` block (a drop in found and a rise in boxes per protected set). It keeps the margins as first registered beside them (one number for both directions). A third set, C, of 12 joint moves is never used for a margin: it checks them. The pass rule called 2 of the 12; the never-regress check none.

**How to run.** `python barrels/experiments/2026-11-26_noise_floor/run.py` (about 2 minutes on 8 processes). Re-run it when the default color model or the default detector changes, and commit the new `margins.json`.

**What can go wrong.** The neighbours are small real changes, not random noise: a place whose barrels sit on a threshold edge gets a wide margin, and that margin describes today's default only. HOME is run with the model as it is, not leave one scene out (a neighbour is moved by hand; there is nothing to re-fit).

### The never-regress check, with a margin — `src/barrels/gate.py`, `tools/report.py --gate` / `--save-baseline` (2026-11-26, same branch; replaces the first version below)

**What it does.** The principle, in Tanush's words of 2026-11-26: never-regress was meant as a general principle, not a strict rule. A change may not make the protected sets worse by more than a change that means nothing moves them. The command prints every barrel and box that changed and ends in `RESULT: PASS` (exit code 0) or `RESULT: FAIL` (1).

**The idea, in the order the data flows.**

1. *The stored run* (`snapshot`, `save_baseline`). `--save-baseline` runs the detector and writes `results/baseline/baseline.json`: one line per frame with its data set, its arrangement, which placement each true barrel is, and one letter per barrel and per box (F found, O box off, M missed; W wrong color, S second box, N on nothing). Because it is per frame, the check can name the barrel that changed, and a change is a one-line diff in git.
2. *What is protected* (`load_protected`). `results/baseline/protected.json` lists the protected sets, each a name and the data sets it is made of: HOME, CAMPUS, GLASS (the glass table under its five lights: one set, 15 placements), PALEWOOD, ABPWHITE (the occlusion set), BATH-COOL (the bathroom under the cool lamp). The last four are new: the conditions we already handle.
3. *The margins* (`load_margins`). From the `gate` block of `margins.json`: per protected set, how far found may drop and how far the boxes that are not on their barrel may rise. Today 1.00 / 1.00 everywhere, except BATH-COOL at 5.00 / 1.00 (one hue unit of the blue range costs it 5 of 15). Without the file: one unit everywhere.
4. *Set by set* (`compare`, `SetChange`). The frames of a protected set are tallied in the baseline and in this run (`counting.tally`: found per placement, boxes once per arrangement). **WORSE** = found lower by more than the margin, or boxes higher by more than the margin. **lower, within the margin** = lower, but not by more. **better**, **same**. **cannot be compared** = the set has other frames or other labels than in the baseline. The barrels and frames that changed are listed under the set whatever its state. One barrel lost in one scenario and one gained in another is "same", with both listed.
5. *Empty frames* (in `compare`). A clean empty frame with more boxes than in the baseline fails the check, in any set: the one hard line, and the noise run gives no reason to move it. Boxes on empty frames that hold clutter, counted once per arrangement over all sets, may rise by their margin (1.00).
6. *Pass or fail* (`GateResult.passed`). Fail: a protected set is WORSE or cannot be compared; a new box on a clean empty frame; boxes on empty frames with clutter up by more than the margin. Every other data set is compared the same way and printed for information; it cannot fail the check.
7. *The reference does not slide* (`GateResult.lower`). A run that is lower inside a margin passes. `--save-baseline` still refuses to store it without `--force`. Otherwise ten changes that each lose one barrel would each pass against a baseline saved just before.

**How to run.** As before: `python barrels/tools/report.py --gate` before every merge that touches the pipeline, the detector or the geometry; `--gate --cand-det field=value` or `--cand-model file` for a change that is not the default yet; `--save-baseline` after a merge that changed the numbers on purpose (commit the file). To protect another condition: add it to `protected.json`, re-run the noise floor so that it gets a margin, save the baseline, commit the three files. On today's defaults: PASS, nothing changed.

**What can go wrong.** A margin of one placement on a set of 15 is 7 % of it; the check sees what the counts see. The margins describe today's default. HOME is still scored leave one scene out here, while its margin was measured with the model as it is. A baseline saved before 2026-11-26 has row counts only and is refused with the command to save a new one.

### The standard pass rule — `src/barrels/judge.py`, `tools/judge.py` (2026-11-26, same branch; awaiting walkthrough)

**What it does.** Says whether a candidate is better than a baseline, worse, or neither: PASS, FAIL, NO CLEAR DIFFERENCE. One rule for a color-step idea, a detector idea or a whole new pipeline. Before it, each experiment wrote its own rule, and they were tighter than the noise.

**The idea, in the order the data flows.**

1. *A detector is a function* `detect_frame(stem, frame) -> list[Barrel]`: the frame's name and its pixels in (`pipeline.Frame`; `frame.rgb` is the picture), boxes out. `model_detector(model, det_overrides)` wraps a color-model file and `DetConfig` fields into one; `fixed_detector()` is what runs today. Anything else can be handed in as it is.
2. *Which frames* (`train_frames`). Every train frame with a box label, by the class of its condition in `data/conditions.json`: ordinary (154 frames, 17 conditions, 8 places), extreme, excluded, unlisted. Dave's frames and the geometry frames are left out.
3. *The guard* (`require_train`, `load_frame`). Before any image is opened: a test frame raises `TestFrameError`, a frame of no split raises too. `load_frame` hands out the picture read-only, so no step can change a pixel.
4. *Detectors on frames* (`run_many`). Each image is read once and handed to the baseline and to the candidate. Every frame's boxes are put through `counting.classify_frame`. Result: a `Run` per detector.
5. *Two runs side by side* (`compare`). Tallies per place, per condition and over all ordinary conditions; the extreme conditions apart, report only. Every true barrel whose state differs is listed (gained = now a strict hit, lost = was one), and every frame whose boxes-not-on-their-barrel differ.
6. *The verdict* (`decide`). Four clauses, candidate minus baseline, numbers from `margins.json`:
   - found over all ordinary places: up by more than 4.00 placements = better; down by more than 10.92 = worse;
   - found at any one place: down by more than that place's margin = worse (1.00 at most places, 6.00 at the ABP gray table, 2.34 on the ottoman, 1.25 on the bathroom floor);
   - boxes that are not on their barrel: up by more than 1.85 = worse; down by more than 1.00 = better;
   - boxes on empty frames: any more on clean empty frames = worse; with clutter, up by more than 1.00 = worse.
   FAIL if any clause says worse. PASS if none does and found is better (or boxes are better and found is not lower). NO CLEAR DIFFERENCE otherwise.
7. *Fitted numbers* (`leave_one_place_out`). A candidate that learned anything from frames is given as `fit(train_stems, held_place) -> detect_frame`. The first time a frame of a place comes up, `fit` is called with the ordinary train frames of the other places, and what it returns is used for that place only. So no frame is scored by something fitted on its own place.

**How to run.**

```
python barrels/tools/judge.py --cand-det stripe_merge=1.68               # a detector number changed, against today
python barrels/tools/judge.py --cand-model results/<x>/config.json        # a color model file against today
python barrels/tools/judge.py --cand-callable path/to/idea.py:detect_frame
python barrels/tools/judge.py --cand-fit path/to/idea.py:fit              # leave one place out
```

From Python: `judge.judge(detect_frame)` or `judge.judge(fit=fit)`; it returns a `Judgement` (`.result`, `.verdict.clauses`, `.changes`, `.places`). One run takes about 25 s (two detectors on 231 frames). A working example of both kinds of candidate: `experiments/2026-11-26_judge/example_candidate.py`. Exit code 0 = PASS, 1 = FAIL, 3 = NO CLEAR DIFFERENCE.

**What it said on known cases** (`experiments/2026-11-26_judge`, `results/2026-11-26_judge/summary.md`). Today's detector against itself: NO CLEAR DIFFERENCE, nothing changed. The 26 changes that mean nothing: all NO CLEAR DIFFERENCE (the margins come from them); of 12 more it had never seen, 10. Tanush's hand thresholds per condition: PASS, found 133.8 → 156.8 placements (211 → 281 per frame). "Blue follows the light", leave one place out as on 2026-11-25: PASS for the version that reads the light from the whole frame (+5.9 placements, 211 → 233 per frame, all at the bathroom floor and the ottoman), NO CLEAR DIFFERENCE for the version that reads it above the horizon gate (+3.2).

**What can go wrong.** NO CLEAR DIFFERENCE is not "no effect": a gain under 4 placements cannot be told from one hue unit with 172 placements. A PASS close to the margin is weak: one of the 12 unseen changes that mean nothing gained 5.4 and passed. The place margin of the ABP gray table is 6 of 16, so the rule is nearly blind there. A candidate's `fit` could read frames it should not; it is handed train frames of other places only, and `load_frame` refuses the rest.

### The never-regress check, first version — `src/barrels/gate.py`, `tools/report.py --gate` / `--save-baseline` (2026-11-18; replaced on 2026-11-26 by the check with a margin above; kept as the record)

**What it does.** The project rule (`TODO.md`, "The goal"): no scenario row of the one table on home or campus may get worse, and nothing may be drawn on an empty frame of any set. Until now this was checked by eye. `python barrels/tools/report.py --gate` runs the detector, compares the table with stored numbers row by row, prints what got worse and what got better, and ends with `RESULT: PASS` (exit code 0) or `RESULT: FAIL` (exit code 1). It decides nothing about what a found barrel is: every number is a cell of the one table.

**The idea, in the order the data flows.**

1. *The stored numbers* (`snapshot`, `save_baseline`). `--save-baseline` runs the detector as it is today and writes `results/baseline/baseline.json` (committed): for every data set and every scenario, the frames, barrels, found, missed, false boxes and boxes on empty frames, and at the top the date, the git hash, the color model and the full `DetConfig` they came from. One row per line, so a change shows as a one-line diff.
2. *The protected sets* (`load_protected`). `results/baseline/protected.json` holds the short list: HOME and CAMPUS. Every other set (the black table, the wood ladder, Dave's) is extreme or indicative: shown, never a reason to fail.
3. *This run.* `--gate` runs one arm, the candidate: today's defaults, or with `--cand-det field=value` / `--cand-model file` a change that is not the default yet. Always every data set, train frames, home leave-one-scene-out (the options that would change that are refused).
4. *Row by row* (`compare`, `RowChange`). For each scenario row: **worse** = fewer barrels found, or more false boxes (one barrel gained and one false box gained is worse); **better** = the opposite with nothing worse; **new** = the row is not in the baseline (a new data set, or a new scenario in an old one); **changed** = the row has other frames or another number of barrels than in the baseline, so the two counts cannot be compared; **gone** = in the baseline, not in this run.
5. *Pass or fail* (`RowChange.fails`, `GateResult.passed`). Fail when a protected row is worse; when a protected row is changed or gone (the check cannot vouch for it: look at the table, then save a new baseline); or when **any** row of **any** set has more boxes on empty frames than the baseline (a new row counts from zero). Everything else passes: a drop on an extreme set is printed under "for information only", a new set is printed as NEW.
6. *Out* (`format_gate`). The rows that got worse, the rows that got better, new boxes on empty frames, new rows, the other sets for information, one line of totals per set, then the result line naming the rows that failed it.

**How to run.** Before every merge that touches the pipeline, the detector or the geometry: `python barrels/tools/report.py --gate` on the branch (about 10 s). To test a changed number before making it the default: `python barrels/tools/report.py --gate --cand-det stripe_merge=1.68` (`DetConfig` holds numbers only since the collapse of 2026-11-26; naming a removed switch stops with a message). After a merge that changed the numbers on purpose: `python barrels/tools/report.py --save-baseline` and commit the file, so that the gain is protected from then on; it prints the comparison with the baseline it replaces and refuses to store worse numbers unless `--force` is given. **A new ordinary condition is protected** by adding its name (as the table prints it) to `results/baseline/protected.json`, running `--save-baseline`, and committing both files. The baseline of 2026-11-18: home 27 of 29, campus 34 of 35, no false box on either, no box on any empty frame.

**What can go wrong.** A scenario row is one or two frames, so the check is as coarse as the table. It compares counts, not which barrel: losing one barrel and finding another in the same row reads as "same". It protects the train frames only; the test frames are never opened by it. If the baseline is not saved again after a gain, the gain is not protected (a later change may lose it and still pass). Adding or re-labeling frames in a protected set makes its rows "changed", which fails until a new baseline is saved with `--force` after a look at the table.

## 5. The live feed — `live/` + `worldmap_bridge.py` + `tracker.py` (2026-11-05; the multi-frame program and frame capture 2026-11-06)

**What it does.** Shows sections 1 and 3 on the robot's own camera, in a window on the laptop. Dave's ask from the 2026-11-03 meeting. Display only: the world map still runs on the vendor detector. Two programs: `BarrelViewer` answers every frame on its own (the spec's answer, once a second); `BarrelTracker` shows boxes that persist over frames (Tanush's ask after the first live run, 2026-11-06).

**The idea.** aim_fsm programs are state machines: *nodes* do something when entered, *transitions* move between nodes when an event happens. `BarrelViewer`'s own node is `ShowFrame`: it reads `robot.camera_image` (the latest frame, RGB; skipped when no new frame has arrived since the last tick), hands it to the hook, and shows the result (`program.show`, which calls Dave's `imshow`). A one-second timer (`=T(1)=>`) re-enters `ShowFrame`, so the window refreshes once a second. The nodes both programs share live in `live/barrel_live.py`: `SetMode` changes which picture is shown, `Save` and `Record` write raw frames (below). Each is reached by a text message (`tm seg` at the `C>` prompt fires `=TM('seg')=>`) and returns to the loop at once (`=N=>`). The whole machine is six lines in `$setup{}`; `genfsm` turns them into the Python that wires nodes to transitions (`live/BarrelViewer.py`, committed so `simple_cli BarrelViewer` works without regenerating).

**The hook.** `BarrelDetectorHook` holds the three things the detector needs — the segmentation model (the hs model `eb863d84` since 2026-11-09), the detector and its config (`detector.detect`, `DetConfig()`, followed by the contrast stage), the camera preset (`AIM_4A8B6118_BOXFIT`) — and `default()` builds that exact combination, so "the detector as it stands" is defined in one place and `tools/detect_image.py` loads the same model file. One escape hatch: the environment variable `BARRELS_MODEL` names another model file, and `default()` (hence both live programs and `detect_image.py`) loads that instead, so a candidate front end can be tried on the robot before it is adopted (`RUNBOOK.md`). `view(image_rgb, mode)` is the live frame's whole path: RGB → BGR (OpenCV's order, what the pipeline takes), `pipeline.detect_frame` (`segment`, section 1; `detect`, section 3; the stripe by contrast, section 3b; since 2026-11-27 — the hook keeps the mask of section 1 for the seg view), then `detector.draw` for `"boxes"` (the v2 output image: yellow = orange barrel, green = blue, 1 px), the dimmed frame for `"seg"` (below), or the dimmed frame with the boxes for `"both"`; then BGR → RGB because `imshow` wants RGB. `render(image_rgb, barrels, mode, raw=None)` is the drawing half on its own, for a program that decides itself which barrels to draw; `view` = `on_image` + `render`. A test checks that `view` produces pixel-for-pixel what `detect_image.py` writes for the same frame.

**The seg view (`tm seg`, two windows since 2026-11-11).** The question it answers is "which pixels did the color step call orange or blue?". *Main window:* the camera frame with every pixel that is **not** classified orange or blue multiplied by 0.3 (`wb.SEG_DIM`), so the scene is still readable but dark; the classified pixels are copied unchanged, at full brightness, in the camera's own colors. *Second window* (titled like the main one plus ` seg`: `barrels seg`, `barrels (tracked) seg`): only the classified pixels, painted in their **class color** (orange RGB 255,128,0; blue RGB 0,100,255 — the colors `viz` uses) on black, so it also says which of the two classes each pixel got; everything else is black. `tm both` is the same pair with the boxes drawn on the main window. `tm boxes` goes back to the plain frame with boxes and closes the second window on the next frame shown. No pixel is corrected anywhere: the frame is only copied or darkened for display, and the detector never sees these pictures. The arithmetic is one plain function, `wb.seg_views(image, mask, dim, colors)` → `(dimmed, seg_only)`, with its own unit test; `hook.render` uses the first for the main window, `hook.seg_only()` builds the second from the last mask. The window work is `BarrelLive.show(title, picture)` in `live/barrel_live.py`: `imshow` the main picture; in `seg` / `both` also `imshow` the second; otherwise `destroyWindow` it if it was open. It is called from `ShowFrame` exactly where `imshow` was called before (Dave's `imshow` / `destroyWindow` hand the Qt work to the main thread themselves). Before 2026-11-11 `tm seg` only tinted the one window (class color blended 50 % over the image), which neither isolated the segmented pixels nor showed their true color.

**The multi-frame view (`BarrelTracker`).** The detector still answers each frame on its own — that answer is what the eval scores and what `tm raw` overlays in white. `tracker.Tracker` sits on top and decides what to *show*. Each frame's barrels are matched to the known tracks by color and box overlap (IoU ≥ 0.3, best overlap first, each used once); unmatched ones start new tracks. A track is shown once it has been seen in 3 of the last 4 frames, stays shown through one missed frame, is hidden at two, and is forgotten after four misses in a row. The box drawn is the per-edge median of the last three matched boxes, so one odd frame cannot move it. `BarrelTracker.fsm` runs the loop five times a second (`=T(0.2)=>`) and counts only distinct camera frames (`robot.frame_count`). Worked example at 5 fps: a barrel that enters the picture is drawn 3 frames later (0.6 s); missing from one frame it stays; missing from two it disappears; back for three it reappears, on the same track. What this cannot do: hide a mistake that is there frame after frame, like the floating box of the first live run — that is the detector's to fix.

**Saving frames (`capture.py`).** `tm save` writes the current frame as `snapshots/robot_snapN.png` under `barrels/live/` (the camera viewer's naming, N continuing past existing files, so `tools/import_snaps.py --src barrels/live/snapshots` takes them as usual). `tm rec` records every frame for ten seconds, at most ten a second, into `snapshots/rec_<stamp>/` with a `frames.json` of times and frame counts. Recording runs in `user_image`, the per-frame callback aim_fsm gives every program, on the image thread, so it costs the detector loop nothing; a disk error stops the recording, never the stream. Frames come in RGB and are written BGR, so they load like every committed frame.

**Where it lives.** `live/BarrelViewer.fsm`, `live/BarrelTracker.fsm` (≈ 50 lines each, half docstring), `live/barrel_live.py` (the base: hook, modes, `new_frame`, `show`, `report`, `user_image`, the shared nodes); `worldmap_bridge.BarrelDetectorHook` with `default()`, `segment`, `on_image` (barrels), `view` (picture), `render` (picture for given barrels), `seg_only` (the second window), `annotate`; `wb.seg_views`, `wb.DEFAULT_MODEL`, `wb.VIEWS`; `tracker.TrackConfig` / `Tracker.update` / `visible`; `capture.FrameCapture.save` / `start_recording` / `on_frame`. The rest of `worldmap_bridge.py` is the parked world-map route.

**How to run it.** `RUNBOOK.md`: `cd barrels/live && simple_cli BarrelViewer` (per frame) or `simple_cli BarrelTracker` (persisting boxes); `tm seg` / `tm boxes` / `tm both`; `tm save` / `tm rec`; `tm raw` in the tracker; Ctrl+\ to quit; `genfsm X.fsm` only after editing an `.fsm`. ≈ 60 ms per frame on the laptop.

**What can go wrong.** First run 2026-11-06 (Tanush, the back of his laptop as a plain backdrop on a campus table): the window and the controls work; the detector drew a floating orange box above the orange barrel and missed the barrel itself. Rebuilt from his screenshots (`experiments/2026-11-06_live_first_run/`): the stripe finder took a band of unlabeled pixels under a false orange smear for a shadowed stripe, and threw the real blue stripe away because the unlabeled shadow glued to it made its band taller than three stripe heights. That is the detector-hardening task; the segmentation's own false orange (the edge of the laptop lid, the glow a barrel throws on the table, skin) and its missing shaded faces are the redesign's. On a scene like Dave's office the segmentation finds nothing (section 1), so the boxes view is just the camera image; `tm seg` shows why (the whole main window dim, the second window black). The camera preset is fit for Tanush's robot; on another robot the predicted bottoms will be off by the tilt difference. If the window never appears, no frame has arrived yet (the console says so); if it freezes, `restart img_thread`.
