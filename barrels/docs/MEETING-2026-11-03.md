   - *How many barrels:* the geometry says how wide one barrel is at that row; blob width ÷ that, rounded, is the count; cut at the waist (the narrowest column near the halfway point — two touching barrels meet only at their bulges); each piece's color is its majority color, the body.
   - *Bottom edge:* the top row says the distance, the distance says where the base must be; keep the seen bottom when it agrees within 15 % of the height, otherwise draw the predicted one and flag it; clip to the frame.
   - A piece must be barrel-sized for its row (0.4–1.6 × the expected width, and nearer than ≈ 60 cm) or it is dropped — that removes a hand at the horizon.

## What works — the numbers

16 October training frames, same pixels for both rows, nothing tuned (`results/2026-11-03_det_step1/`, one sheet per frame to show):

| | new detector | one box per blob (baseline) |
|---|---|---|
| mean F1 over orange and blue | **0.78** | 0.46 |
| overlap of matched boxes with the truth (IoU) | 0.87 | 0.75 |
| bottom-edge error, median | 1 px | 1 px |
| boxes on the two empty frames | 0 | 0 |

F1 = the blend of precision (of the boxes drawn, how many are real) and recall (of the barrels, how many got a box); 0.78 is roughly four in five both ways. Touching rows of two to four barrels split correctly; shadow-shortened boxes get the right bottom.

## Finding 1 — the camera tilt

With the tilt we measured in October (23.3°) every predicted bottom came out about 20 px too low and the detector scored 0.52. Fitting the tilt from 24 labeled barrels — known barrel height and width as the ruler, focal length held at 400 — gives 22.25°, horizon row 76, and bottoms within about 5 px; score 0.78. What the labeled barrels actually pin is the horizon row: focal length and tilt trade off (356 px with 24.75° fits exactly as well as 400 px with 22.25°). Your known-distance frames (0–300 mm, one barrel on the crosshair) separate them — that's your lookup table, used as a calibration so the formula covers every row. Plan: 12 such frames; the question is whether per robot or once.

## Finding 2 — on your snapshots the segmentation finds nothing

Zero barrel pixels on all 20 frames, 0 of 34 barrels found. Two causes, both invisible on my October data:

- Both adaptive parts of the v1 front end (the per-image saturation floor and the wall model) are built from rows 0–50 on the assumption that those rows are plain background. Your shelf is cluttered and colorful, so the floor comes out at 96–120 instead of ≈ 50 and everything is rejected. (Your question — why assume the wall is plain? — is exactly right.)
- With those parts switched off, your pink speckled desk floods the orange class: the fixed RGB color boxes don't separate it from barrel orange either.

Diagnostic variants (not tuning): fixed floor 50 → 3 of 30 barrels; fixed floor + wall model off → 15 of 30 but 67 false boxes. The detector logic cannot be judged on your frames until the front end is redone. Your close-ups (lid filling the frame, bottom far below it) and the dark warm-lit table are cases my data lacks.

## Fallbacks and caveats — say these before being asked

- The 0.78 is on training frames. Five held-out frames are untouched, kept for one look once the detector is complete.
- The tilt was fit on those same training frames (one physical number, but still).
- The truth boxes are the automatic per-barrel split of my October pixel masks, not hand-drawn boxes; a few are wrong (one merged pair), which costs the detector a hit it earned. I'm fixing them by hand today.
- Step 1 alone has no notion of "barrel" beyond color and size (a tomato at the right distance gets a box) and loses a barrel a hand touches; the stripe step fixes both. Still not handled: a barrel whose stripe is entirely hidden behind another barrel.
- Also not handled, by design for now: knocked-over barrels, wood tables, the world map.

## Done at 5:45 — the stripe step (fresh, not yet walked through)

A band of one color sandwiched between the other color above and below, of the height the geometry expects at that row, proves "this is a barrel"; every stripe is one barrel, wherever the blob boundaries fall. On the training frames against my hand-fixed boxes, overlap rule: floor 0.48 → step 1 0.86 → **stripe step 0.97**. Under the strict rule we agreed (every barrel counts, occluded ones included, and the bottom edge within a tenth of the height): floor 0.43 → step 1 0.71 → **stripe step 0.91**, with 23 of 25 matched bottom edges within 1 px. Touching rows, the hand, and a partly hidden barrel whose stripe shows are all handled now; a barrel whose stripe is *entirely* hidden behind another is still missed — the honest remaining gap. (The 0.86 for step 1 is higher than the 0.78 above because my hand-fixed boxes replaced the automatic ones.)
