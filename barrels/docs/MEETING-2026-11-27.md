# The week, for the meeting with Dave (2026-11-27)

Plain English, short. Every number is on train frames unless it says "test". Details behind each line: `REPORT-2026-11-27.md`, `EXPERIMENTS.md`, `DETECTOR-MAP.md`.

## The story in six lines

1. We set out (meeting of 11-16/18) to **match the vendor in its conditions, work in more ordinary ones, fix occlusion, and never regress**.
2. **Occlusion and close-ups** got a week of rules, tested mostly on simulated scenes. They helped on simulation; on real frames the gain was small.
3. **We shot 15 new conditions** (black ottoman, bathroom floor, glass table, gray table, pale wood, each under several lights) plus a real occlusion set, all with the vendor's answers saved.
4. **Adaptive thresholds failed** in four rounds. The new conditions showed the real problem: **color**, not occlusion. On the 11-25 test look the vendor beat us, 23 to 14 of 32.
5. **A review found why**, fixed the measuring, and rebuilt the detector to be readable without changing a single box.
6. **Thirteen experiments** later, one idea wins clearly: **find the stripe by color difference** ("stripe by contrast"). On train frames: 155 of 172 barrels against 134; ahead of the vendor 92 to 83 of 108. Now being adopted, then one planned look at the test frames.

## 1. Occlusion (11-18 to 11-24)

| step | what | result |
|---|---|---|
| side hidden | a partly seen stripe gets a box only when a side has a reason to be hidden: the frame edge, a nearer barrel, or the body and stripe ending apart | campus 35 of 35 (the live miss with you is found) |
| scorecard | 3888 simulated scenes: a barrel hidden 25/50/75 % by 15 kinds of thing | found 2153 → 2811 of 3888; half hidden 771 → 1160 of 1296 |

**Worked:** occlusion by another barrel, cut stripes, frame edges. **Didn't:** a barrel 60–75 % hidden by a non-barrel thing (a box, a book): in the mask that thing looks like bare table. **Caveat found later:** most rules were justified on the simulator only; on the 15 real hidden barrels 9 are found.

## 4. Adaptive thresholds (11-18 to 11-25): did not work, and why

The plan: Tanush sets thresholds by hand per frame; find something in the frame that predicts them; that becomes the rule.

- **The idea was sound in one sense:** Tanush's hand thresholds per condition find 281 of 307 barrels against 211. Colors set per place are enough.
- **Predicting them failed**, four rounds. The reasons, found in the review:
  1. **Barrels are 4 % of the pixels.** Whole-frame statistics describe the floor and the walls, not the barrels. At home the "orange peak" reads hue 21; the barrels are hue 7.
  2. **There is no single light shift.** Orange follows the surface, blue follows the lamp; they move in opposite directions in 15 of 17 conditions.
  3. **Too few places** (3, then 5, two with warm light) against hundreds of settings tried.
  4. **The pass rules were tighter than the noise.** One barrel is 0.03 of a condition's score; the rule allowed 0.02. Even the hand thresholds failed them.
- **One part did work:** "blue follows the light" gained 22 barrels and lost none; it was rejected by one box. Re-judged fairly: +3.9, just under the bar.
- **What replaced it:** instead of asking "is this pixel the home blue", ask "is this band bluer than the body above and below it". The lamp shifts all three together, so the difference survives. That is stripe by contrast.

## 5. The test look (11-25)

One planned look, written first. Home 9 of 9, campus 22 of 24. **New conditions: ours 14, vendor 23 of 32.** The gap was color on the ottoman and the dim and warm bathroom.

## 6. The review and the rebuild (11-26)

- **Measuring fixed:** each physical barrel counted once; a box on the right barrel with a slightly off bottom no longer counts as a miss and a false box; one pass rule for every experiment, with margins measured from how much a meaningless change (one hue unit) moves the score; the never-regress check has a margin.
- **Detector rebuilt to be readable:** 30 on/off switches and 51 settings became 18 numbers and ten named stages. **Every box is the same**, checked on 5435 masks. `DETECTOR-MAP.md` is the flow chart.

## 7. The experiments (11-26 to 11-27)

Judged with one whole place held out at a time. Today's detector: 133.8 of 172 barrels. A gain counts above +4.

| idea | result | in one line |
|---|---|---|
| **stripe by contrast** | **PASS, +21.5** | a band bluer (redder) than the body above and below it, at the size its row predicts; then that barrel's own colors are painted |
| histogram peaks over stripe-pattern pixels | PASS, +14.0 | barrels are small peaks; counting only stripe-pattern pixels finds their color 9 times in 10 |

| near-miss colors next to strong evidence | PASS, +13.7 | a pixel just outside the range counts beside a strong partner color |

| blue follows the light | +3.9, not enough | |
| combinations | +0.2 to +1.8 over contrast | inside the noise |

**Against the vendor** (6 places with vendor files, 108 barrels): today 70.8, vendor 83.3, **stripe by contrast 92.3**, careful combination 94.1. Contrast still loses at the gray table (15 vs 16) and the ottoman (10 vs 12.3).

## 8c. Two questions Tanush asked

- **A barrel hidden by a thing that is not a barrel (a box, a book):** in the mask that thing looks like bare table, so the detector has no reason to call a side hidden. Today it boxes such a barrel only if the outline fits one end, or, when at least 0.4 of the stripe width shows, by a centred guess. The bulge rule (`wip/exp-sidecut`) placed the box from the curved side and found both real cases, but drew a box on a tin's label and one on the wrong side, so it was not adopted. The fix still to try: look at the photo at the cut (an object edge, not table), and shoot more real side cuts to test on. This is the biggest thing left.
- **Is the overlap rule too strict?** Not for us: a box on the right barrel that fails the strict rule (overlap 0.5, bottom within 10 %) happens 1.2 times in 108 barrels for us, 8.2 times for the vendor. The rule costs the vendor more than us. Counting "on the barrel" instead would close part of the gap: vendor 91.5 against our 93.5.
