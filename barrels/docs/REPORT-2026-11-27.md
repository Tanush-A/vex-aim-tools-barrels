## Why the shape checks worked when geometry did not

The geometry checks ask where a thing is and how big it is. A jar with an orange lid and a blue label, at barrel size, passes them: today's detector already boxes it on three frames. What tells it apart is texture: a barrel's paint is solid, a printed label is speckled (traps 0.17–0.39, barrels from 0.40), and the jar's "stripe" is a label too tall for its row. Size, edges and the bulge did not separate the traps: the masks are too ragged.

## 2. What changed in the code (merged into `barrels`)

- **The detector was rebuilt to be readable.** 51 settings and 30 on/off switches became 18 numbers and no switches. `detect` is now ten named stages in order: pieces, stripes, a box per stripe, close-ups, verify, barrels behind barrels, pairs, half stripes, verify again. Stripes come first; geometry is the ruler inside every check. **Every box is the same as before**: checked on 5435 masks, twice, independently. Map: `DETECTOR-MAP.md`.
- **The measuring was fixed.** Barrels are counted once per physical barrel, not once per lamp setting (243 "barrels" were really 108). A box on the right barrel with a slightly-off bottom no longer counts as a miss plus a false box. The vendor is assumed perfect on the white tables (your call).
- **One pass rule for every experiment** (`tools/judge.py`), with margins measured from how much a meaningless change (one hue unit) moves the score: a gain counts above +4 barrels. The old rules rejected everything, including your own hand thresholds.
- **The never-regress check now has a margin** (one barrel, one box) and protects six places instead of two.

## 3. What we learned

1. **Color per place is enough.** The best fixed colors per place find 163 of 172, and what they miss is hidden or frame-cut barrels. The color problem is "how to find the right colors without help", not "colors cannot work".
2. **The warm bathroom was a slider limit.** Its blue reads hue 26–44; the slider stopped at 60. With those hues: 14 of 15 found (the vendor finds 0).
3. **Asking "is this the home blue" is the wrong question.** Asking "is this band bluer than the body right above and below it" survives any lamp, because the lamp shifts all three together. That is why the contrast idea wins.
4. **Room statistics cannot predict the colors.** Barrels are 4 % of the pixels; the room's numbers describe the floor and walls. Orange follows the surface, blue follows the lamp.
5. **What is left after contrast is not color:** of 16.7 barrels still missed, 15 are hidden or cut by the frame edge.
