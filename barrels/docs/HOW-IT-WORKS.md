# HOW-IT-WORKS.md — the main code, in plain English

Written for Tanush, so he can explain the detector to Dave without help. One section per **main** module: color segmentation, camera geometry, the detector, the detection eval, plus the live feed. Side tools (labelers, importers) are not covered — they are allowed to be black boxes. The one-page version is `HOW-IT-WORKS-SHORT.md`; the file-level diagrams are `ARCHITECTURE.md`. Naming: what the lab notebook calls "detector step 2" (the stripe) is **step 4** of section 3 here. Rule since 2026-11-02 (`DECISIONS.md`): every commit that changes main code updates its section here and gets a walkthrough in chat first.

Every number in this file is a real number from the code or from a real frame, so you can check it: `4A8B_window_light_touch_02` (three touching barrels, orange–blue–orange, about 18 cm away) is the worked example throughout.

## 0. The whole thing in one paragraph

A photo comes in (640 × 480). **Section 1** looks at every pixel and says "barrel orange", "barrel blue" or "other", producing a *class mask* — an image the same size holding 0, 1 or 2 per pixel. **Section 3** takes that mask, groups the barrel pixels into blobs, works out how many barrels each blob is and where each barrel's edges are, using the camera geometry from **section 2** whenever an edge cannot be seen. Out comes a list of barrels, each with a color and a box, and the photo is written back with the boxes drawn on it. **Section 4** is how we score that against hand-drawn truth. The one command that does 1 → 3:

```
python barrels/tools/detect_image.py barrels/data/raw/4A8B_window_light_touch_02.png
```

Nothing in this chain learns anything from the photo it is looking at. The only "learned" numbers are two color boxes in section 1, fit once from labeled frames; everything else is either computed fresh from the image (the saturation floor, the wall model) or is physics (barrel size, camera height, tilt).

## 1. Color segmentation — `src/barrels/pipeline.py` (the hs color model, config `eb863d84`, the front end since 2026-11-09; v1 `afd85483` kept below as history)

**What it does.** Takes one image and labels every pixel as barrel-orange (1), barrel-blue (2), or other (0). This is the front end of everything after it; the detector works on these pixel labels, not on the raw image. (A fourth value, 255 = "ignore", exists only in hand-painted label masks for glare we did not want to score.)

### The "hs" color model — the front end (built 2026-11-08, adopted 2026-11-09)

**Why.** On the campus frames (2026-11-07) tests 2 and 3 above removed the barrels themselves: with a dark, cluttered room in rows 0–50 the floor came out at 67–120 and the wall model's spread at 35–52 per channel, so navy-blue bodies (saturation 52–84) and shaded orange faces were rejected as "background" — blue bodies 0–7 % labeled in nine frames (`EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-07, attribution). Every adaptive part assumed the top rows are a plain wall and the barrels the only colorful thing in the picture.

**The idea.** Describe a barrel color by two numbers that do not change with brightness, and read both off the labeled frames once:

- **Hue** (the position on the color circle, OpenCV units 0–179): orange barrels sit at about 5–12, blue ones at 104–118, in the home frames and on campus alike. Hue is computed from the ratios of R, G and B, so a lit face and a shaded face of the same barrel have the same hue. Orange sits next to the wrap at 0/180, so its hue is rotated by +30 before taking percentiles (a sector 3–13 would otherwise read as "3 to 168").
- **HSV saturation**, S = (max − min) / max, 0–255: how far from gray a color is *relative to its brightness*. A navy-blue shaded face (RGB 12, 45, 85) has S = 219 even though v1's max − min is only 73; a bluish-gray wall (160, 150, 140 or so) stays under 110. This is the number that separates a dark barrel from a gray wall, which v1's max − min could not.

Per class: the S floor = the `hs_s_pct` percentile of the labeled pixels' S; the hue sector = the `hs_h_pct` percentiles of the labeled pixels above that floor, widened by `hs_h_margin` on each side. Then a pixel is that class when it is below row 50, brighter than 40, inside the sector and above the floor; in both → the redder one is orange (the same tie rule). **No per-image floor, no wall model, no table model**: nothing is estimated from the image being segmented.

**The adopted numbers** (fit once on the 16 home train frames, `hs_s_pct=(5, 15), hs_h_pct=(5, 95), hs_h_margin=2`, config `eb863d84`, saved in `results/2026-11-09_hs_candidate/config.json`): **orange hue 3–13, S ≥ 140; blue hue 103–119, S ≥ 113.** Two floors because orange wants a low one (the small dull orange stripe of a far blue barrel, `room_01`) and blue a higher one (the bluish home wall enters the blue class below S ≈ 110). The campus `room_01` blue body (RGB ≈ 12, 45, 85; hue 108, S 219) passes; the home wall in the blue hue sector (S ≤ 110) does not; skin on the campus hand (hue 13, S ≈ 149) passes the orange floor of 140 in places and is kept out of the boxes by the detector's stripe requirement; the orange glue-stick cap (hue 9, S 222) passes and is refused by the detector because nothing sandwiches a band in it.

**Where it lives.** `worldmap_bridge.DEFAULT_MODEL` → the saved model file (what `detect_image.py`, `segment_image.py` and both live programs load; `BARRELS_MODEL` in the environment overrides it, `worldmap_bridge.V1_MODEL` is the old one). `Config(color_model="hs", hs_s_pct, hs_h_pct, hs_h_margin)`; `Frame.hs(shift)` (the hue/saturation planes, cached per shift); `fit_hs` (called by `fit`); the `"hs"` branch at the top of `segment`. The fields are omitted from the config hash at their defaults, so every v1 hash (`afd85483` included) is unchanged. `pipeline.HUE_SHIFT` = {orange: 30, blue: 0}.

**How it was chosen** (`EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-08 and 2026-11-08 later; `DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-09). 18 configurations (orange percentile 5/10/15 × blue 10/15/20 × margin 2/4), scored by a rule written before the run: home leave-one-scene-out v2 ≥ 0.91, zero false detections on every negative frame of both sessions, then the campus v2 score on Tanush's boxes. Winner `o5 b15 h5-95 m2`: **campus train 0.99** (v1: 0.41), home 0.93 (= v1), nothing on any negative, Dave's office 0.47 (indicative). An earlier count-level sweep's rule had picked a home-regressing config; that rule flaw is recorded, and the per-class floor was the fix.

**What can go wrong.** Hue is noise where S is low, so dark low-saturation pixels are excluded by the floor rather than by hue; the orange sector (3–13) touches skin and wood hue — the S floor (140) removes most skin and the detector's stripe requirement the rest; wood tables (in scope since 2026-11-09) will segment as orange and are the detector's problem; a bright blue object in the room (a jacket, a chair) is labeled blue, and only the detector's stripe and size checks keep it from becoming a barrel.

### v1 — the `rgb_box` model, the front end until 2026-11-09 (config `afd85483`; kept for the record, selectable with `--model results/2026-10-20_v1_test_adaptive/config.json`)

**The idea.** A pixel has to survive a chain of cheap tests to be called barrel. Each test throws out pixels that *cannot* be barrel; the last test decides which color the survivors are.

1. **Horizon gate.** Nothing above row 50 can be a barrel. The camera is 43 mm above the table and a barrel is 24 mm tall, so every barrel standing on the table appears below the horizon line, which for our robot is around row 76 (section 2). Row 50 is a safe margin above that. Those top rows are therefore *guaranteed background*, which the next two tests exploit.
2. **Saturation floor.** Barrels are vivid; tables, walls and glare are grayish. "Saturation" here is simply max(R, G, B) − min(R, G, B), a number from 0 to 255. The floor is not a fixed number: the program takes rows 0–50 (guaranteed background), finds the 99.5th percentile of their saturation (i.e. "how colorful is the most colorful 0.5 % of the background"), adds a margin of 10, and clamps the result to between 20 and 120. Any pixel below that floor is out. On `touch_02` the floor comes out at 48; on `core_01`, 55. A warm-lit room with a yellowish wall gets a higher floor by itself, with nothing changed in the code. This is what "adaptive thresholds, no image correction" means in practice.
3. **Wall model.** From the same background rows, a robust estimate (median and spread, per channel) of the background color, fit on the low-saturation background pixels; any pixel within 2.5 spreads of that color is rejected. Also per image. This catches bluish or warm walls that pass the floor.
4. **Brightness gate.** Any pixel whose brightest channel is below 40 is out: in near-black pixels the color is noise. This changes nothing on a light table; it exists for dark ones.
5. **Color boxes — the only learned part.** For each class, a box in RGB space: the 1st-to-99th percentile range of hand-labeled barrel pixels that survived the gates above, fit once on the 16 training frames. The frozen numbers are orange = R 74–211, G 14–121, B 4–104 and blue = R 0–127, G 11–110, B 36–185. A surviving pixel inside the orange box is orange, inside the blue box is blue; if it is inside both, the redder one (R > B) is orange. Outside both, it is other.

A "table model" built from the bottom rows, mirroring the wall model, exists as a switch but is **off**: a close barrel's shadow sits in those rows and fools it.

**Worked example.** Take a pixel on the shaded lower face of the blue barrel in `touch_02`, say RGB (30, 45, 110). Row ≈ 160, below 50: passes the horizon gate. Saturation 110 − 30 = 80 ≥ 48: passes the floor. Nothing like the gray-blue wall (the wall median is around (120, 125, 135) with a spread of a few units): passes the wall model. Brightest channel 110 ≥ 40: passes. Inside the blue box (R 0–127, G 11–110, B 36–185) and outside the orange box (B 110 > 104): **blue**. Now a slightly darker pixel on the same face, (25, 30, 60): saturation 35 < 48 — thrown out by the floor. That is the main v1 miss, and it is why the detector (section 3) must be able to fill in a bottom edge the mask does not show.

**Why the boxes alone don't work.** Barrel pixels in RGB form a long smear along the brightness axis (bright lit face, dark shaded face), so a box wide enough to include the shadowed faces also includes a lot of table and wall. The floor and the wall model do the separating; the boxes only decide orange vs blue among the survivors.

**Where it lives.** `Config` (every knob; defaults = the frozen v1 config; `horizon_row=50, s_min_mode="band", s_band_q=99.5, s_band_margin=10, s_min_lo=20, s_min_hi=120, v_min=40, bg_prior=True, k_bg=2.5, table_prior=False, box_space="rgb", box_pct=(1, 99), tie="rb_sign"`). `Frame.from_image(bgr)` (the image plus its saturation and brightness planes). `effective_s_min(frame, cfg)` (the per-image floor). `fit(frames, masks, cfg)` → `Model` (learns the two boxes; done once, saved in `results/2026-10-20_v1_test_adaptive/config.json`). `segment(frame, model)` → the mask + an info dict (`s_min_eff` is the floor it used).

**How to run it.** `python barrels/tools/segment_image.py image.png` writes the orange, blue, class-mask and overlay images to `results/segment_image/`.

**What can go wrong.** Shadowed lower faces of blue barrels fall below the saturation floor (the main miss; `far_02`'s middle orange barrel loses its lower face the same way). Skin lands inside the orange box — hands are orange to this program. Under a strong color cast the fixed RGB boxes can miss while the floor and wall model adapt; v2 allows one re-fit of the boxes on more frames if that happens.

**Numbers.** Held-out test: orange IoU 0.84, blue 0.81, zero false pixels on the empty frame (`EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-10-20). (IoU for pixels = correct barrel pixels ÷ (correct + missed + falsely added).)

## 2. Camera geometry — `src/barrels/geometry.py` (v1.5)

**What it does.** Converts between image rows and real-world distances for this robot's camera, and says how big a barrel should look at any place in the image.

**The idea.** The camera is a pinhole at a known height above the table (h = 43.47 mm), tilted down by an angle θ, with a focal length f that says how many pixels one radian of view is (≈ 400 px). A point on the table at horizontal distance d in front of the camera is seen at an angle atan(h / d) below horizontal; subtract the tilt, and the row it lands on is

    row = 240 + f · tan( atan(h / d) − θ )

(240 is the image center row.) Run it backwards and a row gives a distance:

    d = h / tan( atan((row − 240) / f) + θ )

A point that is not on the table but at height z (the top of a barrel) uses h − z instead of h. Two consequences worth holding in your head: rows near the horizon are very far away and a one-row change there is many millimetres, while rows near the bottom of the frame are close and a row is a fraction of a millimetre; and the **horizon row** — where a point infinitely far away lands — is 240 − f · tan θ, which for our numbers is **row 76**. Nothing on the table can appear above it.

**The barrel.** 24 mm tall, 25.5 mm across at the bulge, 18 mm across at the ends, stripe 6 mm tall centred at mid-height. The box's top is the *far* edge of the lid (9 mm beyond the barrel's axis), its bottom is the *near* edge of the base (9 mm in front of the axis), so top and bottom are at slightly different distances. `barrel_rows(d_axis)` applies all of that and returns the top row, the stripe's rows, the bottom row and the width in pixels for a barrel whose axis is d_axis away. With the current camera:

| barrel axis distance | top row | stripe rows | bottom row | width px | height px |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 mm | 212 | 348–390 | 422 | 204 | 210 |
| 100 mm | 154 | 211–235 | 263 | 102 | 109 |
| 150 mm | 131 | 166–183 | 204 | 68 | 73 |
| 200 mm | 118 | 143–156 | 174 | 51 | 55 |
| 300 mm | 105 | 121–130 | 142 | 34 | 37 |
| 400 mm | 98 | 110–116 | 126 | 26 | 28 |

So a barrel at 20 cm is about 51 px wide and 55 px tall with its bottom at row 174; seeing *any one* of those — the top row, the stripe, the width, the bottom — tells you the distance, and from the distance every other number in that row of the table. That is the whole trick the detector uses. The inverses are `bottom_row_from_top_row`, `bottom_row_from_stripe_row`, `bottom_row_from_width`, `expected_barrel_width_px(bottom_row=…)`, `expected_barrel_height_px(bottom_row=…)`.

**Worked example.** In `touch_02` the left orange barrel's top row is 122. Backwards through the formula with z = 24 mm: the lid's far edge is 191 mm from the camera, so the axis is 182 mm away, so the base's near edge is 173 mm away, which lands on row 182.6. The segmentation saw the bottom at row 175: 7.6 px higher than predicted, which is within what we expect from the camera numbers (next paragraph), so the seen bottom is believed.

**Where it lives.** `CameraModel(f, tilt_deg, height_mm)` with `horizon_row()`, `row_to_ground_distance(row)`, `point_row(d, z)`, `barrel_rows(d_axis)`, the inverses above, and `image_to_ground(u, v)` (reproduces the robot's own ground projection; kept for the world map later, not used in v2). Presets: `AIM_4A8B6118_BOXFIT` (the current default, f 400 / tilt 22.25°), `AIM_4A8B6118` (the October measurement, f 404 / tilt 23.3°), `NOMINAL` (Dave's 23° / 400), and the honest range `AIM_4A8B6118_RANGE`.

**How to run it.** `python -m barrels.geometry --tilt 22.25 --f 400` prints the table in `constraints.md` for any camera numbers.

**What can go wrong.** The tilt and focal length are only known to a few percent, and close to the robot small tilt errors move rows a lot (at 5 cm, 1° of tilt is ≈ 7 px). The known-distance `calib` frames (the calibration session, `TODO.md` P1) will measure both properly.

**Update 2026-11-03 — the preset was off.** On the labeled frames the October camera (f 404, tilt 23.3°, horizon row 66) put every predicted bottom edge about 20 px too low: the top row said "this barrel is closer than it is". Re-fitting the tilt from 24 labeled, fully visible barrels on the train frames (f held at 400, the known barrel height and width as the ruler) gives tilt 22.25°, horizon row 76 — inside the band measured in October. With it, predicted bottoms land within about 5 px of the truth. That is the provisional preset `AIM_4A8B6118_BOXFIT`, now the detector's default, until the known-distance `calib` session measures it properly. Note what the boxes actually pin down: the horizon row, not f and tilt separately (they trade off along a ridge: f 356 with tilt 24.75° fits exactly as well as f 400 with 22.25°). `experiments/2026-11-03_geo_boxfit/`, `EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-03.

**Numbers to remember.** Horizon row ≈ 76. A barrel at 20 cm is ≈ 51 px wide. The bottom edge leaves the frame when the barrel's base is within ≈ 28 mm of the bumper; in that zone the top edge moves ≈ 13 px per mm.

## 3. The detector — `src/barrels/detector.py` (v2; steps 1–3 built 2026-11-03, the stripe step the default since 2026-11-05, hardened a–g 2026-11-08/09)

**What it does.** Takes the class mask from section 1 and returns a list of barrels. Each barrel is a `Barrel` record (`detect_types.py`): `color` (orange or blue), `bbox` = `(x0, y0, x1, y1)` in pixels, half-open (x1 and y1 are one past the last pixel), `confidence` (0–1), `flags` (`bottom_inferred` / `top_inferred` when that edge was computed rather than seen; `occluded` when the stripe was partly hidden and the full box drawn anyway; `truncated` when the box reaches a frame edge), `area` (how many mask pixels it came from) and `source` (`"det2"` for a barrel found from its stripe, `"det1"` for a stripeless piece). `detect(mask, cfg, cam)` is the one entry point: mask in, barrels out, same signature as the dumb reference `detect_cc.detect`, so either can be plugged into `detect_image.py` or the experiment scripts. `draw(image, barrels)` paints a 1-px rectangle per barrel — yellow for an orange barrel, green for a blue one, so the lines stand out from the barrels — and nothing else: the v2 deliverable image.

Nothing is remembered between images and nothing is fit per image. The numbers in `DetConfig` (table below) were written down before the first run and have not been tuned.

### Step 1 — blobs (`find_blobs`)

Take every pixel that is orange *or* blue (both at once, so a barrel's body and its stripe land in the same blob). Run a 3 × 3 *morphological close* over it — "fill any hole or gap up to 3 px wide" — so a shaded, speckled face does not break a barrel into two or three bits. Then find the 8-connected components (groups of touching barrel pixels; diagonal touching counts). Throw away components under 20 px (specks). For each surviving blob, record its bounding box and how many of its pixels were orange and how many blue.

*`touch_02`:* the mask has 3409 orange and 2337 blue pixels. They form **two** blobs: one at x 149–237, y 122–178 (88 px wide, 56 tall, 1763 orange / 1884 blue — this is the orange *and* blue barrel merged, because the orange barrel's blue stripe touches the blue barrel's body, exactly the case Dave's notes call "color adjacency"), and one at x 240–288, y 122–175 (48 wide, 1646 orange / 453 blue — the right orange barrel on its own; the 453 blue are its stripe).

### Step 2 — how many barrels is this blob? (`expected_width_px`, `split_blob`)

Section 2 says how wide one barrel is at the blob's distance. Which distance? If the blob's bottom is inside the frame, its bottom row gives the distance (the base sits on the table, and the bottom row of a thing on the table is the most reliable distance cue). If the blob touches the frame's bottom edge, the real bottom is somewhere below the frame and the row is meaningless, so the top row is used instead (`expected_width_px` makes that choice).

Divide the blob's width by that expected width and round to the nearest whole number: that is how many barrels stand side by side in the blob. One → done. Two or more → cut the blob into that many pieces. Where to cut: not at the exact halfway point but at the *narrowest column* near it — barrels bulge in the middle, so two touching barrels have a waist where they meet, and the column with the fewest barrel pixels in a window of ± a quarter barrel-width around the equal-spacing point is that waist (if several columns tie, the one nearest the equal-spacing point wins). Each piece gets its own tight box around its own pixels and its own orange/blue counts, and its **color is whichever count is larger** — that is the body color, because the stripe is always the minority of a barrel's pixels.

*`touch_02`, first blob:* bottom row 178 → the expected barrel width at that row is 53.4 px. 88 ÷ 53.4 = 1.65 → rounds to **2** barrels. The narrowest column near x = 193 is found and the blob is cut there: piece A, x 149–193, 1301 orange / 335 blue → **orange**; piece B, x 193–237, 462 orange / 1549 blue → **blue**. Second blob: 48 ÷ 51.8 = 0.93 → **1** barrel, 1646 orange / 453 blue → orange. Three barrels, correct colors, at this point without the geometry having drawn a single edge.

### Step 3 — the bottom edge (`predicted_bottom`, `resolve_bottom`)

This is the step that exists because the bottom edge is the one the world map will use and the one most often hidden (shadow, another barrel, clutter, the frame edge). The top row of a piece says how far away the barrel is — a 24 mm-tall thing whose top appears at that row can only be at one distance — and from the distance, section 2 says where its base must be: that is `predicted_bottom(cam, top_row)`. Then `resolve_bottom` compares the predicted bottom with the bottom the mask shows:

- If they agree to within a tolerance — 15 % of the predicted box height, but never less than 4 px — the seen bottom is kept (it was seen; the geometry is only a check).
- If they disagree, the mask's bottom is wrong for one of the known reasons (a shadowed face made the blob too short, a reflection below the barrel made it too tall, an occluder hides the base) and the **predicted bottom replaces it**, with the flag `bottom_inferred`.
- Exception: when both the seen bottom and the predicted one are at or below the frame edge, the box is simply cut at the frame (no inference needed, nothing visible to infer from).
- Finally the bottom is clipped to the frame and any box touching a frame edge gets `truncated`.

*`touch_02`, piece A:* top row 122 → axis 182 mm away → predicted bottom 182.6. Seen bottom 175. Tolerance = max(4, 0.15 × (182.6 − 122)) = 9.1 px; the difference is 7.6 → **seen bottom kept**, no flag. Same for the other two (differences 9.5 and 7.6 against tolerances 9.5 and 9.1).

*`far_02`, the middle orange barrel:* top row 106 → 290 mm → predicted bottom 144.4; the mask's bottom is 136 because the shaded lower face fell below the saturation floor; tolerance 5.8; difference 8.4 → **bottom replaced by 144**, flagged `bottom_inferred`. The hand-labeled truth has the bottom at 137, so here the inference overshot by 7 px: at 30 cm the camera numbers are good to about 5 px and the tolerance is only 6, so the geometry can "correct" a bottom that was in fact right. That is a known weakness of step 1 at far range (see "What can go wrong").

### Shape checks — is this piece barrel-shaped for its row?

After the bottom is settled, the expected width at that bottom row is computed once more and the piece must satisfy all of:

- expected width ≥ 16 px, i.e. the barrel is nearer than about 60 cm (anything "smaller" would be off the far end of a table — or above the horizon, where the expected width is infinite or zero);
- piece width between 0.4 × and 1.6 × the expected width (narrower is a sliver — a stripe fragment, a reflection edge; wider is not one barrel).

*`far_02`:* the hand resting on the table edge produces orange specks around rows 60–85, near the horizon. Expected width there is 1.8–4.4 px → **all dropped** by the 16 px rule. That is how the hand's fingers disappear without any "skin detector".

### Step 4 — the stripe is the barrel (`find_stripes`, `barrel_from_stripe`; built 2026-11-03, the default since 2026-11-05)

Steps 1–3 box anything barrel-colored and barrel-sized. Step 4 adds the one thing that proves "barrel": the **stripe**, a band of the other color around the middle. With it, every stripe is one barrel, wherever the blob boundaries happen to fall, so touching and partly hidden barrels separate by themselves, and an orange thing without a stripe is not a barrel.

*Finding stripes.* Take the orange pixels as "body" (then repeat with blue). For every column, note where body pixels exist above and below. A **band** is a connected region of non-body pixels that has body above it *and* below it in every one of its columns — that is the sandwich. The band's pixels are either blue (a proper stripe) or unlabeled (a stripe the segmentation lost to shadow): either counts, and the ratio sets the confidence. The band must have stripe proportions for its row: the height of its colored core (10th-to-90th percentile of its blue rows) between 0.45 and 1.6 × the stripe height the geometry expects there, the whole band (core plus shadow) at most 3 ×, and the band at least 0.4 barrel-widths wide and not taller than wide. A band wider than 1.4 barrels is the merged stripes of touching same-color barrels (their stripes meet at the bulges) and is cut into equal parts.

*A barrel from its stripe.* The stripe's middle row (the median row of its colored pixels) gives the distance; the distance gives the full box the geometry predicts: top, bottom and width (section 2's table). Then the seen body extent in the stripe's own columns — walked upward from the band and downward from it while body pixels keep coming, gaps of 3 rows allowed — supplies the top and bottom when they agree with the prediction within the same 15 % tolerance as step 3; otherwise the predicted edge is drawn and flagged (`top_inferred`, `bottom_inferred`). A stripe narrower than 0.8 barrel-widths is partly hidden: the box is widened to a full barrel, on the side where something barrel-colored borders the stripe (the occluder), and flagged `occluded`. Clip, `truncated` as before.

*Stripeless pieces.* The step-1 pieces are then only kept where no stripe barrel already covers them **and** a stripe could not have been seen: the piece reaches the frame bottom (a close barrel, stripe below the frame — `topstripe_01`, `top_01`) or the expected stripe at that distance is thinner than 4 px. Everything else without a stripe is dropped — the hand, a tomato, a reflection.

*`touch_02`:* three bands found — blue between orange at x 152–191, orange between blue at 189–235, blue between orange at 241–288 — three barrels, no width rule needed. *`cluster_01`:* three of the four bunched barrels have a visible stripe and come out right; the fourth, directly behind another of the same color, shows no stripe and is missed (and the width rule, no longer trusted alone, is not allowed to guess it). That is the honest limit: **a barrel whose stripe is completely hidden is not found.**

*Status.* The default since 2026-11-05 (`DetConfig.require_stripe=True`): it beat steps 1–3 on the train frames under both rules (below). `detect_image.py --step1` runs steps 1–3 alone for comparison.

### The hardening (a–g; 2026-11-08/09, walkthrough acknowledged and merged 2026-11-09)

The campus attribution (`EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-07) confirmed the two flaws pre-registered after the first live run and found a third; a fourth showed up under the new color model. Each is a `DetConfig` field, so the 2026-11-05 behaviour stays reproducible (`stripe_mode="bands"`, `body_min=0`, `height_max=0`).

- **(a) A dark gap is not a stripe** (`dark_stripe=False`). The 2026-11-03 finder accepted a band with *no* stripe-colored pixel as a "shadowed stripe". On the laptop-lid scene that made a floating box out of the dark gap under a false orange smear; under the hue model it made boxes out of gaps in the patchily labeled home wall. Off by default; `dark_stripe=True` restores it.
- **(b) The stripe is measured from its own pixels** (`stripe_mode="cores"`). The old band was *everything that is not body color* between body above and below, so unlabeled shadow glued to a stripe made its band taller than 3 × the expected stripe height and the stripe was dropped (campus `core_01`, `plain_01`, `close_01`, `window_01`). Now a stripe is a connected component of the *other* color whose every pixel has body color within 3 stripe heights above **and** below it (the sandwich, pixel by pixel — so a stripe touching a neighbouring barrel's body is cut from that body), with the same proportions checks as before on the component itself: height 0.45–1.6 × the expected stripe height, at least 0.4 barrels wide, not taller than wide; wider than 1.4 barrels → cut evenly as before. Shadow around the stripe changes nothing because it is never part of the component.
- **(c) A box must hold body color** (`body_min=0.1`). **`stripe_join`: a stripe in pieces is one stripe (`_join_pieces`).** The stripe finder takes every connected piece of sandwiched stripe color and tests it alone. ### Occlusion: one idea, and the rules in the order the data flows (2026-11-18 to 2026-11-20; the state of branch `wip/occl-combined`, awaiting the walkthrough)

This section replaces five that were written branch by branch (side occlusion, hidden bottoms, the scorecard, up and down, the outline). The history, round by round, is in the experiments' result folders: `results/2026-11-19_det_side`, `2026-11-19_det_bottom`, `2026-11-19_det_occl`, `2026-11-20_occl_vertical`, `2026-11-20_occl_pin`, `2026-11-19_damage_realism`, `2026-11-20_stencil`, and the combination `2026-11-20_occl_combined` (`verdict.md`, `scorecard.md`, and `walkthrough.md`, the same logic in short sentences).

**What is on.** On by default: `stripe_join`, `cap_behind` (2026-11-18, merged), `side_hidden`, `seam_height`, `cap_seen_bottom`, `edge_check`, `cap_same` (2026-11-19), `half_stripe`, `half_thin`, `post_pair`, `stripe_wide`, `cap_low`, `pin_outline`, `pin_thin` (2026-11-20), `thin_span` (2026-11-20, A8, branch `wip/occl-sliver`). Built and **off**: `slice_behind`, `slice_box`, `cover_unpinned`, `stripe_two`, `occl_body_side`, `stripe_bridge`, `thin_frame`, `own_close` (A8). `stencil.py` (a stencil-matching second opinion) is not part of the detector.

**The one idea.** *What is seen fixes the distance; the distance fixes the full size; the box is the full barrel, placed where the seen part allows; a rule acts only when what is seen could not be anything else.* Every rule has three parts: a **reading** that gives the distance (the stripe's row, a seam, a top row), a **second reading that agrees** (a seen edge where the first predicts it), and a **place** (where the seen part pins a box W wide and H tall). No second reading, or nothing that pins the place: no box. A miss is allowed; a box in the wrong place is not.

**How it is measured: the scorecard (`occlude.py`, `experiments/2026-11-19_det_occl/run.py`).** There are too few real occluded frames to measure anything, so the test makes them. Each of the 50 barrels of the train frames that stands free (12 from the HOME hand masks, 12 from the same frames segmented as in the eval table, 26 from CAMPUS) is cut out of its mask and put alone on an empty mask at its own place, so its row and size are real. Then one thing hides 25, 50 or 75 % of it: the frame edge, a straight edge, a slanted edge, both sides, from above, from below, a post (pixels erased); a real barrel's pixels pasted in front, of the other or the same body color, scaled for where it stands (beside, beside and lower, straight in front); a plain block of barrel color. 3888 scenes, scored against the barrel's **full** box under the v2 rule. What it cannot show: a real occluder also throws a shadow, reflects and bleeds color at the seam. Next to it: the damaged masks (`degrade.py`: the three made-up levels light / medium / heavy, and since 2026-11-19 two levels measured on real white-table segmentation, "typical" and "bad day"; real masks are close to "typical", and "medium" is far harsher than anything measured), the eval table, and the 337 recorded frames by eye.

**The order in `detect`.** Stripe finder → stripe step (stripes seen whole first, then the partly seen ones, nearest first) → stripeless pieces → `caps_behind` → `post_pairs` → (`slices_behind`, off) → `half_stripes`. A rule runs after everything it leans on: a cap leans on found barrels; the left-over rules take only pieces that gave no box, and each adds a box only where no box of that color already is (overlap over half), so nothing found earlier changes.

#### In the stripe finder

- **`stripe_join`: a stripe in pieces is one stripe (`_join_pieces`).** Pieces of sandwiched stripe color that share rows (half the height of the shorter) and together span at most `body_width` (1.2) × W are one stripe, because two barrels cannot both have their stripe inside one barrel's width. A piece joins only if it is stripe-shaped (not taller than 1.6 stripe heights, not taller than wide). A pale patch 0.15 W wide across every stripe: 85 → 125 of 145 found.
- **`seam_height`: a stripe eaten thin at its seams.** Mixed pixels at the two seams are neither color, so a far stripe 8 px tall can be 4 px in the mask and fail the height floor (0.45). The body loses the same pixels, so the gap between the two body parts is taller by what the stripe lost. A stripe too thin by its own rows, and seen whole (0.8 W), is read again as the mean of its height and of that gap. *HOME `far_02` with 2 px eaten:* stripe rows 120–123 (4), gap rows 116–126 (11), reading 7.5 against 8.3 expected → a stripe.
- **`stripe_wide`: a stripe wider than a barrel is two barrels.** A stripe wider than 1.2 × W, with body color seen right above and right below it, is cut into max(2, round(width / W)) parts; each part is hidden where it was cut, so its box is W from its own outer edge. W here is the larger of the row's width and the width of a barrel as tall as the body seen. (`stripe_two`, off, is the same without the "body right above and below" guard: it cut one large near barrel in two.) *HOME `core_02`, orange (368, 120, 418, 173) half hidden by an orange barrel at (340, 124, 393, 178):* stripe columns 342–416, 74 px, W 50. Before: one box (348, 120, 410, 178). Now (342, 121, 396, 178) and (367, 120, 418, 178).
- **The width floor.** A stripe piece must be at least `stripe_h_min` (0.45) of a stripe height wide (since `pin_thin`; one stripe height with `side_hidden` alone; 0.4 W before). A piece under one stripe height wide, or taller than wide, is "thin" (`Stripe.small`) and is a barrel only by `pin_thin` below. Pieces under the old floor are also handed on as `narrow` pieces for `post_pairs` and `slices_behind`.

#### In the stripe step (`barrel_from_stripe`, `_partly_seen`)

The stripe's row gives the distance, hence W, H, the top and the bottom. Seen body edges are used when they agree with the prediction (15 % of H, at least 4 px).

- **`edge_check`: an edge is seen only against something that is not a barrel.** (a) A candidate with neither its top nor its bottom where its row predicts them, whose top ends against the other barrel color (that color in half of its columns in the 4 rows beyond), is refused: a thing right above a top, inside the box, stands behind and cannot hide it; such a band is a strip of a back barrel between its own stripe and the front barrel. (b) A kept seen bottom with the other barrel color right under it becomes the lower of seen and predicted. *HOME `core_01`, orange (292, 121, 342, 176) with a blue barrel pasted in front:* before, a wrong blue box (287, 124, 346, 188) and an orange box 9 px short; now the band is refused and the orange box is (293, 121, 340, 174), overlap 0.91.
- **`side_hidden`: a stripe narrower than 0.8 W is placed by what is seen.** What is seen = the stripe's columns joined with the columns holding body color above or below it (`_body_span`; if that is wider than 1.2 W a same-colored neighbour is in it and the body is not trusted). Seen span ≥ 0.8 W: that span is the box. Otherwise a side counts as hidden only for a reason: (a) the frame edge; (b) a found barrel standing nearer (its box holds one of the 3 pixels beside the seen span at the stripe's row and its bottom is lower than this barrel's predicted bottom); (c) the body and the stripe end at different columns there, further apart than the edge tolerance (at a barrel's own side they end together; a part cut from a joined stripe is hidden where it was cut). Exactly one side hidden: the box is W wide from the seen edge of the other. Left: body and stripe 1 px apart, the barrel's own edge. Right: 17 px apart (tolerance 8), cut. Box (277, 127, 331, 178); hand-drawn (275, 123, 328, 180); overlap 0.82.
- **`pin_outline`: with no reason on either side, the barrel's own corner pins it (`Outline`, `_seen_lines`, `_end_costs`, `_outline_place`).** The distance gives the whole outline as the camera sees it: top line, stripe line, bottom line, a row per column. Its arcs are 1 to 2 px and useless; its corners are large: in the last 8 % before a barrel's own side the top is 10 to 13 px lower and the bottom 13 to 17 px higher than in the middle (55 px barrel); a cut side has square corners. Each end of the seen part is asked by itself: with the outline's side put on that end, each of the three lines is compared with the seen one over half a lid's width (0.353 W); the end's cost is the **largest** of the three mean distances (after taking out each line's own up-down offset), in barrel heights. An end costing at most `pin_good` (0.05) is the barrel's own side. **Exactly one such end: the box is W wide from it.** Neither or both: as before (centred on what is seen if the stripe is at least 0.4 W, else no box). A box placed this way never replaces a found barrel's box. Why each end alone and the largest line: a slanted cut imitates one rounded corner and never both. *HOME `core_02`, left half behind a straight edge:* W 50, H 54, limit 2.7 rows; the cut end costs 4.6, the own end 1.8 → box (368, 120, 418, 172), overlap 0.98; before, centred: (380, 120, 431, 172), overlap 0.59.
- **`pin_thin`: a thin slice with one own corner.** A thin piece (three quarters hidden leaves a piece narrower than a stripe is tall) is a barrel only when it is a **full slice** (neither its top nor its bottom inferred: the body above and below ends where the piece's row predicts) **and** the outline finds exactly one own end; the box is W wide from that end. The band is a barrel's stripe when: it is not taller than a stripe (1.6 ×; `half_thin` drops the 0.45 floor, since half hidden from below leaves 2 to 6 rows of stripe); it is 0.8 to 1.2 W wide; **the body's far edge is where the seam predicts it** (the second reading) and inside the frame; the body joined to it is 0.8 to 1.2 W wide; the open side is clear of barrel color; the far edge ends against something that is not barrel color; it is not a found barrel's; and it is thinner than the body beside it (a stripe is 6 mm, a body part 9 mm or more). The hidden edge is drawn where the seam's distance puts it. A box the stripe step made from a corner of the same band is replaced. *HOME `core_02`, upper half hidden:* band columns 368–417, rows 146–154; seam 154 → 205.9 mm, W 49.5, top 117.1, bottom 170.9; orange ends at 173 (2.1 px off, allowed 8.1) → (368, 117, 417, 173), overlap 0.93. *Lower half hidden:* 5 rows of stripe, seam 142, top predicted 117.6 and seen 120 → (368, 120, 418, 172), overlap 0.98.

#### A stripe cut through, a sliver with stripe and body in view (A8, 2026-11-20, branch `wip/occl-sliver`; awaiting the walkthrough)

The user's two asks: a stripe cut by a gap should be filled in when there is a strong enough sense of one stripe; and when any piece of stripe with body shows, the box should be good. `experiments/2026-11-20_occl_sliver` (PREREG, two rounds), `results/2026-11-20_occl_sliver/verdict.md`, `walkthrough.md`, `examples.md`.

- **`thin_span` (on): a thin slice whose body shows the whole barrel (`_partly_seen`, `_body_crosses`).** A thin piece (narrower than a stripe is tall) was placed by the outline only, "whatever its span". - **`thin_frame` (off): a thin full slice cut by the frame edge is W wide from its other end.** The frame is a known hidden side, but a thin piece never asked it. Frame edge at 75 %: 29 → 88 of 100, no false box anywhere. **Off** by one clause: "too big" 3 → 8 (five boxes 2 to 3 px wider than a hand box clipped at the frame; the paint reaches past the hand box).
- **`own_close` (off): the end where the body closes on the stripe is the barrel's own side (`_end_closure`, `_closed_end`).** Over the last 0.05 W of each end of a full slice: how far both the top and the bottom have come in from the rows the stripe predicts. ### What went wrong with steps 1–3 — and what step 4 (the stripe) fixed

- **Same-color occlusion.** A barrel partly behind another of the same color merges into one blob with it, and the width rule cannot tell "one barrel in front of another" from "one wide blob": `occl_01` and `occl_02` are missed. Four barrels bunched together (`cluster_01`) come out as two.
- **Anything orange or blue of about the right size gets a box.** The shape checks only ask "is this barrel-sized for its row", not "is this a barrel". A hand that *touches* a barrel is worse: in `far_02` the fingers merge with the right blue barrel into one blob whose top row is at the hand, so the geometry thinks the barrel is 2.5 m away and throws it out — a real barrel lost because of a hand.
- **Far range.** At 30 cm the tolerance (6 px) is about the size of the camera error (5 px), so the geometry can overrule a seen bottom that was right (`far_02`, 7 px). The calibration session will shrink the camera error; the tolerance may also need to be looser far away.

Step 4 (the stripe) attacks the first two directly and fixed them on the train frames (which is why it is the default): a band of one color sandwiched between the other color above and below, at the height the geometry expects, proves "this is a barrel", gives one barrel per stripe even inside a merged blob, and refuses hands and other orange or blue objects. What remains is a barrel whose stripe is entirely hidden behind another barrel.

## 4. Detection eval — `src/barrels/eval_det.py`

**What it does.** Scores predicted boxes against the hand-drawn boxes: for each image, which predictions match which true barrels, and from that precision, recall and F1 per color, plus how far off the bottom edges are.

**The idea.** A prediction and a true box *match* only if they have the same color and overlap enough — intersection over union ≥ 0.5, i.e. the overlapping area is at least half the area the two boxes cover together. Matching is greedy: the most confident prediction takes the best-overlapping free truth box, then the next, so each true barrel is used at most once. What is left over is either a *false* box (a prediction with no truth) or a *miss* (a truth with no prediction). On frames with no barrels every box is false. Per color: precision = matches ÷ boxes drawn, recall = matches ÷ true barrels, F1 = their harmonic mean (2PR ÷ (P + R)), and the headline number is the mean of the orange and blue F1. That is the **v1 rule**, which also ignores true barrels flagged occluded / truncated / top-only (they count neither as hits nor as misses). The **v2 rule** (`EVAL.md`, in the code since 2026-11-03 evening, `rule="v2"`) is stricter in two ways: a match also needs the predicted bottom edge within max(3 px, a tenth of the true box height), and **every** true barrel counts — occluded, truncated, held, all of them; the flags only split the error reports (bottom error for seen vs `bottom_estimated` boxes). Matching under v2 takes the highest-overlap eligible pair first. A false box on an empty frame marked `clean` (nothing orange or blue in it) disqualifies the configuration outright. Scores are also broken down per scene tag.

**Where it lives.** `evaluate_frame(name, preds, gts, cam, rule="v1"|"v2", clean=…)` → per-frame counts and the list of matches with their IoU and bottom-row error; `bottom_tol_px` / `bottom_within_tol` (the v2 bottom condition); `match_greedy` (v1) and `match_best_first` (v2); `aggregate` pools frames per condition, per scene and overall; `write_results` writes `summary.md`, `summary.csv`, `per_image.csv` (counts per frame) and `per_barrel.csv` (every match, miss and false box with its IoU, bottom error and `width_ratio` = predicted width / true width, added 2026-11-09 because the IoU match passes a box 15 % too thin); `score` reduces a run to one dict for sweeps (primary = mean F1; `disqualified` when a clean empty frame got a box). CLI: `tools/eval_boxes.py --pred DIR --split train --gt-boxes --rule v2`.

**What can go wrong.** The true bottom edge of a hidden base is itself a human estimate, so bottom-edge error is reported separately for `bottom_estimated` boxes (`per_barrel.csv`). Five test frames hold about ten barrels, so the test number will be coarse: one miss moves recall by 10 %.

### The one table — `src/barrels/report.py`, `tools/report.py` (2026-11-11)

**What it does.** Prints one table that shows what is better where: rows are data set × scenario, columns are the same five numbers for a *candidate* and a *baseline* next to each other, then candidate − baseline. It changes nothing about how a box is judged — every count comes from `evaluate_frame(rule="v2")` and the headline from `score`, as above. Before it there were three sets, three experiment scripts and a different table from each.

**The idea, in the order the data flows.**

1. *Which frames* (`labeled_stems`, `select_frames`). Every file `data/labels/<stem>_boxes.json` names a frame with truth boxes. The stem `{robot}_{light}_{surface}_{scene}_{nn}` is cut in two: the first three fields are the **data set** (`4A8B_window_light` is shown as HOME, `4A8B_campus_light` as CAMPUS, Dave's as `DAVE*`; any other group, e.g. `4A8B_lampwarm7_wood`, shows up under its own name with no code change), the fourth is the **scenario**. Kept: frames in the train split, and Dave's (they are in no split; marked `*` = indicative, because his boxes group touching barrels). Left out: the 15 test frames, `calib` / `ruler` / `horizon` frames, and labeled frames not yet assigned to a split (they might still become test frames; the footer lists them).
2. *The test-frame guard* (`refuse_test_frames`). `run_arm` calls it before any image is opened and it raises `TestFrameError` if a test frame is in the list. The only way past is `--split test` on the command line, for a planned look.
3. *An arm* (`Arm`, `parse_det_overrides`, `run_arm`). An arm is a color-model file (a results `config.json`) plus the `DetConfig` fields changed from the default, written `field=value`. For every frame: segment with the model, run `detector.detect`, score against the box label (`score_frame`). **Home is special:** the color model's four numbers were read off the home frames, so scoring them with that same model would be marking its own homework. As in the experiment that produced the published 0.93, each home scenario is segmented by a model re-fit (same `Config`, `pipeline.fit`) on the *other* home scenarios — leave-one-scene-out. Every other set never taught the model anything, so the model file is used as it is. `--home-protocol fixed` turns the re-fit off; the footer then says the number is not the published one.
4. *One cell* (`summarize`). A list of scored frames becomes: found = true barrels with a right box; missed = without; false = boxes matching no barrel; width = the median of box width ÷ true width over the found barrels; bot = the median of |predicted bottom row − true bottom row| over the found barrels; and, for a whole set, the score (mean of the orange and blue F1), the false boxes on empty frames and the disqualifier — the last three taken from `eval_det.score` unchanged.
5. *The rows* (`build_rows`, `delta`). Per data set: one row per scenario, then the set's subtotal. `delta` is candidate minus baseline, column by column; a dot means no change, a dash means there is no number (no barrel found on one side). The last row, ALL, adds up the counts of the sets that are not indicative and has no score, because no agreed headline pools different sets.
6. *Out* (`format_text`, `format_markdown`, `write_csv`, `to_json`, `write_all`). The same rows as fixed-width text (≈ 140 columns), and with `--out` as `report.md`, `report.csv`, `report.json` in `results/<date>_<name>/`.

**How to run.** `python barrels/tools/report.py` = today (hs `eb863d84` + `DetConfig()`) against the v1 front end with the same detector. To test a detector change behind a switch against today: `--cand-det <field>=<value> --base-model default`. To test a new color model: `--cand-model results/<x>/config.json --base-model default`. `--sets CAMPUS` limits the rows. A full `python -m pytest` run prints the default table at the end (`tests/conftest.py`, ≈ 15 s; `BARRELS_NO_REPORT=1` skips it; it is skipped when only some tests are run).

**Numbers it reproduces** (2026-11-11, `experiments/2026-11-11_eval_report/`): HOME 0.93 for both front ends, CAMPUS 0.99, DAVE\* 0.47, no box on any empty frame. One thing to know: the default baseline (v1 + *today's* detector) scores 0.39 on campus; the published "v1 0.41" is v1 + the *2026-11-05* detector, which the experiment script prints as a second table (`--base-det stripe_mode=bands,body_min=0,height_max=0,body_width=0,cover_rows=false`). Both were already in `EXPERIMENTS.md` 2026-11-08.

**What can go wrong.** A scenario row is one or two frames, so one barrel moves it a lot — read the rows for *where*, the subtotals for *how much*. The width and bottom medians are over found barrels only, so an arm that finds only the easy barrels can look more exact. A new session appears only once its frames have box labels *and* a split assignment.

### The vendor next to ours — `report.py` (`vendor_arm`, `vendor_coverage`, `loose_counts`), `tools/report.py --vendor` (2026-11-18, branch `wip/eval-vendor-gate`; awaiting walkthrough; the default table prints exactly what it printed before)

**What it does.** The week's goal is a detector at least as good as the robot's own ("aivision", the vendor). Since 2026-11-18 `tm save` stores, with every frame, what the vendor detector reported at that instant (`data/vendor/<stem>_vendor.json`, `DATA.md` "Vendor detections"). `python barrels/tools/report.py --vendor` puts the vendor in the one table as the baseline arm, so the delta block reads "ours minus the vendor". Nothing about a hit changes: the vendor's boxes go through the same `evaluate_frame(rule="v2")` against the same hand-drawn labels.

**The idea, in the order the data flows.**

1. *Which frames.* As before: labeled train frames grouped into data sets; test frames refused. The refusal (`refuse_test_frames`) runs before any vendor file is read, as it runs before any image is opened.
2. *Which of those the vendor was measured on* (`vendor_coverage`). Each selected frame's vendor file is read once. The file is there and says `available: true` → **measured**, also when its object list is empty: the vendor looked and saw nothing, which is an answer, and every barrel in that frame is a miss for it. No file (frames shot before 2026-11-18), `available: false` (the robot's status could not be read at that moment) or a file that cannot be read → the frame is **left out for both arms** and counted in the footer. `available: false` means "not measured"; it is never scored as "the vendor saw nothing". A fair comparison uses the same frames on both sides, so the rows of a vendor comparison hold the measured frames only, a set with none disappears from the table, and the footer says how many frames of each set have a file ("HOME 0 of 16, …").
3. *The vendor's boxes* (`io.vendor_barrels`). Objects named `OrangeBarrel` / `BlueBarrel` become boxes in our 640×480 pixels (the vendor reports 320×240 units; times 2, done when the file was written). Balls, robots and tags are ignored and counted in the footer; a barrel object with no usable box is ignored and counted too.
4. *Scoring* (`score_boxes`, used by both arms). Boxes plus the frame's label → `evaluate_frame(rule="v2")`. A vendor box on an empty table is a false box, and on a *clean* empty table it disqualifies, exactly as for us. Our arm on these frames is what it always is; a home frame is still segmented by the model re-fit without its scene, and that re-fit learns from every other home scene whether or not those have a vendor file (`run_arm(..., only=)`), so a frame's score does not depend on which frames the vendor was measured on.
5. *The two looser counts* (`loose_counts`), printed as a small second table under the footer, for the vendor and for us alike. The v2 rule wants IoU ≥ 0.5 **and** the bottom edge within a tenth of the height. Our labels are the whole barrel, lid to base, drawn through occluders; the vendor may draw tighter, or only what it sees. Then the strict rule punishes it for how it draws, not for what it finds. So next to *strict* (the v2 rule): **overlap** = same color and IoU ≥ 0.5, the bottom edge not checked; **place** = same color and the two box centers at most half the true box's width apart (for a barrel 50 px wide: within 25 px), nearest pair first. The place count does not depend on how tight a box is; it only asks "is there a box of the right color on this barrel". Each barrel and each box is used once; `false` = the boxes left over under that count. Worked example (the unit test): the true box is 50 × 60. A vendor box 30 × 40 around the same center has IoU 0.4: strict miss, overlap miss, place found. A vendor box over the top two thirds has IoU 0.67 but its bottom edge is 20 px high (the tolerance is 6): strict miss, overlap found, place found. **These counts are reporting only**: found / missed / false and the score in the table stay the v2 rule.

**How to run.** `python barrels/tools/report.py --vendor` (= `--base-model vendor`): today's detector against the vendor. `--cand-det …` / `--cand-model …` as always, to compare a candidate with the vendor. `--cand-model vendor --base-model default` turns the two around. `--loose` prints the looser counts for any two arms without the vendor (today: home 27 strict / 27 overlap / 27 place of 29, campus 34 / 34 / 34 of 35). `--out` writes them too: `report.json` gets a `loose` dict in every cell and a `vendor` block (frames per set, what was left out, what was ignored), `report.csv` eight more columns, `report.md` a second table. Without `--vendor` or `--loose` every output is byte-for-byte what it was.

**What can go wrong.** No real vendor file exists yet (2026-11-18): everything is tested on files the tests write themselves, with the schema `tm save` uses. Not known until the first shoot: how the vendor draws its boxes, whether its list lags the frame, how many frames come back `available: false`. The place count passes a box of the right color sitting on the barrel even when it is a quarter of the barrel's size, and for a barrel cut by the frame edge the true width is the clipped one, so the tolerance shrinks. With today's data the command prints "no frame to compare" and the per-set "0 of N" line, not a table of zeros.

### The never-regress check — `src/barrels/gate.py`, `tools/report.py --gate` / `--save-baseline` (2026-11-18, same branch)

**What it does.** The project rule (`TODO.md`, "The goal"): no scenario row of the one table on home or campus may get worse, and nothing may be drawn on an empty frame of any set. Until now this was checked by eye. `python barrels/tools/report.py --gate` runs the detector, compares the table with stored numbers row by row, prints what got worse and what got better, and ends with `RESULT: PASS` (exit code 0) or `RESULT: FAIL` (exit code 1). It decides nothing about what a found barrel is: every number is a cell of the one table.

**The idea, in the order the data flows.**

1. *The stored numbers* (`snapshot`, `save_baseline`). `--save-baseline` runs the detector as it is today and writes `results/baseline/baseline.json` (committed): for every data set and every scenario, the frames, barrels, found, missed, false boxes and boxes on empty frames, and at the top the date, the git hash, the color model and the full `DetConfig` they came from. One row per line, so a change shows as a one-line diff.
2. *The protected sets* (`load_protected`). `results/baseline/protected.json` holds the short list: HOME and CAMPUS. Every other set (the black table, the wood ladder, Dave's) is extreme or indicative: shown, never a reason to fail.
3. *This run.* `--gate` runs one arm, the candidate: today's defaults, or with `--cand-det field=value` / `--cand-model file` a change that is not the default yet. Always every data set, train frames, home leave-one-scene-out (the options that would change that are refused).
4. *Row by row* (`compare`, `RowChange`). For each scenario row: **worse** = fewer barrels found, or more false boxes (one barrel gained and one false box gained is worse); **better** = the opposite with nothing worse; **new** = the row is not in the baseline (a new data set, or a new scenario in an old one); **changed** = the row has other frames or another number of barrels than in the baseline, so the two counts cannot be compared; **gone** = in the baseline, not in this run.
5. *Pass or fail* (`RowChange.fails`, `GateResult.passed`). Fail when a protected row is worse; when a protected row is changed or gone (the check cannot vouch for it: look at the table, then save a new baseline); or when **any** row of **any** set has more boxes on empty frames than the baseline (a new row counts from zero). Everything else passes: a drop on an extreme set is printed under "for information only", a new set is printed as NEW.
6. *Out* (`format_gate`). The rows that got worse, the rows that got better, new boxes on empty frames, new rows, the other sets for information, one line of totals per set, then the result line naming the rows that failed it.

**How to run.** Before every merge that touches the pipeline, the detector or the geometry: `python barrels/tools/report.py --gate` on the branch (about 10 s). To test a switch before making it the default: `python barrels/tools/report.py --gate --cand-det cap_behind=false` (this one fails: `HOME occl` goes from 4 to 2 found of 4). After a merge that changed the numbers on purpose: `python barrels/tools/report.py --save-baseline` and commit the file, so that the gain is protected from then on; it prints the comparison with the baseline it replaces and refuses to store worse numbers unless `--force` is given. **A new ordinary condition is protected** by adding its name (as the table prints it) to `results/baseline/protected.json`, running `--save-baseline`, and committing both files. The baseline of 2026-11-18: home 27 of 29, campus 34 of 35, no false box on either, no box on any empty frame.

**What can go wrong.** A scenario row is one or two frames, so the check is as coarse as the table. It compares counts, not which barrel: losing one barrel and finding another in the same row reads as "same". It protects the train frames only; the test frames are never opened by it. If the baseline is not saved again after a gain, the gain is not protected (a later change may lose it and still pass). Adding or re-labeling frames in a protected set makes its rows "changed", which fails until a new baseline is saved with `--force` after a look at the table.

## 5. The live feed — `live/` + `worldmap_bridge.py` + `tracker.py` (2026-11-05; the multi-frame program and frame capture 2026-11-06)

**What it does.** Shows sections 1 and 3 on the robot's own camera, in a window on the laptop. Dave's ask from the 2026-11-03 meeting. Display only: the world map still runs on the vendor detector. Two programs: `BarrelViewer` answers every frame on its own (the spec's answer, once a second); `BarrelTracker` shows boxes that persist over frames (Tanush's ask after the first live run, 2026-11-06).

**The idea.** aim_fsm programs are state machines: *nodes* do something when entered, *transitions* move between nodes when an event happens. `BarrelViewer`'s own node is `ShowFrame`: it reads `robot.camera_image` (the latest frame, RGB; skipped when no new frame has arrived since the last tick), hands it to the hook, and shows the result (`program.show`, which calls Dave's `imshow`). A one-second timer (`=T(1)=>`) re-enters `ShowFrame`, so the window refreshes once a second. The nodes both programs share live in `live/barrel_live.py`: `SetMode` changes which picture is shown, `Save` and `Record` write raw frames (below). Each is reached by a text message (`tm seg` at the `C>` prompt fires `=TM('seg')=>`) and returns to the loop at once (`=N=>`). The whole machine is six lines in `$setup{}`; `genfsm` turns them into the Python that wires nodes to transitions (`live/BarrelViewer.py`, committed so `simple_cli BarrelViewer` works without regenerating).

**The hook.** `BarrelDetectorHook` holds the three things the detector needs — the frozen segmentation model (`afd85483`), the detector and its config (`detector.detect`, `DetConfig()`), the camera preset (`AIM_4A8B6118_BOXFIT`) — and `default()` builds that exact combination, so "the detector as it stands" is defined in one place and `tools/detect_image.py` loads the same model file. One escape hatch: the environment variable `BARRELS_MODEL` names another model file, and `default()` (hence both live programs and `detect_image.py`) loads that instead, so a candidate front end can be tried on the robot before it is adopted (`RUNBOOK.md`). `view(image_rgb, mode)` is the live frame's whole path: RGB → BGR (OpenCV's order, what the pipeline takes), `segment` (section 1), `detect` (section 3), then `detector.draw` for `"boxes"` (the v2 output image: yellow = orange barrel, green = blue, 1 px), the dimmed frame for `"seg"` (below), or the dimmed frame with the boxes for `"both"`; then BGR → RGB because `imshow` wants RGB. `render(image_rgb, barrels, mode, raw=None)` is the drawing half on its own, for a program that decides itself which barrels to draw; `view` = `on_image` + `render`. A test checks that `view` produces pixel-for-pixel what `detect_image.py` writes for the same frame.

**The seg view (`tm seg`, two windows since 2026-11-11).** The question it answers is "which pixels did the color step call orange or blue?". *Main window:* the camera frame with every pixel that is **not** classified orange or blue multiplied by 0.3 (`wb.SEG_DIM`), so the scene is still readable but dark; the classified pixels are copied unchanged, at full brightness, in the camera's own colors. *Second window* (titled like the main one plus ` seg`: `barrels seg`, `barrels (tracked) seg`): only the classified pixels, painted in their **class color** (orange RGB 255,128,0; blue RGB 0,100,255 — the colors `viz` uses) on black, so it also says which of the two classes each pixel got; everything else is black. `tm both` is the same pair with the boxes drawn on the main window. `tm boxes` goes back to the plain frame with boxes and closes the second window on the next frame shown. No pixel is corrected anywhere: the frame is only copied or darkened for display, and the detector never sees these pictures. The arithmetic is one plain function, `wb.seg_views(image, mask, dim, colors)` → `(dimmed, seg_only)`, with its own unit test; `hook.render` uses the first for the main window, `hook.seg_only()` builds the second from the last mask. The window work is `BarrelLive.show(title, picture)` in `live/barrel_live.py`: `imshow` the main picture; in `seg` / `both` also `imshow` the second; otherwise `destroyWindow` it if it was open. It is called from `ShowFrame` exactly where `imshow` was called before (Dave's `imshow` / `destroyWindow` hand the Qt work to the main thread themselves). Before 2026-11-11 `tm seg` only tinted the one window (class color blended 50 % over the image), which neither isolated the segmented pixels nor showed their true color.

**The multi-frame view (`BarrelTracker`).** The detector still answers each frame on its own — that answer is what the eval scores and what `tm raw` overlays in white. `tracker.Tracker` sits on top and decides what to *show*. Each frame's barrels are matched to the known tracks by color and box overlap (IoU ≥ 0.3, best overlap first, each used once); unmatched ones start new tracks. A track is shown once it has been seen in 3 of the last 4 frames, stays shown through one missed frame, is hidden at two, and is forgotten after four misses in a row. The box drawn is the per-edge median of the last three matched boxes, so one odd frame cannot move it. `BarrelTracker.fsm` runs the loop five times a second (`=T(0.2)=>`) and counts only distinct camera frames (`robot.frame_count`). Worked example at 5 fps: a barrel that enters the picture is drawn 3 frames later (0.6 s); missing from one frame it stays; missing from two it disappears; back for three it reappears, on the same track. What this cannot do: hide a mistake that is there frame after frame, like the floating box of the first live run — that is the detector's to fix.

**Saving frames (`capture.py`).** `tm save` writes the current frame as `snapshots/robot_snapN.png` under `barrels/live/` (the camera viewer's naming, N continuing past existing files, so `tools/import_snaps.py --src barrels/live/snapshots` takes them as usual). `tm rec` records every frame for ten seconds, at most ten a second, into `snapshots/rec_<stamp>/` with a `frames.json` of times and frame counts. Recording runs in `user_image`, the per-frame callback aim_fsm gives every program, on the image thread, so it costs the detector loop nothing; a disk error stops the recording, never the stream. Frames come in RGB and are written BGR, so they load like every committed frame.

**Where it lives.** `live/BarrelViewer.fsm`, `live/BarrelTracker.fsm` (≈ 50 lines each, half docstring), `live/barrel_live.py` (the base: hook, modes, `new_frame`, `show`, `report`, `user_image`, the shared nodes); `worldmap_bridge.BarrelDetectorHook` with `default()`, `segment`, `on_image` (barrels), `view` (picture), `render` (picture for given barrels), `seg_only` (the second window), `annotate`; `wb.seg_views`, `wb.DEFAULT_MODEL`, `wb.VIEWS`; `tracker.TrackConfig` / `Tracker.update` / `visible`; `capture.FrameCapture.save` / `start_recording` / `on_frame`. The rest of `worldmap_bridge.py` is the parked world-map route.

**How to run it.** `RUNBOOK.md`: `cd barrels/live && simple_cli BarrelViewer` (per frame) or `simple_cli BarrelTracker` (persisting boxes); `tm seg` / `tm boxes` / `tm both`; `tm save` / `tm rec`; `tm raw` in the tracker; Ctrl+\ to quit; `genfsm X.fsm` only after editing an `.fsm`. ≈ 60 ms per frame on the laptop.

**What can go wrong.** First run 2026-11-06 (Tanush, the back of his laptop as a plain backdrop on a campus table): the window and the controls work; the detector drew a floating orange box above the orange barrel and missed the barrel itself. Rebuilt from his screenshots (`experiments/2026-11-06_live_first_run/`): the stripe finder took a band of unlabeled pixels under a false orange smear for a shadowed stripe, and threw the real blue stripe away because the unlabeled shadow glued to it made its band taller than three stripe heights. That is the detector-hardening task; the segmentation's own false orange (the edge of the laptop lid, the glow a barrel throws on the table, skin) and its missing shaded faces are the redesign's. On a scene like Dave's office the segmentation finds nothing (section 1), so the boxes view is just the camera image; `tm seg` shows why (the whole main window dim, the second window black). The camera preset is fit for Tanush's robot; on another robot the predicted bottoms will be off by the tilt difference. If the window never appears, no frame has arrived yet (the console says so); if it freezes, `restart img_thread`.
